At that time, Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña Bodhisattva addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! How does Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva travel in the Sahā world? World-Honored One! How many hundreds of thousands of millions of inconceivable numbers of difficult and bitter practices has this Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva performed? Excellent, World-Honored One! Pray explain a little."
All devas, Nagas, spirits, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, non-humans, the Bodhisattvas who had arrived from other lands, and this assembly of Śrāvakas, hearing this, all rejoiced.
Then the Buddha addressed Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña Bodhisattva: "In the distant past, kalpas equal to immeasurable Ganges sands ago, there was a Buddha named Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha, Vidyācaraṇasampanna, Sugata, Lokavid, Anuttara, Puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstā Devamanuṣyāṇām, Buddha, Bhagavān.
That Buddha had eighty billion great Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas and a great assembly of Śrāvakas equal to the sands of seventy-two Ganges rivers. The Buddha's lifespan was forty-two thousand kalpas, and the lifespans of the Bodhisattvas were also equal. That land had no women, hells, hungry ghosts, animals, asuras, or tribulations; the ground was flat as the palm of a hand, made of lapis lazuli, adorned with jewel trees, covered with jewel curtains, draped with jewel flower banners; jewel vases and censers pervaded the realm; seven-jeweled platforms stood, one tree to each platform, the trees separated from the platforms by the distance of an arrow's flight.
Beneath all these jewel trees, Bodhisattvas and Śrāvakas sat. Upon each jewel platform, tens of billions of devas made heavenly music, singing praises to the Buddha as offerings. At that time, that Buddha expounded the Lotus Sūtra for Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva, the assembly of Bodhisattvas, and the assembly of Śrāvakas.
This Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva delighted in practicing austerities. Within the Dharma of Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Buddha, he vigorously practiced walking meditation, single-mindedly seeking Buddhahood; fulfilling twelve thousand years, he attained the samādhi of manifesting all physical bodies. Having attained this samādhi, his heart rejoiced greatly, thinking: 'My attaining the samādhi of manifesting all physical bodies is entirely through the power of hearing the Lotus Sūtra; I should now make offerings to Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Buddha and the Lotus Sūtra.'
Immediately he entered this samādhi, and from open space rained down māndārava flowers, mahā-māndārava flowers, and fine powdered hard black sandalwood, filling open space, descending like clouds; he also rained the sandalwood incense of this shore of the ocean—six grains of this incense being worth the Sahā world—to make offerings to the Buddha.
Having made this offering, he arose from samādhi and thought to himself: 'Although I have made offerings to the Buddha through spiritual powers, it is not equal to offering my own body.'
Immediately he ingested various fragrances—sandalwood, frankincense, turuṣka, priyaṅgu, aloeswood, and resin incense—and drank the scented oil of campaka flowers. Fulfilling twelve hundred years, he anointed his body with scented oil, and before Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Buddha, wrapped his body in heavenly jewel robes, poured scented oil over himself, and through the power of spiritual vows burned his own body. Its light universally illuminated worlds equal to the sands of eighty billion Ganges rivers.
The Buddhas within them simultaneously praised: 'Excellent, excellent! Good son! This is genuine vigor; this is named making offerings of the genuine Dharma to the Tathāgata. If with flowers, incense, necklaces, burning incense, powdered incense, paste incense, heavenly silk banners, canopies, and the sandalwood incense of this shore of the ocean, one were to make offerings with such various things, it could not equal this; even if giving countries, cities, wives, and children, it could not equal this. Good son! This is named the foremost giving; among all givings, it is supreme and unexcelled, because one makes offerings of the Dharma to the Tathāgatas!' Having spoken these words, they became silent.
His body burned with fire for twelve hundred years; only after this did his body become exhausted.
Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva, having made such a Dharma offering, after his life ended was reborn in the land of Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Buddha, in the home of King Pure Virtue, sitting cross-legged, suddenly born by transformation. Immediately he spoke gāthās for his father:
Great King, you should now know:
Practicing walking meditation in that place,
Immediately I attained
The samādhi of manifesting all bodies.
Diligently practicing great vigor,
I abandoned the body I cherished,
Making offerings to the World-Honored One,
To seek unexcelled wisdom.
Having spoken these gāthās, he addressed his father: 'Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Buddha is even now present. Earlier, having made offerings to the Buddha, I attained the dhāraṇī of understanding the languages of all wandering beings, and further heard this Lotus Sūtra in hundreds of thousands of millions of inconceivable numbers of kaṅkaras, bimbaras, and akṣobhyas of verses. Great King! I should now return to make offerings to this Buddha.'
Having spoken thus, he sat upon a seven-jeweled platform, ascended into open space to the height of seven tāla trees, went to where the Buddha was, bowed with his head touching the Buddha's feet, joined his ten fingers, and praised the Buddha in gāthās:
Your countenance is extraordinarily wondrous,
Your light illuminates the ten directions;
I recently made offerings to you,
And now return in person to behold you.
Then Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva, having spoken these gāthās, addressed the Buddha: 'World-Honored One! The World-Honored One is still in the world!'
At that time, Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Buddha addressed Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva: 'Good son! The time of my nirvāṇa has arrived, the time of extinction is here; you may arrange a seat; tonight I will enter parinirvāṇa.'
He further commanded Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva: 'Good son! I entrust the Buddha-Dharma to you and all the Bodhisattva great disciples, along with the Dharma of anuttarasamyaksambodhi; and I entrust to you the seven-jeweled worlds of the great three-thousand-fold worlds, with all their jewel trees, jewel platforms, and attendant devas. After my extinction, whatever relics there are I also entrust to you; you should cause them to be distributed, widely setting forth offerings, erecting several thousand stūpas.'
In this manner Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Buddha commanded Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva, and in the latter part of the night entered nirvāṇa.
Then Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva, seeing the Buddha pass into extinction, grieved, lamented, and longed for the Buddha. Immediately with sandalwood from this shore of the ocean as a pyre, he made offerings to the Buddha's body, and cremated it.
After the fire died out, he gathered the relics, made eighty-four thousand precious urns, and erected eighty-four thousand stūpas, three worlds in height, with adorned spires, hanging banners, canopies, and jewel bells.
Then Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva thought to himself again: 'Although I have made these offerings, my mind is not yet satisfied; I should now make further offerings to the relics.'
He said to the Bodhisattva great disciples, devas, Nagas, yakṣas, and the entire assembly: 'You should all be single-mindedly mindful; I now make offerings to the relics of Candrasūryavimalaprabhāsaśrī Buddha.'
Having spoken these words, before the eighty-four thousand stūpas, he burned his arm adorned with a hundred merits for seventy-two thousand years as an offering, causing countless multitudes seeking to be Śrāvakas and immeasurable incalculable people to generate the mind of anuttarasamyaksambodhi, all enabling them to dwell in the samādhi of manifesting all physical bodies.
At that time, the Bodhisattvas, devas, humans, and asuras, seeing him without an arm, grieved, sorrowed, and lamented, saying: 'This Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva is our teacher who taught and transformed us; yet now he has burned his arm, his body incomplete!'
Then Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva, amidst the great assembly, made this vow: 'Having cast off both arms, I will certainly attain the golden body of a Buddha; if this is genuine and not false, may both my arms be restored as before!'
Having made this vow, they were naturally restored as before, brought about by the deep purity of this Bodhisattva's merit and wisdom. At that moment, the great three-thousand-fold worlds shook in six ways, heaven rained down jewel flowers, and all humans and devas attained what they had never had before."
The Buddha addressed Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña Bodhisattva: "What do you think: could Sarvasattvapriyadarśana Bodhisattva have been someone else? It was Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva himself. The instances of his abandoning his body in giving are immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of inconceivable numbers like this.
Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña! If someone generates the resolve wishing to attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi, and is able to burn a finger or even a single toe as an offering to a Buddha stūpa, it surpasses offering countries, cities, wives, children, and the mountains, forests, rivers, ponds, and treasures of the great three-thousand-fold worlds.
If someone fills the great three-thousand-fold worlds with the seven treasures to make offerings to the Buddha, great Bodhisattvas, Pratyekabuddhas, and arhats, the merit attained by that person does not equal receiving and upholding this Lotus Sūtra, down to a single four-line gāthā; its merit is foremost!
Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña! Just as among all streams, rivers, and waters, the ocean is foremost; this Lotus Sūtra is also like this: among scriptures expounded by the Tathāgatas, it is the deepest and greatest.
Just as among earthen mountains, black mountains, the Small Iron Ring Mountains, the Great Iron Ring Mountains, and the ten jewel mountains, Mount Sumeru is foremost; this Lotus Sūtra is also like this: among all scriptures, it stands supreme.
Just as among the stars, the Moon Deva is foremost; this Lotus Sūtra is also like this: among thousands of billions of scriptural dharmas, it illuminates most brightly.
Just as the Sun Deva can dispel all darkness; this scripture is also like this: able to shatter all darkness of unwholesome things.
Just as among petty kings, the wheel-turning sage king is foremost; this scripture is also like this: among all scriptures, it is most revered.
Just as Śakra is king among the Trāyastriṃśa devas; this scripture is also like this: the King among scriptures.
Just as the Great Brahmā King is the father of all wandering beings; this scripture is also like this: the father of all worthy sages, practitioners in training and beyond training, and those who generate the Bodhisattva resolve.
Just as among all common mortals, the srotāpanna, sakṛdāgāmin, anāgāmin, arhat, and pratyekabuddha are foremost; this scripture is also like this: among all scriptural dharmas expounded by Tathāgatas, Bodhisattvas, or Śrāvakas, it is foremost. Those who can receive and uphold this scripture are also like this: among all wandering beings, they are foremost.
Among all Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas, the Bodhisattva is foremost; this scripture is also like this: among all scriptural dharmas, it is foremost.
Just as the Buddha is the King of all dharmas; this scripture is also like this: the King of all scriptures.
Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña! This scripture can rescue all wandering beings; this scripture can cause all wandering beings to be free of suffering and afflictions; this scripture can greatly benefit all wandering beings, fulfilling their vows.
Like a cool, clear pond, able to satisfy all who are thirsty; like the cold finding fire, like the naked finding clothing, like merchants finding a caravan leader, like a child finding a mother, like a ferry finding a boat, like the sick finding a physician, like darkness finding a lamp, like the poor finding treasure, like subjects finding a king, like traders finding the ocean, like a torch dispelling darkness: this Lotus Sūtra is also like this, able to cause wandering beings to abandon all suffering and illness, able to untie all the bonds of birth and death.
If someone hears this Lotus Sūtra, whether copying it oneself or causing others to copy it, the merit attained, calculating it with the Buddha's wisdom, cannot reach its limit. If copying this scriptural scroll, making offerings of flowers, incense, necklaces, burning incense, powdered incense, paste incense, banners, canopies, clothing, and various lamps—ghee lamps, oil lamps, scented oil lamps, campaka oil lamps, sumanā oil lamps, pāṭala oil lamps, vārṣikā oil lamps, and navamallikā oil lamps—the merit attained is also immeasurable.
Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña! If someone hears this Former activities of Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva Chapter, they also attain immeasurable, boundless merit. If a woman hears this Former activities of Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva Chapter and can receive and uphold it, when this female body ends, she will never receive a female body again.
If after the Tathāgata's extinction, during the final five hundred years, a woman hears this scripture and practices as taught: dying here, she will immediately go to the Land of Joyful Ease, where Amitābha Buddha dwells surrounded by the assembly of great Bodhisattvas, born upon a jewel seat within a lotus flower, no longer afflicted by greed and lust, nor afflicted by anger and delusion, nor afflicted by pride, envy, and defilements; attaining the Bodhisattva's spiritual superknowledges and the forbearance of non-arising dharmas.
Having attained this forbearance, her eye faculty is pure; with this pure eye faculty, she beholds Buddhas, Tathāgatas equal to the sands of seven hundred and two thousand billion inconceivable numbers of Ganges rivers.
At that time, the Buddhas praise her from afar: 'Excellent, excellent! Good son! That within the Dharma of Śākyamuni Buddha you were able to receive, uphold, read, recite, and contemplate this scripture, and expound it for others, the merit you attain is immeasurable and boundless; fire cannot burn it, water cannot sweep it away; your virtues, a thousand Buddhas speaking together could not exhaust. You have now shattered the thieves of Mara, destroyed the army of birth and death, and crushed all other enemies. Good son! Hundreds of thousands of Buddhas guard you together through spiritual powers; in all the world, among devas and humans, none is equal to you, except the Tathāgata; among Śrāvakas, Pratyekabuddhas, down to Bodhisattvas, none possess wisdom and dhyāna meditation equal to yours!'
Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña! This Bodhisattva accomplishes such power of virtue and wisdom. If someone hears this Former activities of Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva Chapter, and can rejoice in accord and praise its goodness, in this present life the fragrance of a blue lotus flower will constantly issue from their mouth, and the fragrance of ox-head sandalwood will constantly issue from the pores of their body; the merits attained are as spoken above.
Therefore, Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña! I entrust this Former activities of Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva Chapter to you. After my extinction, in the final five hundred years, widely proclaim and propagate it in Jambudvīpa, do not allow it to be cut off, lest evil Maras, the subjects of Mara, devas, Nagas, yakṣas, and kumbhāṇḍas gain an opening!
Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña! You should guard this scripture through spiritual powers. For what reason? This scripture is good medicine for the illnesses of people in Jambudvīpa. If someone is ill and hears this scripture, their illness will be eliminated, free of old age and death.
Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña! If you see someone who upholds this scripture, you should take a blue lotus flower filled with powdered incense and scatter it over them as an offering. Having scattered it, have this thought: 'This person before long will certainly take grass and sit at the place of awakening, shattering the armies of Mara, blowing the Dharma conch, striking the great Dharma drum, and liberating all wandering beings from the ocean of old age, sickness, and death.'
Therefore, those who seek the Buddha path, seeing those who uphold this scripture, should give rise to such a reverent mind as this."
When this Former activities of Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva Chapter was expounded, eighty-four thousand Bodhisattvas attained the dhāraṇī of understanding the languages of all wandering beings.
Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata, from within the jewel stūpa, praised Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña Bodhisattva: "Excellent, excellent, Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña! You have accomplished inconceivable merits, that you are able to question Śākyamuni Buddha on such matters as these, benefiting immeasurable wandering beings!"
