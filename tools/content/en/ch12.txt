At that time, the Buddha addressed the Bodhisattvas, devas, humans, and the fourfold assembly: "In past immeasurable kalpas, I sought the Lotus Sūtra without weariness or fatigue. Throughout many kalpas, I constantly served as a king, making vows to seek unexcelled bodhi, my mind irreversible.
Wishing to fulfill the Six Pāramitās, I diligently practiced giving, without stinginess in heart: elephants, horses, the seven treasures, countries, cities, wives, children, male and female servants, attendants, head, eyes, marrow, brain, body, flesh, hands, and feet, not begrudging physical life.
At that time, the lifespan of people in the world was immeasurable. For the sake of the Dharma, I abandoned the royal throne, entrusted the government to the prince, struck the drum and proclaimed in the four directions, seeking the Dharma: 'Who can expound the Great Vehicle for me? I will serve and attend upon them for the rest of my life!'
At that time, an ascetic came and addressed the king: 'I possess the Great Vehicle, named the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma. If you do not disobey me, I will explain it to you.'
The king, hearing the ascetic's words, rejoiced and leaped, immediately following the ascetic, supplying what was needed: picking fruits, drawing water, gathering firewood, preparing food, and even using his body as a bed and seat, body and mind unwearied, serving him at that time. Throughout a thousand years, for the sake of the Dharma, he diligently attended upon him, leaving him lacking in nothing."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
I recall past kalpas,
For the sake of seeking the great Dharma;
Although a king of the world,
Not craving the pleasures of the five desires.
Striking the bell to announce to the four directions:
"Who possesses the great Dharma?
If someone explains it for me,
I will become their servant."
At that time there was the seer Asita,
Who came and addressed the great king:
"I have a subtle, sublime Dharma,
Rare in the world;
If you can practice it,
I will speak it for you."
The king, hearing the seer's words,
Gave rise to great joy in his heart,
Immediately following the seer,
Supplying whatever was needed,
Gathering firewood, fruits, and melons,
Respectfully presenting them in due season;
Because his heart cherished the wonderful Dharma,
Body and mind were free of weariness.
Universally for the sake of wandering beings,
Diligently seeking the great Dharma,
Not for his own body,
Nor for the pleasures of the five desires.
Thus as a great king,
Diligently seeking, he attained this Dharma,
And finally achieved Buddhahood;
Therefore I now speak it to you.
The Buddha addressed the bhikṣus: "The king at that time was myself; the aesthetic was Devadatta. Because Devadatta was a good friend, he caused me to be fully endowed with the Six Pāramitās, love, compassion, joy, equanimity, the thirty-two marks, the eighty subtle characteristics, purple-tinged golden color, the ten powers, the four fearlessnesses, the four means of embracing, and the eighteen distinct spiritual and path powers; accomplishing equal and right awakening, widely liberating wandering beings—all through the good friend Devadatta."
He addressed the fourfold assembly: "Devadatta, after immeasurable kalpas have passed, will become a Buddha named Devarāja Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha, Vidyācaraṇasampanna, Sugata, Lokavid, Anuttara, Puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstā Devamanuṣyāṇām, Buddha, Bhagavān. His world will be named Deva-satya.
At that time, Devarāja Buddha will dwell in the world for twenty intermediate kalpas, widely expounding the wonderful Dharma for wandering beings. Wandering beings equal to the sands of the Ganges will attain the arhat fruit; immeasurable wandering beings will generate the mind of Conditioned Awakening; wandering beings equal to the sands of the Ganges will generate the unexcelled path-mind, attaining the forbearance of non-arising dharmas and reaching non-retrogression.
When Devarāja Buddha enters parinirvāṇa, the Correct Dharma will abide in the world for twenty intermediate kalpas. A seven-jeweled stūpa will be erected for his complete-body relics, sixty yojanas in height, and forty yojanas in length and breadth. All devas and people will with various flowers, powdered incense, burning incense, paste incense, clothing, necklaces, banners, canopies, music, and hymns, bow down and make offerings to the seven-jeweled wondrous stūpa. Immeasurable wandering beings will attain the arhat fruit, immeasurable wandering beings will awaken to Pratyekabuddhahood, and inconceivable wandering beings will generate the bodhicitta, reaching non-retrogression."
The Buddha addressed the bhikṣus: "In future ages, if there are good men and good women who, hearing the Devadatta Chapter of the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma, with pure minds believe and revere it without giving rise to doubts, they will not fall into hells, hungry ghosts, or animals, but will be born before Buddhas of the ten directions; wherever they are born, they will constantly hear this scripture. If born among humans or devas, they will experience superior, subtle joy; if born before a Buddha, they will be born by transformation from a lotus flower."
At that time, an attendant Bodhisattva of Prabhūtaratna World-Honored One from below, named Prajñākūṭa, addressed Prabhūtaratna Buddha: "We should now return to our native land."
Śākyamuni Buddha said to Prajñākūṭa: "Good son! Wait an instant. Here is a Bodhisattva named Mañjuśrī; you can meet with him and discuss the wonderful Dharma, and then return to your native land."
Then Mañjuśrī, seated upon a thousand-petaled lotus as large as a carriage wheel—the accompanying Bodhisattvas also seated on jewel lotuses—spontaneously sprang forth from the great ocean, from the palace of Sāgara Naga King, suspended in open space, went to Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, descended from the lotuses, went to where the Buddhas were, and bowed reverently with his head to the feet of the two World-Honored Ones. Having completed his reverence, he went to where Prajñākūṭa was, exchanged greetings, and withdrew to sit on one side.
Prajñākūṭa Bodhisattva asked Mañjuśrī: "Virtuous one, going to the Naga palace, how many wandering beings did you transform?"
Mañjuśrī said: "Their numbers are immeasurable, beyond calculation, cannot be proclaimed by the mouth, nor fathomed by the mind. Wait an instant, and there will be proof of itself."
Before he had finished speaking, countless Bodhisattvas seated upon jewel lotuses sprang forth from the sea, went to Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, and resided in open space. All these Bodhisattvas were transformed and liberated by Mañjuśrī, endowed with Bodhisattva conduct, all discussing the Six Pāramitās together. Those who had originally been Śrāvakas, while in open space, had spoken of Śrāvaka practices, but now were all cultivating the Great Vehicle meaning of emptiness.
Mañjuśrī said to Prajñākūṭa: "The teaching and transformation in the ocean were like this."
Then Prajñākūṭa Bodhisattva praised in gāthās:
Great in wisdom, virtue, and valor,
Transforming and liberating immeasurable multitudes;
Now this great assembly,
And I have all seen it.
Proclaiming the meaning of the genuine mark,
Revealing and clarifying the One Vehicle Dharma,
Widely guiding all wandering beings,
Causing them swiftly to accomplish bodhi.
Mañjuśrī said: "Within the ocean, I constantly proclaimed only the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma."
Prajñākūṭa asked Mañjuśrī: "This scripture is profound, subtle, and sublime, a treasure among scriptures, rare in the world. Are there wandering beings who, diligently exerting vigor and practicing this scripture, swiftly attain Buddhahood?"
Mañjuśrī said: "There is the daughter of Sāgara Naga King, just eight years old, whose wisdom faculties are sharp, skillfully knowing the faculties and karmic actions of wandering beings, having attained dhāraṇī, able fully to receive and uphold the profound, secret treasuries expounded by the Buddhas. Deeply entering dhyāna meditation, penetrating all dharmas, in an instant she generated the bodhicitta, attaining non-retrogression, with unhindered eloquence. Lovingly mindful of wandering beings as of infant children, her merits fully perfected, what she contemplates in mind and proclaims with mouth is subtle, sublime, and vast, compassionate, benevolent, yielding, her resolve gentle and elegant, able to arrive at bodhi."
Prajñākūṭa Bodhisattva said: "I see that Śākyamuni Tathāgata, throughout immeasurable kalpas of difficult and bitter practices, accumulated merit and piled up virtue, seeking the path of bodhi without resting. Observing the great three-thousand-fold worlds, there is not even a spot as small as a mustard seed that is not a place where the Bodhisattva cast down body and life for the sake of wandering beings; and only afterward did he accomplish the path of bodhi. I do not believe that this girl in the space of an instant could accomplish right awakening."
Before the discussion ended, the Naga king's daughter suddenly appeared before them, bowed reverently with her head touching the ground, withdrew to stand on one side, and praised in gāthās:
Deeply penetrating the marks of transgressions and blessings,
Universally illuminating the ten directions;
The subtle, sublime, pure Dharma-body,
Endowed with the thirty-two marks,
With eighty subtle characteristics,
Adorning the Dharma-body.
Looked up to by devas and humans,
Revered by Nagas and spirits,
All classes of wandering beings,
None fail to venerate.
Furthermore, hearing of accomplishing bodhi,
Only the Buddha can bear witness;
I proclaim the Great Vehicle teaching,
Liberating suffering wandering beings.
Then Śāriputra said to the Naga girl: "You state that before long you will attain the unexcelled path; this matter is difficult to believe.
For what reason? A woman's body is filthy and impure, not a vessel for Dharma; how could you attain unexcelled bodhi? The Buddha path is vast and distant; only after immeasurable kalpas of enduring hardship, piling up practices, and fully cultivating all pāramitās can it be accomplished.
Furthermore, a woman's body has five obstacles: first, she cannot become a Brahmā King; second, Śakra; third, a Mara King; fourth, a wheel-turning sage king; fifth, the body of a Buddha. How could a woman's body swiftly attain Buddhahood?"
At that time, the Naga girl had a precious jewel, worth the great three-thousand-fold worlds, and presented it to the Buddha. The Buddha immediately accepted it.
The Naga girl said to Prajñākūṭa Bodhisattva and the Venerable Śāriputra: "I offered the precious jewel, and the World-Honored One accepted it. Was this swift or not?"
They answered: "Extremely swift."
The girl said: "With your spiritual powers, observe me becoming a Buddha, swifter even than this!"
At that moment, the entire assembly saw the Naga girl suddenly transform into a being endowed with Bodhisattva conduct, immediately go to the southern Vimala world, sit upon a jewel lotus, and accomplish equal and right awakening, with thirty-two marks and eighty subtle characteristics, universally expounding the wonderful Dharma for all wandering beings in the ten directions.
Then in the Sahā world, the Bodhisattvas, Śrāvakas, the eightfold assemblies of devas and Nagas, humans, and non-humans, from afar saw the Naga girl become a Buddha, universally expounding the Dharma for the devas and humans assembled at that time, and rejoiced greatly, all bowing in reverence from afar.
Immeasurable wandering beings, hearing the Dharma, understood and awakened, attaining non-retrogression; immeasurable wandering beings received prophecies of the path. The Vimala world shook in six ways; in the Sahā world, three thousand wandering beings came to dwell on the ground of non-retrogression, and three thousand wandering beings generated the bodhicitta and received prophecies. Prajñākūṭa Bodhisattva, Śāriputra, and the entire assembly silently believed and accepted.
