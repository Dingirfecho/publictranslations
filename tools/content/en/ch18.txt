At that time, Maitreya Bodhisattva Mahāsattva addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! If there is a good man or good woman who, hearing this Lotus Sūtra, rejoices in accord, how much merit does that person attain?"
And he spoke gāthās:
After the World-Honored One passes into extinction,
If there are those who hear this scripture,
If they can rejoice in accord,
How much merit do they attain?
At that time, the Buddha addressed Maitreya Bodhisattva Mahāsattva: "Ajita! After the Tathāgata's extinction, if a bhikṣu, bhikṣuṇī, upāsaka, upāsikā, or other wise person, whether aged or young, hearing this scripture, rejoices in accord, emerges from the Dharma assembly, and goes elsewhere—whether in a monastery, an empty leisure place, a city, an alley, a village, or a hamlet—and according to what they heard, expounds it according to their capacity for parents, relatives, good friends, and acquaintances; and these people, having heard it, rejoice in accord and in turn teach others; and those other people, having heard it, also rejoice in accord and in turn teach others; passing along in this manner successively to the fiftieth person:
Ajita! The merit of joyful acceptance of that fiftieth good man or good woman, I will now speak of it; listen well!
Suppose in four hundred million billion incalculable worlds, all wandering beings of the six destinies and four modes of birth—egg-born, womb-born, moisture-born, and transformation-born, whether having form or formless, conscious or unconscious, neither conscious nor unconscious, footless, two-footed, four-footed, or many-footed, all such as are numbered among wandering beings—suppose someone seeking merit gives them all the articles of entertainment according to their desires. To each wandering being, he gives Jambudvīpa full of gold, silver, lapis lazuli, mother-of-pearl, agate, coral, amber, and various wonderful treasures, as well as elephants, horses, carriages, and palaces and pavilions made of the seven treasures.
This great donor, having practiced giving in this manner for a full eighty years, has this thought: 'I have already bestowed upon wandering beings articles of entertainment according to their desires; yet these wandering beings are all now old and decrepit, over eighty years of age, with white hair and wrinkled faces, approaching death before long. I should instruct and guide them through the Buddha-Dharma.'
Immediately he gathers these wandering beings, proclaims the Dharma transformation, instructing, benefiting, and delighting them, so that all at once they attain the srotāpanna path, sakṛdāgāmin path, anāgāmin path, and arhat path, exhausting all outpourings, attaining mastery in deep dhyāna meditations, endowed with the eight liberations.
What do you think: is the merit attained by this great donor plentiful or not?"
Maitreya addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! This person's merit is exceedingly plentiful, immeasurable and boundless. Even if this donor had merely given all articles of joy to wandering beings, his merit would be immeasurable; how much more so causing them to attain the arhat fruit!"
The Buddha addressed Maitreya: "I now clearly tell you: this person, with all articles of joy, gives to wandering beings of the six destinies in four hundred million billion incalculable worlds, and causes them to attain the arhat fruit; yet the merit he attains does not equal that of this fiftieth person who, hearing a single gāthā of the Lotus Sūtra, rejoices in accord: it does not equal one part in a hundred, one part in a thousand, or one part in hundreds of thousands of millions of billions; even calculation and parables cannot know it.
Ajita! If the merit of joyful acceptance of the fiftieth person who successively hears the Lotus Sūtra is immeasurable, boundless, and incalculable like this, how much more so one who initially hears it in the assembly and rejoices in accord! That merit is supreme, immeasurable, boundless, and incalculable, beyond all comparison.
Furthermore, Ajita! If a person, for the sake of this scripture, goes to a monastery, and whether sitting or standing listens and receives it even for an instant, through the causal condition of this merit, in their next rebirth they will attain fine, superior elephants, horses, carriages, palanquins adorned with rare jewels, and ride in heavenly palaces.
If someone sits in the place where the Dharma is expounded, and when another person arrives, urges them to sit and listen, or shares their seat to let them sit, through the causal condition of this merit, in their next rebirth they will attain the seat of Śakra, the seat of Brahmā King, or the seat of a wheel-turning sage king.
Ajita! If someone says to another person: 'There is a scripture named the Lotus; let us go together to listen to it,' and that person follows the instruction and listens even for an instant, through the causal condition of this merit, in their next rebirth they will be born together with dhāraṇī Bodhisattvas.
With sharp faculties and wisdom, for hundreds of thousands of lifetimes they will never be mute; their breath will not be foul; their tongue will constantly be free of disease, and their mouth free of disease; their teeth will not be stained, black, yellow, or sparse, nor missing, uneven, or crooked; their lips will not droop, shrink, be rough, ulcerated, cleft, skewed, thick, large, or dark, having no repulsive marks.
Their nose will not be flat or crooked; their facial complexion will not be dark, narrow, long, hollow, or twisted, free of all unpleasing marks. Their lips, tongue, and teeth will all be adorned and fine; their nose long, high, and straight; their face full and round; their eyebrows high and long; their forehead broad and flat; fully endowed with human marks, life after life seeing the Buddha, hearing the Dharma, and faithfully receiving instruction.
Ajita! Look upon this: the merit of encouraging one person to go listen to the Dharma is like this; how much more so single-mindedly listening, speaking, reading, reciting, distinguishing it for others amidst the great assembly, and practicing as taught!"
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
If a person in the Dharma assembly,
Attains hearing this scripture,
Even down to a single gāthā,
Rejoices in accord and speaks it to others,
In this manner successively teaching,
Reaching the fiftieth person;
The merit attained by this final person,
I will now distinguish it.
Suppose a great donor,
Supplies immeasurable multitudes,
Fulfilling eighty years,
Accordant with all their desires.
Seeing their marks of old age,
White hair and wrinkled faces,
Sparse teeth and withered forms,
Thinking of their approaching death:
"I should now teach them,
Causing them to attain the fruits of the path."
Immediately through expedient means,
Expounding the genuine Dharma of nirvāṇa:
"The world is entirely unsolid,
Like foam, bubbles, or flames on water;
You all should
Quickly give rise to aversion."
All the people, hearing this Dharma,
All attain arhatship,
Endowed with the six spiritual superknowledges,
The three knowledges, and eight liberations.
The final fiftieth person,
Hearing a single gāthā and rejoicing in accord,
This person's merit surpasses the former,
Beyond all parables.
Successively hearing it like this,
That merit is already immeasurable;
How much more so in the Dharma assembly,
One who initially hears and rejoices in accord!
If someone encourages a single person,
Leading them to listen to the Lotus,
Saying: "This scripture is profound and subtle,
Encountered once in tens of millions of kalpas";
Receiving the teaching, they go to listen,
Even if hearing for an instant;
The karmic retribution of this person,
I will now distinguish and speak of it.
Life after life free of mouth afflictions,
Teeth not sparse, yellow, or black;
Lips neither thick, shrunken, nor cleft,
Free of all repulsive marks;
Tongue not dry, black, or short;
Nose high, long, and straight;
Forehead broad and flat;
Face and eyes upright and dignified;
Delighted in by people who see them;
Breath free of foul stench,
The fragrance of the utpala flower
Constantly issuing from their mouth.
If someone goes intentionally to a monastery,
Wishing to listen to the Lotus Sūtra,
Hearing it for an instant with joy,
I will now speak of their merit:
Reborn among devas and humans,
Attaining wondrous elephants, horses, carriages,
Palanquins of rare jewels,
And riding in heavenly palaces.
If in the place of expounding the Dharma,
One urges another to sit and listen to the scripture,
Through the causal conditions of this merit,
One attains the seats of Śakra, Brahmā, and wheel-turning kings.
How much more so single-mindedly listening,
Explaining its meaning,
And practicing as taught;
That merit is beyond measure!
