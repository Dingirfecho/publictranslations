Thus have I heard:
At one time, the Buddha was dwelling in the City of Royal Palaces, on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, together with a great assembly of twelve thousand bhikṣus. All were arhats whose outpourings were exhausted, who had no further afflictions, had attained their own benefit, had exhausted the bonds of existence, and whose minds had attained mastery. Their names were: Ājñātakauṇḍinya, Mahākāśyapa, Uruvilvākāśyapa, Gayākāśyapa, Nadīkāśyapa, Śāriputra, Mahāmaudgalyāyana, Mahākātyāyana, Aniruddha, Kapphiṇa, Gavāmpati, Revata, Pilindavatsa, Bakkula, Mahākausthila, Nanda, Sundarananda, Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇīputra, Subhūti, Ānanda, and Rāhula—such great arhats known to the assembly.
Furthermore, there were two thousand practitioners in training and beyond training. The bhikṣuṇī Mahāprajāpatī was present with her six thousand attendants. The bhikṣuṇī Yaśodharā, mother of Rāhula, was also present with her attendants.
There were eighty thousand Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas, all irreversible from anuttarasamyaksambodhi. All had attained dhāraṇī, possessed pleasant and unhindered eloquence, and turned the irreversible wheel of Dharma. They had made offerings to immeasurable hundreds of thousands of Buddhas, planted roots of virtue under those Buddhas, and were constantly praised by the Buddhas. They cultivated their bodies with compassion, skillfully entered the Buddha's wisdom, penetrated great wisdom, and arrived at the other shore. Their fame was heard universally throughout immeasurable worlds, and they were able to liberate countless hundreds of thousands of wandering beings.
Their names were: Mañjuśrī Bodhisattva, Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, Mahāsthāmaprāpta Bodhisattva, Nityodyukta Bodhisattva, Anikṣiptadhura Bodhisattva, Ratnapāṇi Bodhisattva, Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva, Pradānaśūra Bodhisattva, Ratnacandra Bodhisattva, Candraprabha Bodhisattva, Pūrṇacandra Bodhisattva, Mahābala Bodhisattva, Anantabala Bodhisattva, Trailokavikrāmin Bodhisattva, Bhadrapāla Bodhisattva, Maitreya Bodhisattva, Ratnākara Bodhisattva, and Nāyaka Bodhisattva—such Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas, eighty thousand in all.
At that time, Śakra Devānām Indra was present with his retinue of twenty thousand sons of devas. There were also the son of devas Candradeva, the son of devas Gandhadeva, the son of devas Prabhāketu, and the Four Heavenly Kings with their retinue of ten thousand sons of devas. The son of devas Īśvara and the son of devas Maheśvara were present with their retinue of thirty thousand sons of devas. The lord of the Sahā world, Brahmā King Śikhin Mahābrahmā and Jyotiṣprabha Mahābrahmā were present with their retinue of twelve thousand sons of devas.
There were eight Naga kings: Nanda Naga King, Upananda Naga King, Sāgara Naga King, Vāsuki Naga King, Takṣaka Naga King, Anavatapta Naga King, Manasvin Naga King, and Utpala Naga King, each with several hundred thousand attendants.
There were four kiṃnara kings: Dharma Kiṃnara King, Sudharma Kiṃnara King, Mahādharma Kiṃnara King, and Dharmadhara Kiṃnara King, each with several hundred thousand attendants.
There were four gandharva kings: Gīta Gandharva King, Gītarava Gandharva King, Madhura Gandharva King, and Madhurasvaraghoṣa Gandharva King, each with several hundred thousand attendants.
There were four asura kings: Bali Asura King, Kharaskandha Asura King, Vemacitrin Asura King, and Rāhu Asura King, each with several hundred thousand attendants.
There were four garuḍa kings: Mahātejas Garuḍa King, Mahākāya Garuḍa King, Mahāpūrṇa Garuḍa King, and Ṛddhimat Garuḍa King, each with several hundred thousand attendants.
King Ajātaśatru, son of Vaidehī, was present with several hundred thousand attendants. Each bowed to the Buddha's feet and withdrew to sit on one side.
At that time, the World-Honored One, surrounded by the fourfold assembly, received offerings, reverence, veneration, and praise. For the sake of the Bodhisattvas, he expounded the Mahāyāna sūtra named Innumerable Meanings, a teaching for Bodhisattvas and guarded in the mindfulness of the Buddhas.
When the Buddha finished expounding this sūtra, he sat cross-legged and entered the samādhi of the Abode of Innumerable Meanings, his body and mind unmoving. At that time, heaven rained down māndārava flowers, mahā-māndārava flowers, mañjūṣaka flowers, and mahā-mañjūṣaka flowers, scattering them over the Buddha and the great assembly. The universal Buddha-worlds shook in six ways.
Then in the assembly, the bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, upāsikās, devas, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, non-humans, petty kings, and wheel-turning sage kings—this entire great assembly—attained what they had never experienced before. Joyful with joined palms, they single-mindedly gazed upon the Buddha.
Then the Buddha radiated a light from the white hair between his eyebrows, illuminating eighteen thousand worlds to the east without omission, reaching downward to the Avīci Hell and upward to the Akaniṣṭha Heaven. From this world, all the wandering beings of the six destinies in those lands were fully seen; the present Buddhas of those lands were seen, and the sūtra teachings expounded by those Buddhas were heard. The bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, and upāsikās of those lands who practiced the path and attained realization were also seen.
Furthermore, the Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas were seen practicing the Bodhisattva path through various causal conditions, various faiths and understandings, and various appearances. The Buddhas entering parinirvāṇa were seen. After those Buddhas entered parinirvāṇa, seven-jeweled stūpas erected with their relics were also seen.
At that time, Maitreya Bodhisattva had this thought: Now the World-Honored One displays signs of spiritual transformations; for what cause is there this auspicious sign? Now the Buddha, the World-Honored One, has entered samādhi; this is an inconceivable, rare occurrence. Whom should I ask? Who can answer this?
He further thought: This Mañjuśrī, son of the Dharma King, has already attended upon and made offerings to past immeasurable Buddhas; he must have seen such rare signs. I will now ask him.
At that time, the bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, upāsikās, devas, nāgas, ghosts, and spirits all had this thought: Regarding this sign of the Buddha's radiant light and spiritual powers, whom should we now ask?
Then Maitreya Bodhisattva, wishing to resolve his own doubt and observing the thoughts of the fourfold assembly of bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, and upāsikās, as well as the gathered assembly of devas, nāgas, ghosts, and spirits, addressed Mañjuśrī:
For what cause is there this auspicious sign of spiritual power, emitting a great light illuminating eighteen thousand lands to the east, so that the adornments of those Buddha realms are fully seen?
Thereupon, Maitreya Bodhisattva, wishing to restate this meaning, inquired in gāthās:
Mañjuśrī! For what reason does the Guide,
From the white curl between his eyebrows,
Radiate a vast light universally illuminating?
Raining down māndārava
And mañjūṣaka flowers,
With sandalwood scented breezes
Delighting the minds of the assembly?
By this causal condition,
The earth is completely adorned and purified,
And this world
Shakes in six ways.
At that time the fourfold assembly
Are all filled with joy,
Body and mind delighted,
Attaining what they never had before.
The light from between his eyebrows
Illuminates the eastern direction,
Eighteen thousand lands,
All turning golden in color,
From the Avīci Hell
Upward to the Peak of Existence.
Within all those worlds,
The wandering beings of the six destinies,
The destinations of their births and deaths,
The karmic conditions of good and evil,
And their retributions fine or ugly,
Are all seen from here.
Furthermore, one beholds the Buddhas,
The holy masters and lions,
Expounding the scriptural texts,
Subtle, sublime, and foremost.
Their voices are pure,
Emitting gentle sounds,
Teaching the Bodhisattvas
Countless hundreds of millions,
With deep and subtle Brahma-sounds
Causing people to rejoice in hearing them.
Each within their own worlds
Expounds the correct Dharma
Through various causal conditions.
By immeasurable parables,
They illuminate the Buddha-Dharma
To awaken wandering beings.
If a person encounters suffering,
Weary of aging, sickness, and death,
They speak to them of nirvāṇa,
Exhausting the limits of suffering.
If a person has merit,
Having made offerings to Buddhas,
Aspiring to seek the supreme Dharma,
They speak to them of Conditioned Awakening.
If there are Buddha-children
Cultivating various practices,
Seeking unexcelled wisdom,
They speak to them of the pure path.
Mañjuśrī!
Dwelling here,
I see and hear such things
And thousands of millions of occurrences,
Vast numbers like these,
Which I will now briefly state.
I see in those lands
Bodhisattvas like Ganges sands,
Through various causal conditions,
Seeking the Buddha path.
Some practice giving
Of gold, silver, and coral,
Pearls and mani jewels,
Mother-of-pearl and agate,
Diamonds and various treasures,
Servants, carriages,
And palanquins adorned with jewels,
Joyfully giving them away.
Dedicating them to the Buddha path,
They vow to attain this vehicle,
Foremost in the three realms,
Praised by all the Buddhas.
There are Bodhisattvas who give
Precious carriages drawn by four steeds,
With railings and canopies,
And decorated canopies.
Again I see Bodhisattvas
Giving their flesh, hands, and feet,
As well as wives and children,
Seeking the unexcelled path.
Again I see Bodhisattvas
Giving heads, eyes, and bodies
With joy and delight,
Seeking the Buddha's wisdom.
Mañjuśrī!
I see various kings
Going to where the Buddhas dwell,
Inquiring of the unexcelled path,
Forthwith abandoning joyful lands,
Palaces, ministers, and consorts,
Shaving off beard and hair,
To put on the Dharma robes.
Some see Bodhisattvas
Becoming bhikṣus,
Dwelling alone in quiet leisure,
Delighting in reciting scriptures.
Again I see Bodhisattvas,
Vigorous and heroic,
Entering deep mountains
To contemplate the Buddha path.
Again I see those free of desires
Constantly dwelling in emptiness and leisure,
Deeply cultivating dhyāna meditation,
Attaining the five spiritual superknowledges.
Again I see Bodhisattvas
Resting in meditation with joined palms,
With thousands of millions of verses
Praising the Dharma Kings.
Again I see Bodhisattvas
Of deep wisdom and firm resolve,
Able to question the Buddhas,
Hearing and upholding all.
Again I see Buddha-children
Endowed with meditation and wisdom,
With immeasurable parables
Expounding the Dharma for the assembly.
Joyfully speaking the Dharma,
Transforming the Bodhisattvas,
Shattering the hosts of Maras,
And striking the Dharma drum.
Again I see Bodhisattvas
Silent and tranquil,
Revered by devas and Nagas,
Yet not finding joy in it.
Again I see Bodhisattvas
Dwelling in forests, emitting light,
Relieving the suffering of hells,
Causing them to enter the Buddha path.
Again I see Buddha-children
Who have never slept,
Walking in the forests,
Diligently seeking the Buddha path.
Again I see those endowed with precepts,
Flawless in deportment,
Pure like precious jewels,
To seek the Buddha path.
Again I see Buddha-children
Dwelling in the power of patience,
When people of arrogant pride
Abuse and strike them,
Able to endure it all,
To seek the Buddha path.
Again I see Bodhisattvas
Free from frivolous laughter
And foolish companions,
Drawing close to the wise,
Single-mindedly removing distraction,
Collecting their thoughts in mountain forests
For hundreds of thousands of millions of years,
To seek the Buddha path.
Some see Bodhisattvas
With delicious foods and drinks,
And a hundred kinds of medicinal broths,
Giving to the Buddha and Saṅgha.
Renowned robes of superior cloth,
Worth thousands and millions,
Or priceless garments,
Giving to the Buddha and Saṅgha.
Thousands of millions of varieties
Of sandalwood precious dwellings,
And exquisite bedding,
Giving to the Buddha and Saṅgha.
Pure gardens and forests,
With lush flowers and fruits,
Flowing springs and bathing ponds,
Giving to the Buddha and Saṅgha.
Givings such as these,
Of various subtle kinds,
Rejoicing without weariness,
Seeking the unexcelled path.
There are Bodhisattvas
Speaking the Dharma of tranquil extinction,
With various instructions
Teaching countless wandering beings.
Some see Bodhisattvas
Contemplating the nature of all dharmas
As having no dual marks,
Just like empty space.
Again I see Buddha-children
With minds having no attachment,
Using this wonderful wisdom
To seek the unexcelled path.
Mañjuśrī!
Furthermore, there are Bodhisattvas
Who, after the Buddha's extinction,
Make offerings to the relics.
Again I see Buddha-children
Constructing stūpas and temples,
Countless as the sands of the Ganges,
Adorning the lands.
Precious stūpas lofty and sublime,
Five thousand yojanas high,
Their length and breadth equal,
Two thousand yojanas wide.
Each stūpa and shrine
Has a thousand banners and streamers,
Beaded curtains hung all around,
Precious bells chiming in harmony.
All devas, Nagas, and spirits,
Humans and non-humans,
With incense, flowers, and music,
Constantly make offerings.
Mañjuśrī!
All these Buddha-children,
To make offerings to the relics,
Adorn the stūpas and shrines;
The lands are naturally
Extraordinarily fine and wonderful,
Like the heavenly King of Trees
With its flowers blossoming open.
The Buddha emits a single light,
And I and the assembled gathering
Behold these lands,
Variously extraordinary and wonderful.
The spiritual powers of the Buddhas
And their wisdom are exceedingly rare;
Emitting a single pure light,
It illuminates immeasurable lands.
We, seeing this,
Have attained what we never had before.
Buddha-child Mañjuśrī!
We pray you will resolve the assembly's doubts.
The fourfold assembly looks up with hope,
Gazing upon you and me.
For what cause does the World-Honored One
Emit this radiant light?
Buddha-child, answer in due time;
Resolve our doubts and make us rejoice.
What benefit is brought forth
By displaying this radiant light?
That wonderful Dharma the Buddha attained
Sitting in the place of awakening—
Does he wish to expound it now?
Or is he about to bestow prophecies?
To display all the Buddha-lands,
Adorned and pure with many jewels,
And to reveal all the Buddhas—
This is no small cause.
Mañjuśrī, you must know,
The fourfold assembly, Nagas, and spirits
Gaze upon you, the virtuous one:
What is it that will be expounded?
Then Mañjuśrī addressed Maitreya Bodhisattva Mahāsattva and all the great beings:
Good children! As I consider it, now the Buddha, the World-Honored One, wishes to expound the great Dharma, rain down the great Dharma rain, blow the great Dharma conch, strike the great Dharma drum, and proclaim the great Dharma meaning. Good children! In the presence of past Buddhas, I have seen this auspicious sign. Having emitted this light, they immediately expounded the great Dharma. Therefore, you should know that the present Buddha displaying this light is also like this; wishing to cause wandering beings to hear and know the Dharma difficult to believe in all the world, he displays this auspicious sign.
Good children! In the past, immeasurable, boundless, inconceivable, incalculable kalpas ago, there was a Buddha named Candrasūryapradīpa Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha, Vidyācaraṇasampanna, Sugata, Lokavid, Anuttara, Puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstā Devamanuṣyāṇām, Buddha, Bhagavān.
He expounded the correct Dharma, good in the beginning, good in the middle, and good in the end, its meaning profound and far-reaching, its words skillful and subtle, pure and unmixed, complete with the marks of pure Brahma conduct. For those seeking to be Śrāvakas, he expounded the Dharma responding to the Four Noble Truths, to cross beyond birth, old age, sickness, and death, to ultimate nirvāṇa. For those seeking to be Pratyekabuddhas, he expounded the Dharma responding to the Twelve Links of Dependent Origination. For Bodhisattvas, he expounded the Dharma responding to the Six Pāramitās, causing them to attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi and accomplish all-knowledge.
Next, there was another Buddha also named Candrasūryapradīpa, and next another Buddha also named Candrasūryapradīpa. In this way, there were twenty thousand Buddhas all with the same name, Candrasūryapradīpa, and also with the same clan name, Bhāradvāja. Maitreya, you should know that from the first Buddha to the last Buddha, all had the same name, Candrasūryapradīpa, complete with the ten titles. The Dharma they expounded was good in the beginning, middle, and end.
Before the last Buddha left home, he had eight princely sons: the first named Mati, the second Sumati, the third Anantamati, the fourth Ratnamati, the fifth Viśeṣamati, the sixth Vimatisamudghātin, the seventh Ghōṣamati, and the eighth Dharmamati. These eight princes possessed sovereign majesty and virtue, each ruling over the four continents. When these princes heard that their father had left home and attained anuttarasamyaksambodhi, they all abandoned the royal throne and likewise left home, generating the Mahāyāna resolve, constantly cultivating Brahma conduct. All became Dharma teachers, having already planted roots of virtue under thousands of millions of Buddhas.
At that time, Candrasūryapradīpa Buddha expounded the Mahāyāna sūtra named Innumerable Meanings, a teaching for Bodhisattvas and guarded in the mindfulness of the Buddhas. Having expounded this sūtra, he immediately sat cross-legged amidst the great assembly and entered the samādhi of the Abode of Innumerable Meanings, his body and mind unmoving. At that time, heaven rained down māndārava flowers, mahā-māndārava flowers, mañjūṣaka flowers, and mahā-mañjūṣaka flowers, scattering them over the Buddha and the great assembly. The universal Buddha-worlds shook in six ways.
Then in the assembly, the bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, upāsikās, devas, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, non-humans, petty kings, and wheel-turning sage kings—this entire great assembly—attained what they had never experienced before. Joyful with joined palms, they single-mindedly gazed upon the Buddha.
Then the Tathāgata emitted a light from the white hair between his eyebrows, illuminating eighteen thousand Buddha lands to the east without omission, just like all the Buddha lands now seen. Maitreya, you should know that in the assembly at that time, there were twenty billion Bodhisattvas who rejoiced in wishing to hear the Dharma. When these Bodhisattvas saw this light universally illuminating the Buddha lands, they attained what they had never experienced before, wishing to know the causal conditions of this light.
At that time, there was a Bodhisattva named Varaprabha, who had eight hundred disciples. Then Candrasūryapradīpa Buddha arose from samādhi, and on account of Varaprabha Bodhisattva, expounded the Mahāyāna sūtra named the Lotus Flower of the Wonderful Dharma, a teaching for Bodhisattvas and guarded in the mindfulness of the Buddhas. For sixty minor kalpas he did not rise from his seat. The listeners in the assembly also sat in one place for sixty minor kalpas, their bodies and minds unmoving, listening to what the Buddha expounded as if it were the space of a meal. At that time, not a single person in the assembly gave rise to weariness in body or mind.
Candrasūryapradīpa Buddha, having expounded this sūtra for sixty minor kalpas, forthwith in the midst of the Brahmās, Maras, śramaṇas, brāhmaṇas, devas, humans, and asuras, proclaimed these words: Today at midnight, the Tathāgata will enter nirvāṇa without remainder.
At that time, there was a Bodhisattva named Deva-kūṭa; Candrasūryapradīpa Buddha immediately conferred a prophecy upon him, telling the bhikṣus: This Deva-kūṭa Bodhisattva will next become a Buddha, named Śuddhakāya Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha.
After the Buddha conferred the prophecy, at midnight he entered nirvāṇa without remainder. Following the Buddha's extinction, Varaprabha Bodhisattva upheld the Sūtra of the Lotus Flower of the Wonderful Dharma, expounding it to people for a full eighty minor kalpas. The eight sons of Candrasūryapradīpa Buddha all took Varaprabha as their teacher. Varaprabha taught and transformed them, causing them to become firm in anuttarasamyaksambodhi.
These princes, having made offerings to immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of Buddhas, all attained the Buddha path. The last to attain Buddhahood was named Dīpaṃkara.
Among the eight hundred disciples, there was one named Yaśaskāma, who was greedily attached to gain and offerings. Although he repeatedly read and recited various sūtras, he did not penetrate them fluently and forgot much; therefore, he was named Yaśaskāma. Because this person had also planted wholesome roots, he was able to encounter immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of Buddhas, making offerings, honoring, respecting, and praising them.
Maitreya, you should know: could Varaprabha Bodhisattva at that time have been someone else? It was I myself; and Yaśaskāma Bodhisattva was you yourself. Now seeing this auspicious sign, it is no different from the original one. Therefore I consider: Today the Tathāgata will expound the Mahāyāna sūtra named the Lotus Flower of the Wonderful Dharma, a teaching for Bodhisattvas and guarded in the mindfulness of the Buddhas.
Then Mañjuśrī, in the midst of the great assembly, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
I recall the past world,
Immeasurable, countless kalpas ago,
There was a Buddha, Honored Among Humans,
Named Candrasūryapradīpa.
The World-Honored One expounded the Dharma,
Liberating immeasurable wandering beings,
And countless millions of Bodhisattvas,
Causing them to enter the Buddha's wisdom.
Before the Buddha left home,
The eight princes born to him,
Seeing the Great Sage leave home,
Also followed to cultivate Brahma conduct.
Then the Buddha expounded the Great Vehicle,
The sūtra named Innumerable Meanings,
Amidst the great assembly,
Widely distinguishing it for them.
When the Buddha finished speaking this sūtra,
Forthwith upon the Dharma seat,
He sat cross-legged in samādhi,
Named the Abode of Innumerable Meanings.
Heaven rained māndārava flowers,
Heavenly drums naturally sounded,
Devas, Nagas, ghosts, and spirits
Made offerings to the Honored Among Humans.
All the Buddha lands
Immediately shook greatly.
The Buddha emitted light from between his eyebrows,
Displaying various rare matters.
This light illuminated the east,
Eighteen thousand Buddha lands,
Revealing to all wandering beings
The places of their karmic retributions of birth and death.
One saw the Buddha lands
Adorned with various jewels,
Colors of lapis lazuli and crystal,
All illuminated by the Buddha's light.
And one saw devas, humans,
Nāgas, spirits, yakṣa hosts,
Gandharvas, and kiṃnaras,
Each making offerings to their Buddhas.
Again one saw the Tathāgatas,
Spontaneously attaining the Buddha path,
Their body color like golden mountains,
Upright, majestic, subtle, and sublime.
Like pure lapis lazuli,
Internally revealing an image of pure gold.
The World-Honored One amidst the great assembly
Expounded the profound Dharma meaning.
In each of the Buddha lands,
The Śrāvaka assemblies were countless;
Because of the illumination of the Buddha's light,
Those great assemblies were fully seen.
There were bhikṣus
Dwelling in mountain forests,
Vigorously upholding pure precepts,
As if guarding a bright pearl.
Again one saw Bodhisattvas
Practicing giving, patience, and the like,
Their numbers like Ganges sands,
All illuminated by the Buddha's light.
Again one saw Bodhisattvas
Deeply entering various dhyāna meditations,
Body and mind quiet and unmoving,
To seek the unexcelled path.
Again one saw Bodhisattvas
Knowing the mark of tranquil extinction of dharmas,
Each within their own land
Speaking the Dharma to seek the Buddha path.
At that time the fourfold assembly,
Seeing Candrasūryapradīpa Buddha
Display great spiritual powers,
Were all joyful in their minds,
Each asking one another:
For what causal condition is this matter?
The Honored One revered by devas and humans
Just then arose from samādhi,
Praising Varaprabha Bodhisattva:
You are the eye of the world,
The refuge of all,
Able to uphold the Dharma treasury;
The Dharma as I expound it,
Only you can bear witness to and know.
The World-Honored One, having praised him,
Caused Varaprabha to rejoice,
And expounded this Lotus Sūtra,
For sixty minor kalpas,
Without rising from this seat.
The supreme, wonderful Dharma spoken,
This Dharma Teacher Varaprabha
Was completely able to uphold.
The Buddha, having spoken this Lotus
And caused the assembly to rejoice,
Soon after on that very day
Addressed the assembly of devas and humans:
The meaning of the genuine mark of all dharmas
Has already been expounded for you;
I now at midnight
Will enter into nirvāṇa.
Single-mindedly advance with vigor;
You must depart from negligence.
The Buddhas are exceedingly difficult to encounter,
Met once in hundreds of millions of kalpas.
All the sons of the World-Honored One,
Hearing that the Buddha was entering nirvāṇa,
Each harbored grief and distress:
Why is the Buddha's extinction so swift?
The Holy Lord, King of Dharma,
Comforted the immeasurable assembly:
When I pass into extinction,
Do not harbor sorrow or fear.
This Deva-kūṭa Bodhisattva,
Regarding the uncontaminated genuine mark,
Has already attained penetration in mind.
Next he will become a Buddha,
Named Śuddhakāya,
And will also liberate immeasurable assemblies.
The Buddha that night passed into extinction,
As fire dies out when fuel is exhausted.
They divided his relics
And erected immeasurable stūpas.
Bhikṣus and bhikṣuṇīs,
Their numbers like Ganges sands,
Doubled their vigor
To seek the unexcelled path.
This Dharma Teacher Varaprabha
Upheld the Buddha's Dharma treasury;
Throughout eighty minor kalpas,
He widely proclaimed the Lotus Sūtra.
All these eight princes,
Taught and transformed by Varaprabha,
Became firm in the unexcelled path
And were to see countless Buddhas.
Having made offerings to the Buddhas,
They followed and practiced the Great Path,
Successively attaining Buddhahood,
In turn receiving prophecies.
The last Deva among Devas
Was named Dīpaṃkara Buddha,
Guide of all sages,
Liberating immeasurable assemblies.
This Dharma Teacher Varaprabha
At that time had a disciple
Whose mind was constantly negligent,
Greedily attached to fame and gain,
Seeking fame and gain without satiety,
Frequently visiting noble families,
Abandoning what he had practiced and recited,
Neglecting and forgetting without penetration.
By this causal condition,
He was named Yaśaskāma.
He also performed many wholesome deeds,
Enabling him to see countless Buddhas,
Making offerings to the Buddhas,
Following and practicing the Great Path,
Fulfilling the Six Pāramitās,
And now beholds the Śākya Lion.
Afterward he will become a Buddha,
Named Maitreya,
Widely liberating wandering beings,
Their numbers having no measure.
After that Buddha passed into extinction,
The negligent one was you;
The Dharma Teacher Varaprabha
Is now none other than myself.
I saw the original auspicious light
Of Candrasūryapradīpa Buddha like this;
By this I know the present Buddha
Wishes to speak the Lotus Sūtra.
The present signs are like the original auspicious signs;
These are the expedient means of the Buddhas.
Now the Buddha radiates light
To help reveal the meaning of the genuine mark.
All people should now know:
Join your palms and single-mindedly wait;
The Buddha will rain down the Dharma rain
To satisfy those who seek the path.
Those who seek the three vehicles,
If they have doubts or regrets,
The Buddha will resolve and sever them,
Causing them to be exhausted without remainder.
