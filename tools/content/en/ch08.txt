At that time, Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇīputra, hearing from the Buddha this wisdom, expedient means, and expounding according to what is appropriate, and hearing the bestowal of prophecies of anuttarasamyaksambodhi upon the great disciples, hearing the matters of past causal conditions, and hearing of the Buddhas' great sovereign spiritual powers, attained what he had never experienced before, his heart pure, rejoicing, and leaping.
Immediately rising from his seat, he went before the Buddha, bowed with his head touching the Buddha's feet, withdrew to stand on one side, and gazed upon the venerable countenance without glancing away for an instant, thinking:
"The World-Honored One is extraordinarily unique; what he does is rare! Accordant with the various dispositions of the world, through expedient knowledge and vision he expounds the Dharma, extracting wandering beings from their greedy attachments in every place. We cannot proclaim in words the virtues of the Buddha; only the Buddha, the World-Honored One, is able to know our deep hearts' original vows."
Then the Buddha addressed the bhikṣus: "Do you see this Pūrṇamaitrāyaṇīputra? I have constantly praised him as foremost among expounders of the Dharma, and have constantly praised his various merits: diligently upholding and assisting in proclaiming my Dharma, able to instruct, benefit, and delight the fourfold assembly, fully explaining the correct Dharma of the Buddha, and greatly benefiting his fellow practitioners of Brahma conduct. Except for the Tathāgata, none can exhaust the eloquence of his discourse.
You must not think that Pūrṇa is able only to protect and proclaim my Dharma; under ninety billion past Buddhas, he also protected and proclaimed the correct Dharma of the Buddhas, and among those expounders of Dharma was also foremost.
Furthermore, regarding the Dharma of emptiness expounded by the Buddhas, he clearly understood and penetrated it, attaining the four unhindered knowledges. He was constantly able to expound the Dharma purely, carefully, and truly, free of doubts, fully endowed with the spiritual powers of a Bodhisattva. Throughout his lifespan, he constantly cultivated Brahma conduct; the people of those Buddha-eras all considered him a genuine Śrāvaka. Yet Pūrṇa, by this expedient means, benefited immeasurable hundreds of thousands of wandering beings, and transformed immeasurable incalculable people, causing them to stand in anuttarasamyaksambodhi. To purify Buddha lands, he constantly performed Buddha activities, teaching and transforming wandering beings.
Bhikṣus! Pūrṇa was also foremost among expounders of Dharma under the seven Buddhas of the past, is now foremost among expounders of Dharma under me, and will also be foremost among expounders of Dharma under all future Buddhas of the Auspicious Kalpa, protecting and proclaiming the Buddha-Dharma for them all. In the future, he will also protect and proclaim the Dharma of immeasurable, boundless Buddhas, teaching and benefiting immeasurable wandering beings, causing them to stand in anuttarasamyaksambodhi.
To purify Buddha lands, he will constantly exert diligence and vigor in teaching wandering beings, gradually perfecting the path of a Bodhisattva. After immeasurable incalculable kalpas have passed, in this land he will attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi, named Dharmaprabhāsa Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha, Vidyācaraṇasampanna, Sugata, Lokavid, Anuttara, Puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstā Devamanuṣyāṇām, Buddha, Bhagavān.
That Buddha will take great three-thousand-fold worldss equal to the sands of the Ganges as a single Buddha land, with the seven treasures as its ground, flat as the palm of a hand, without hills, mountains, ravines, or gullies. Towers and pavilions of the seven treasures will fill it; heavenly palaces will dwell nearby in open space, where humans and devas will interact and behold one another. There will be no evil destinies, and no women; all wandering beings will be born by transformation, without sexual lust. Attaining great spiritual powers, their bodies will emit light; flying freely, firm in resolve and mindfulness, vigorous and wise, all golden in color, adorned with the thirty-two marks.
The wandering beings of that land will constantly partake of two foods: the food of Dharma joy, and the food of dhyāna delight. There will be immeasurable, incalculable tens of billions of inconceivable number of Bodhisattvas who have attained great spiritual powers and the four unhindered knowledges, skillfully teaching classes of wandering beings. His Śrāvaka assembly will be beyond calculation to know, all fully endowed with the six spiritual superknowledges, the three knowledges, and the eight liberations.
The land of that Buddha will be adorned and perfected with such immeasurable merits. The kalpa will be named Ratnāvabhāsa, and the land named Suviśuddha. That Buddha's lifespan will be immeasurable, incalculable kalpas, and the Dharma will abide for an exceedingly long time. After the Buddha's extinction, seven-jeweled stūpas will fill the entire land."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Bhikṣus, listen attentively!
The path practiced by the Buddha-child,
Because he skillfully learns expedient means,
Cannot be conceived.
Knowing that multitudes delight in small dharmas,
And fear great wisdom,
Therefore all the Bodhisattvas
Act as Śrāvakas and Conditioned Buddhas,
Through countless expedient means,
Transforming classes of wandering beings.
Proclaiming themselves to be Śrāvakas,
Far removed from the Buddha path,
They liberate immeasurable multitudes,
Causing them all to attain accomplishment.
Though of small desire and indolent,
Gradually they cause them to become Buddhas.
Internally concealing Bodhisattva conduct,
Externally appearing as Śrāvakas,
Of few desires, weary of birth and death,
In reality they purify Buddha lands.
Displaying the three poisons to the multitude,
And manifesting marks of false views,
My disciples act like this,
Expediently liberating wandering beings.
If I were to speak fully
Of various manifesting transformations,
Wandering beings, hearing this,
Would harbor doubts in their hearts.
Now this Pūrṇa,
Under thousands of billions of past Buddhas,
Diligently cultivated the path he practiced,
Proclaiming and protecting the Buddhas' Dharma.
To seek unexcelled wisdom,
In the presence of the Buddhas,
He appeared foremost among disciples,
Learned and possessing wisdom.
What he spoke was fearless,
Able to cause the multitude to rejoice;
Never weary or fatigued,
He assisted in Buddha activities.
Already possessing great spiritual powers,
Endowed with the four unhindered knowledges,
Knowing whether faculties were sharp or dull,
He constantly expounded the pure Dharma.
Proclaiming such meanings,
He taught thousands of billions of multitudes,
Causing them to abide in the Great Vehicle Dharma,
While purifying his own Buddha land.
In the future he will also make offerings
To immeasurable, countless Buddhas,
Protecting and proclaiming the correct Dharma,
Also purifying his own Buddha land.
Constantly through various expedient means,
Speaking the Dharma without fear,
Liberating countless multitudes,
Accomplishing all-knowledge.
Making offerings to the Tathāgatas,
Protecting the treasury of the Dharma jewel,
Afterward he will become a Buddha,
Named Dharmaprabhāsa.
His land will be named Suviśuddha,
Composed of the seven treasures;
The kalpa will be named Ratnāvabhāsa.
The Bodhisattva assembly will be numerous,
Their numbers immeasurable billions,
All possessing great spiritual powers,
Endowed with majesty and virtue,
Filling that Buddha land.
Śrāvakas will also be countless,
With the three knowledges, eight liberations,
And four unhindered knowledges;
Such as these will form the Saṅgha.
The wandering beings of that land
Will have all severed sexual lust,
Purely born by transformation,
Adorning their bodies with marks.
Taking Dharma joy and dhyāna delight as food,
Having no thought of other food.
There will be no women,
Nor any evil destinies.
The bhikṣu Pūrṇa,
His merits completely fulfilled,
Will attain this pure land,
With many worthy and holy assemblies.
Such immeasurable matters as these,
I now have only briefly spoken.
At that time, the twelve hundred arhats whose minds had attained mastery thought: "We rejoice, having attained what we never had before. If the World-Honored One would bestow prophecies on each of us, as upon the other great disciples, would that not be joyful!"
The Buddha, knowing the thoughts in their minds, addressed Mahākāśyapa: "Upon these twelve hundred arhats, I will now successively bestow prophecies of anuttarasamyaksambodhi before them.
In this assembly, my great disciple, the bhikṣu Kauṇḍinya, will make offerings to sixty-two thousand billion Buddhas, and afterward will become a Buddha named Samantaprabhāsa Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha, Vidyācaraṇasampanna, Sugata, Lokavid, Anuttara, Puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstā Devamanuṣyāṇām, Buddha, Bhagavān.
His five hundred arhats: Uruvilvākāśyapa, Gayākāśyapa, Nadīkāśyapa, Kālodayin, Udayin, Aniruddha, Revata, Kapphiṇa, Bakkula, Cunda, Svāgata, and the others, will all attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi, all with the same name, Samantaprabhāsa."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
The bhikṣu Kauṇḍinya
Will behold immeasurable Buddhas;
After an incalculable kalpa has passed,
He will accomplish equal and right awakening.
Constantly emitting great light,
Endowed with all spiritual superknowledges,
His fame pervading the ten directions,
Revered by all,
Constantly expounding the unexcelled path,
Therefore named Samantaprabhāsa.
His land will be pure,
His Bodhisattvas all heroic,
Ascending wonderful towers,
Traveling to lands of the ten directions,
With unexcelled articles of offering,
Presenting them to the Buddhas.
Having made these offerings,
Their hearts filled with great joy,
In an instant returning to their land,
Possessing such spiritual powers.
That Buddha's lifespan will be sixty thousand kalpas;
The Correct Dharma will abide for double his life;
The Semblance Dharma will be double that again;
When the Dharma ends, devas and humans will grieve.
These five hundred bhikṣus
Will successively become Buddhas,
All with the same name, Samantaprabhāsa,
Conferring prophecies in succession:
"After my extinction,
Such-and-such will become a Buddha;
The world he transforms
Will be just as mine is today."
The purity and adornment of the lands,
The spiritual powers,
The Bodhisattva and Śrāvaka assemblies,
The Correct Dharma and Semblance Dharma,
The length of their lifespans and kalpas,
Will all be as spoken above.
Kāśyapa, you now know
These five hundred who have attained mastery;
The rest of the Śrāvaka assembly
Will also be like this.
For those not present in this assembly,
You should proclaim it to them.
At that time, the five hundred arhats, having received prophecies before the Buddha, rejoiced and leaped. Rising from their seats, they went before the Buddha, bowed with heads touching his feet, repented of their errors, and reproached themselves:
"World-Honored One! We constantly had this thought, considering that we had already attained ultimate extinction. Now we finally understand that we were like those without wisdom. For what reason? We ought to have attained the Tathāgata's wisdom, yet we were content with small wisdom.
World-Honored One! Suppose a man goes to the house of a close friend, gets drunk on wine, and lies down to sleep. At that time, his close friend has official business and must depart; he sews a priceless jewel inside his garment, gives it to him, and leaves. The man, lying drunk, is entirely unaware.
Having arisen, he travels to another country. For the sake of food and clothing, he exerts his strength in seeking them, enduring great hardship; if he obtains a little, he is satisfied.
Afterward, the close friend happens to encounter him, and says: 'Alas, my man! Why have you done this for the sake of food and clothing? In the past, wishing you to attain peace, joy, and the free indulgence of the five desires, in a certain year, month, and day, I sewed a priceless jewel inside your garment. It is still there now, yet you did not know it. Suffering and worrying in order to sustain yourself—this is great foolishness! You can now trade this jewel for whatever you need, and you will always have what you desire, lacking nothing.'
The Buddha is also like this. When he was a Bodhisattva, he taught and transformed us, causing us to generate the mind of all-knowledge. Yet we quickly neglected and forgot it, unaware and uncomprehending. Having attained the arhat path, we considered that we had attained extinction, finding it difficult to sustain our livelihood, and were content with little. Yet the vow for all-knowledge still remained without being lost.
Now the World-Honored One awakens us, speaking thus: 'Bhikṣus! What you attained is not ultimate extinction. Long ago I caused you to plant the Buddha's wholesome roots; through expedient means, I displayed the marks of nirvāṇa, yet you considered that you had truly attained extinction.'
World-Honored One! We now know that we are truly Bodhisattvas, receiving prophecies of anuttarasamyaksambodhi. By this causal condition, we rejoice greatly, having attained what we never had before."
Thereupon, Ājñātakauṇḍinya and the others, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Hearing the unexcelled,
Peaceful voice of bestowed prophecy,
We rejoice as never before,
Bowing to the Buddha of immeasurable wisdom.
Now before the World-Honored One,
We repent our errors and faults;
Regarding the immeasurable Buddha treasure,
We attained a meager portion of nirvāṇa,
And like foolish people lacking wisdom,
Considered ourselves satisfied.
Just like a poor man
Going to the house of a close friend,
Whose household was exceedingly rich,
Setting out a feast of delicacies;
With a priceless jewel,
Sewing it inside his inner garment,
Silently giving it and departing,
While the man slept unaware.
When this man had arisen,
He traveled to another country,
Seeking clothing and food to sustain himself,
His livelihood exceedingly hard,
Content with whatever little he gained,
Not wishing for anything better.
Unaware that inside his inner garment,
There was a priceless jewel.
The friend who gave the jewel,
Later seeing this poor man,
Severely rebuked him,
And showed him the sewn jewel.
The poor man, seeing this jewel,
Rejoiced greatly in his heart,
Rich with wealth and goods,
Freely indulging in the five desires.
We too are like this;
The World-Honored One throughout the long night,
Constantly pitied and taught us,
Causing us to plant the unexcelled vow.
Because we lacked wisdom,
Unaware and uncomprehending,
Attaining a meager portion of nirvāṇa,
We were content and sought nothing further.
Now the Buddha has awakened us,
Saying it is not genuine extinction;
Attaining the Buddha's unexcelled wisdom,
Only that is genuine extinction.
Now hearing from the Buddha
The matters of prophecy and adornment,
And receiving prophecies in turn,
Body and mind are filled with joy.
