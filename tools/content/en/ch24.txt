At that time, Śākyamuni Buddha radiated a light from the flesh-topknot of the mark of a great person, and radiated a light from the white hair between his eyebrows, universally illuminating the Buddha worlds of one million eighty thousand billion inconceivable numbers of Ganges sands in the eastern direction.
Past these numbers, there was a world named Virocanaraśmipratimaṇḍita. In that land was a Buddha named Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha, Vidyācaraṇasampanna, Sugata, Lokavid, Anuttara, Puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstā Devamanuṣyāṇām, Buddha, Bhagavān, reverently surrounded by an immeasurable, boundless assembly of Bodhisattvas for whom he expounded the Dharma. The white hair radiant light of Śākyamuni Buddha universally illuminated that land.
At that time, in the land Virocanaraśmipratimaṇḍita, there was a Bodhisattva named Gadgadasvara, who had long planted roots of virtue, making offerings to and attending upon immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of Buddhas, fully accomplishing profound wisdom. He had attained the Wonderful Banner Mark samādhi, the Lotus of Dharma samādhi, the Pure Virtue samādhi, the Constellation King's Sport samādhi, the Objectless samādhi, the Wisdom Seal samādhi, the Understanding All Wandering Beings' Languages samādhi, the Gathering All Virtues samādhi, the Purity samādhi, the Sport of Spiritual Superknowledges samādhi, the Torch of Wisdom samādhi, the King of Adornment samādhi, the Pure Light samādhi, the Pure Treasury samādhi, the Distinct samādhi, and the Sun-Rotation samādhi, attaining hundreds of thousands of millions of Ganges sands of great samādhis such as these.
When the light of Śākyamuni Buddha illuminated his body, he immediately addressed Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha: "World-Honored One! I should go to the Sahā world to bow down, draw near, and make offerings to Śākyamuni Buddha, and to see Mañjuśrī, Prince of the Dharma Bodhisattva, Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva, Pradānaśūra Bodhisattva, Nakṣatrarājasamkusumitābhijña Bodhisattva, Viśiṣṭacāritra Bodhisattva, Śubhavyūharāja Bodhisattva, and Bhaiṣajyasamudgata Bodhisattva."
Then Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha addressed Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva: "Do not look down upon that land and give rise to thoughts of its inferiority. Good son! That Sahā world is uneven with heights and depths; its earth and stone mountains are filled with filth and evil; the Buddha's body is small and low, and the Bodhisattva assemblies are also small in stature. Yet your body is forty-two thousand yojanas in height, and my body is six million eight hundred thousand yojanas; your form is foremost in beauty, with hundreds of thousands of merits, its radiance extraordinary. Therefore, when you go, do not look down upon that land, giving rise to thoughts of inferiority toward the Buddha, Bodhisattvas, or the realm."
Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva addressed that Buddha: "World-Honored One! That I now travel to the Sahā world is entirely through the Tathāgata's power, the Tathāgata's sporting in spiritual powers, and the Tathāgata's adornments of merit and wisdom."
Thereupon, Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva, without rising from his seat and without moving his body, entered samādhi. Through the power of samādhi, on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, not far from the Dharma seat, there appeared eighty-four thousand jewel lotuses, with stalks of Jāmbūnada gold, leaves of white silver, stamens of diamond, and calyxes of kimśuka jewels.
At that time, Mañjuśrī, Prince of the Dharma, seeing these lotus flowers, addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! For what causal condition has this auspicious sign first appeared? There are several tens of thousands of lotus flowers, with stalks of Jāmbūnada gold, leaves of white silver, stamens of diamond, and calyxes of kimśuka jewels."
Then Śākyamuni Buddha addressed Mañjuśrī: "This is Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva Mahāsattva, who wishes to come from the land of Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha, surrounded by eighty-four thousand Bodhisattvas, arriving in this Sahā world to make offerings, draw near, and bow to me, and also wishing to make offerings to and hear the Lotus Sūtra."
Mañjuśrī addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! What roots of virtue did this Bodhisattva plant, and what merits did he cultivate, to be able to possess such great spiritual powers? What samādhi does he practice? Pray speak the names of these samādhis for us; we too wish diligently to cultivate them; practicing this samādhi, we will be able to behold the size of this Bodhisattva's physical appearance, and his deportment in advancing and halting. We only pray that the World-Honored One will use spiritual power to cause that Bodhisattva to arrive so that we may see him."
Then Śākyamuni Buddha addressed Mañjuśrī: "Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata, who has long passed into extinction, will manifest his appearance for you all."
Then Prabhūtaratna Buddha addressed that Bodhisattva: "Good son! Come; Mañjuśrī, Prince of the Dharma, wishes to see your body."
Thereupon, Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva vanished from that land and departed together with eighty-four thousand Bodhisattvas. The lands they passed through shook in six ways, all raining seven-jeweled lotus flowers; hundreds of thousands of heavenly instruments sounded of themselves without being struck.
This Bodhisattva's eyes were like broad blue lotus petals; even if combining hundreds of thousands of moons, his facial countenance was more upright and handsome than this; his body was of pure golden color, adorned with immeasurable hundreds of thousands of merits, ablaze with majesty and virtue, radiant with light, endowed with all marks, firm as the body of Nārāyaṇa.
Entering a seven-jeweled tower, he ascended into open space to the height of seven tāla trees, reverently surrounded by the Bodhisattva assembly, arriving at Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa in this Sahā world. Having arrived, he stepped down from the seven-jeweled tower, took necklaces worth hundreds of thousands, proceeded to where Śākyamuni Buddha was, bowed with his head touching the Buddha's feet, presented the necklaces, and addressed the Buddha:
"World-Honored One! Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha inquires after the World-Honored One: Are you free of sickness and affliction, light and energetic in daily life, practicing in peace and joy? Are the four great elements in harmony? Are worldly activities endurable? Are wandering beings easy to liberate? Are they free from excessive greed, anger, delusion, envy, stinginess, and arrogance? Are they free from unfilial conduct toward parents, irreverence toward śramaṇas, false views, unwholesome minds, and unbridled five passions?
World-Honored One! Are wandering beings able to subdue the enemies of Mara? Does Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata, long passed into extinction within the seven-jeweled stūpa, come to hear the Dharma? He also inquires after Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata: Is he at peace, free of affliction, able to endure a long stay? World-Honored One! I now wish to behold the body of Prabhūtaratna Buddha; we only pray that the World-Honored One will show it to me, causing me to see him."
Then Śākyamuni Buddha said to Prabhūtaratna Buddha: "This Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva wishes to meet you."
Then Prabhūtaratna Buddha addressed Gadgadasvara: "Excellent, excellent! That you, for the sake of making offerings to Śākyamuni Buddha, hearing the Lotus Sūtra, and seeing Mañjuśrī and the others, have arrived here."
At that time, Padmaśrī Bodhisattva addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! What roots of virtue did this Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva plant, and what merits did he cultivate, to possess such spiritual powers?"
The Buddha addressed Padmaśrī Bodhisattva: "In the past, there was a Buddha named Meghadundubhisvararāja Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha. His land was named Sarvalokasaṃdarśana, and his kalpa was named Priyadarśana. For twelve thousand years, Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva, with a hundred thousand kinds of music, made offerings to Meghadundubhisvararāja Buddha, and presented eighty-four thousand alms bowls of the seven treasures.
Through the causal conditions of this karmic retribution, he was born into the land of Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha, possessing such spiritual powers.
Padmaśrī! What do you think: could Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva who at that time made music offerings and presented precious vessels to Meghadundubhisvararāja Buddha have been someone else? It was this Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva Mahāsattva himself.
Padmaśrī! This Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva had already made offerings to and attended upon immeasurable Buddhas, long planting roots of virtue, and had encountered hundreds of thousands of millions of inconceivable numbers of Buddhas equal to the sands of the Ganges.
Padmaśrī! You see only the body of Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva present here; yet this Bodhisattva manifests various bodies, in various places expounding this scripture for wandering beings:
Sometimes manifesting as a Brahmā King, sometimes manifesting as Śakra, sometimes manifesting as Īśvara, sometimes manifesting as Maheśvara, sometimes manifesting as a heavenly great general, sometimes manifesting as Vaiśravaṇa Heavenly King, sometimes manifesting as a wheel-turning sage king, sometimes manifesting as a petty king, sometimes manifesting as an elder, sometimes manifesting as a householder, sometimes manifesting as an official, sometimes manifesting as a brāhmaṇa, sometimes manifesting as a bhikṣu, bhikṣuṇī, upāsaka, or upāsikā;
Sometimes manifesting as the wife of an elder, householder, official, or brāhmaṇa, sometimes manifesting as a boy or girl; sometimes manifesting as a deva, nāga, yakṣa, gandharva, asura, garuḍa, kiṃnara, mahoraga, human, or non-human body, expounding this scripture.
All those in hells, hungry ghosts, animals, and difficult realms, he is able to rescue; down to transforming into a woman's body in royal inner palaces to expound this scripture.
Padmaśrī! This Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva is one who can rescue and protect wandering beings of the Sahā world. This Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva, through such various manifesting transformations of body, in this Sahā world expounds this scripture for wandering beings, without his spiritual superknowledges, transformations, or wisdom suffering any diminishment.
This Bodhisattva, with various wisdoms, clearly illuminates the Sahā world, causing all wandering beings each to attain what they should know; and in worlds of the ten directions equal to the sands of the Ganges, it is also like this.
If they should be liberated through the form of a Śrāvaka, he manifests the form of a Śrāvaka to expound the Dharma; if they should be liberated through the form of a Pratyekabuddha, he manifests the form of a Pratyekabuddha to expound the Dharma; if they should be liberated through the form of a Bodhisattva, he manifests the form of a Bodhisattva to expound the Dharma; if they should be liberated through the form of a Buddha, he immediately manifests the form of a Buddha to expound the Dharma. Various forms like these he manifests according to what is appropriate to liberate them, down to manifesting extinction for those who should be liberated through extinction.
Padmaśrī! Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva Mahāsattva has accomplished such power of great spiritual superknowledges and wisdom."
Then Padmaśrī Bodhisattva addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! This Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva has deeply planted roots of virtue. World-Honored One! In what samādhi does this Bodhisattva dwell, that he is able to manifest transformations in this manner to liberate wandering beings?"
The Buddha addressed Padmaśrī Bodhisattva: "Good son! That samādhi is named Manifesting All Physical Bodies. Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva, dwelling within this samādhi, is able in this manner to benefit immeasurable wandering beings."
When this Bodhisattva Gadgadasvara Chapter was expounded, the eighty-four thousand persons who had accompanied Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva all attained the samādhi of Manifesting All Physical Bodies; immeasurable Bodhisattvas of this Sahā world also attained this samādhi and dhāraṇīs.
Then Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva Mahāsattva, having made offerings to Śākyamuni Buddha and the stūpa of Prabhūtaratna Buddha, returned to his native land. The lands he passed through shook in six ways, raining jewel lotuses, and producing hundreds of thousands of millions of varieties of music.
Having arrived in his native land, surrounded by eighty-four thousand Bodhisattvas, he proceeded to where Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha was, and addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! I arrived in the Sahā world and benefited wandering beings; I beheld Śākyamuni Buddha, and beheld the stūpa of Prabhūtaratna Buddha, bowing and making offerings. I also saw Mañjuśrī, Prince of the Dharma Bodhisattva, as well as Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva, Viryabalavegadhārin Bodhisattva, and Pradānaśūra Bodhisattva, and caused these eighty-four thousand Bodhisattvas to attain the samādhi of Manifesting All Physical Bodies."
When this chapter on the Coming and Going of Bodhisattva Gadgadasvara was expounded, forty-two thousand sons of devas attained the forbearance of non-arising dharmas, and Padmaśrī Bodhisattva attained the Lotus of Dharma samādhi.
