At that time, the World-Honored One arose serenely from samādhi and addressed Śāriputra:
The wisdom of the Buddhas is extremely profound and immeasurable. The gate to their wisdom is difficult to understand and difficult to enter; all Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas cannot know it.
For what reason? The Buddhas have attended upon hundreds of thousands of millions of countless Buddhas, fully practicing the immeasurable path of the Buddhas, heroic, vigorous, their names universally renowned, accomplishing the profound, unprecedented Dharma. Expounded according to what is appropriate, its intent is difficult to understand.
Śāriputra! Since I attained Buddhahood, through various causal conditions and various parables, I have widely expounded spoken teachings with countless expedient means, guiding wandering beings to cause them to abandon all attachments. For what reason? The Tathāgata is fully endowed with expedient means and the pāramitā of knowledge and vision.
Śāriputra! The knowledge and vision of the Tathāgata is vast, deep, and far-reaching, immeasurable and unobstructed. His powers, fearlessnesses, dhyāna meditations, and samādhis of liberation deeply enter the boundless, accomplishing all unprecedented dharmas.
Śāriputra! The Tathāgata can distinguish variously and skillfully expound all dharmas, his speech gentle and pleasing the minds of the assembly. Śāriputra! In essence: the Buddha has fully accomplished immeasurable, boundless, unprecedented dharmas.
Stop, Śāriputra! There is no need to speak further. For what reason? What the Buddha has accomplished is the foremost, rare, difficult-to-understand Dharma. Only a Buddha and just a Buddha can exhaust the genuine mark of all dharmas: that is to say, the suchness of the marks of all dharmas, the suchness of their nature, the suchness of their substance, the suchness of their power, the suchness of their function, the suchness of their causes, the suchness of their conditions, the suchness of their effects, the suchness of their retributions, and the suchness of their ultimate equality from beginning to end.
At that time, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
The Hero of the World is immeasurable.
All devas and worldly humans,
All classes of wandering beings,
None can know the Buddha.
The Buddha's powers, fearlessnesses,
Liberations, and various samādhis,
As well as the Buddha's other dharmas,
None are able to fathom.
Originally following countless Buddhas,
He fully practiced all paths;
The profound, subtle, and sublime Dharma
Is difficult to see and difficult to understand.
Throughout immeasurable millions of kalpas,
Having practiced these various paths,
Attaining the fruit at the place of awakening,
I have fully known and seen it all.
Such great fruits and retributions,
The meanings of various natures and marks,
Only I and the Buddhas of the ten directions
Are able to know these matters.
This Dharma cannot be demonstrated;
The marks of words are tranquil and extinguished.
All other kinds of wandering beings,
None are able to attain understanding,
Except the assemblies of Bodhisattvas
Whose power of faith is firm.
The assemblies of disciples of the Buddhas,
Who have made offerings to the Buddhas,
With all outpourings exhausted,
Dwelling in this final body—
Such people as these,
Their strength is not equal to it.
Even if the world were full
Of those like Śāriputra,
Exhausting their thoughts to measure it together,
They could not fathom the Buddha's wisdom.
Even if filling the ten directions,
All were like Śāriputra,
And other disciples
Also filled the lands of the ten directions,
Exhausting their thoughts to measure it together,
They also could not know it.
Pratyekabuddhas of sharp wisdom,
Uncontaminated in their final bodies,
Even if filling the worlds of the ten directions,
Their numbers like bamboo groves,
If these joined together with one mind
Throughout millions of immeasurable kalpas,
Wishing to contemplate the Buddha's genuine wisdom,
None could know the slightest part.
Newly resolved Bodhisattvas,
Having made offerings to countless Buddhas,
Penetrating all meanings and principles,
And able to skillfully expound the Dharma,
Like rice, hemp, bamboo, and reeds,
Filling the lands of the ten directions,
With one mind and subtle wisdom
Throughout kalpas like Ganges sands,
All reflecting and calculating together,
Could not know the Buddha's wisdom.
Irreversible Bodhisattvas,
Their numbers like Ganges sands,
With one mind reflecting and seeking together,
Also could not know it.
Furthermore, I address Śāriputra:
This uncontaminated, inconceivable,
Profound, subtle, and sublime Dharma,
I have now fully attained;
Only I know its marks,
And the Buddhas of the ten directions are also thus.
Śāriputra, you must know:
The words of the Buddhas do not differ;
Toward the Dharma expounded by the Buddha,
You must generate great power of faith.
The Dharma of the World-Honored One, after a long time,
Must at last speak the genuine reality.
I address the assemblies of Śrāvakas
And those seeking the Pratyekabuddha vehicle:
I have caused you to be freed from the bonds of suffering
And to attain nirvāṇa.
The Buddha, by the power of expedient means,
Reveals the teachings of the three vehicles;
Wandering beings are attached at every point,
So he guides them to cause them to emerge.
At that time, in the great assembly, there were Śrāvakas, outpourings-exhausted arhats, Ājñātakauṇḍinya and others, twelve hundred persons, as well as bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, and upāsikās who had resolved upon the minds of Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas. Each had this thought: Now, for what reason does the World-Honored One so earnestly praise expedient means and make this statement: "The Dharma attained by the Buddha is profound and difficult to understand; what is spoken has an intent difficult to know, beyond the reach of all Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas"?
The Buddha has spoken of the single meaning of liberation, and we too have attained this Dharma, reaching nirvāṇa. Yet now we do not know where this principle leads.
At that time, Śāriputra, knowing the doubt in the hearts of the fourfold assembly and having not yet understood it himself, addressed the Buddha: World-Honored One! For what cause and condition do you so earnestly praise the foremost expedient means, profound, subtle, and difficult to understand Dharma of the Buddhas? From the past until now, I have never heard such a teaching from the Buddha. Now the fourfold assembly all harbor doubts. We only pray that the World-Honored One will expound this matter. For what reason does the World-Honored One earnestly praise this profound, subtle, and difficult to understand Dharma?
Thereupon, Śāriputra, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Sun of Wisdom, Great Holy Honored One,
Only after a long time do you expound this Dharma;
You yourself state that you have attained such
Powers, fearlessnesses, and samādhis,
Dhyāna meditations, liberations, and the like,
Inconceivable dharmas.
Regarding the Dharma attained at the place of awakening,
None are able to raise questions;
My intent is difficult to fathom,
And none are able to question it.
Without being questioned, you speak of your own accord,
Praising the path you have walked,
Wisdom exceedingly subtle and sublime,
Attained by all the Buddhas.
All uncontaminated arhats
And those seeking nirvāṇa
Have now fallen into a net of doubt:
Why does the Buddha speak this?
Those who seek Conditioned Awakening,
Bhikṣus and bhikṣuṇīs,
Devas, nāgas, ghosts, spirits,
And gandharvas,
Look at one another harboring hesitation,
Gazing up at the Honored Among Two-Legged Beings.
What is the meaning of this matter?
We pray the Buddha will explain it for us.
Among the assembly of Śrāvakas,
The Buddha declared me foremost;
Now I, regarding my own wisdom,
Am doubtful and unable to understand:
Is this the ultimate Dharma?
Or is it the path to be practiced?
The children born from the Buddha's mouth,
With palms joined, gaze up and wait;
We pray you will emit the subtle, sublime sound
And in due time speak the reality as it is.
Devas, nāgas, and spirits,
Their numbers like Ganges sands,
The Bodhisattvas seeking Buddhahood,
Numbering eighty thousand,
As well as from tens of billions of lands
Wheel-turning sage kings have arrived,
With joined palms and reverent minds,
Wishing to hear the complete path.
Then the Buddha addressed Śāriputra: Stop, stop! There is no need to speak further. If I speak of this matter, all the world with its devas and humans will be startled and suspicious.
Śāriputra addressed the Buddha again: World-Honored One, we only pray that you will speak it! We only pray that you will speak it! For what reason? In this assembly, there are countless hundreds of thousands of millions of incalculable wandering beings who have seen past Buddhas; their faculties are sharp, their wisdom clear. Hearing what the Buddha expounds, they will be able to revere and believe.
Thereupon, Śāriputra, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Dharma King, Unexcelled Honored One,
Only speak it, pray do not be anxious.
In this assembly are immeasurable multitudes
Who are able to revere and believe.
The Buddha again stopped Śāriputra: If I speak of this matter, all the world with its devas, humans, and asuras will be startled and suspicious, and bhikṣus of arrogant pride will fall into a great pit.
Then the World-Honored One restated it in gāthās:
Stop, stop, there is no need to speak;
My Dharma is subtle and difficult to conceive.
Those of arrogant pride,
Hearing it, will certainly not revere or believe.
Then Śāriputra addressed the Buddha again: World-Honored One! We only pray that you will speak it! We only pray that you will speak it! Now in this assembly, there are hundreds of thousands of millions like us who, life after life, have received transformation from the Buddha. Such persons as these will certainly be able to revere and believe, resting in peace through the long night, receiving vast benefits.
Thereupon, Śāriputra, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Unexcelled Honored Among Two-Legged Beings,
Pray speak the foremost Dharma;
I am the eldest child of the Buddha,
Pray deign to distinguish and expound it.
The immeasurable multitudes in this assembly
Can revere and believe this Dharma;
The Buddha has already, life after life,
Taught and transformed such as these.
All with one mind and joined palms
Wish to hear and receive the Buddha's words.
We twelve hundred,
And others seeking Buddhahood,
Pray for the sake of this assembly,
Deign to distinguish and expound it.
These, hearing this Dharma,
Will then give rise to great joy.
Then the World-Honored One addressed Śāriputra: You have already earnestly requested three times; how can I not speak? Now listen attentively and contemplate it well; I will distinguish and explain it for you.
When this was said, there were five thousand bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, and upāsikās in the assembly who immediately rose from their seats, bowed to the Buddha, and withdrew. For what reason? The roots of transgression of this group were deep and heavy, and their arrogant pride was great; not having attained, they claimed to have attained; not having realized, they claimed to have realized. Having such faults, they did not remain. The World-Honored One remained silent and did not restrain them.
Then the Buddha addressed Śāriputra: My assembly is now free of branches and leaves; only pure truth remains. Śāriputra! It is good that such persons of arrogant pride have withdrawn. Listen well now; I will speak it for you.
Śāriputra said: Yes, World-Honored One! We joyfully wish to hear it.
The Buddha addressed Śāriputra: Such a wonderful Dharma as this is spoken by the Buddhas, the Tathāgatas, only at certain times, just like the uḍumbara flower appears but once in a long age. Śāriputra! You must believe what the Buddha expounds; his words are not false.
Śāriputra! The Buddhas expound the Dharma according to what is appropriate; its intent is difficult to understand. For what reason? I, by countless expedient means, various causal conditions, parables, and words, expound all dharmas. This Dharma cannot be understood through thought and discrimination; only Buddhas can know it.
For what reason? The Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, appear in the world only for the sake of one great causal condition.
Śāriputra! What is meant by: the Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, appear in the world only for the sake of one great causal condition?
The Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, wishing to cause wandering beings to open the Buddha's knowledge and vision so that they may attain purity, appear in the world.
Wishing to reveal the Buddha's knowledge and vision to wandering beings, they appear in the world. Wishing to cause wandering beings to awaken to the Buddha's knowledge and vision, they appear in the world. Wishing to cause wandering beings to enter the path of the Buddha's knowledge and vision, they appear in the world.
Śāriputra! This is the one great causal condition for which the Buddhas appear in the world.
The Buddha addressed Śāriputra: The Buddhas, the Tathāgatas, teach and transform only Bodhisattvas. All their actions are constantly for this one thing: solely with the Buddha's knowledge and vision do they reveal and awaken wandering beings.
Śāriputra! The Tathāgata, solely by the One Buddha Vehicle, expounds the Dharma for wandering beings; there are no other vehicles, neither two nor three. Śāriputra! The Dharma of all Buddhas of the ten directions is also like this.
Śāriputra! The Buddhas of the past, with immeasurable, countless expedient means, various causal conditions, parables, and words, expounded all dharmas for wandering beings; these dharmas were all for the sake of the One Buddha Vehicle. These wandering beings, hearing the Dharma from the Buddhas, ultimately all attain all-knowledge.
Śāriputra! The Buddhas of the future who will appear in the world will also, with immeasurable, countless expedient means, various causal conditions, parables, and words, expound all dharmas for wandering beings; these dharmas are all for the sake of the One Buddha Vehicle. These wandering beings, hearing the Dharma from the Buddhas, will ultimately all attain all-knowledge.
Śāriputra! The present Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, in immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of Buddha lands of the ten directions, extensively benefit and bring peace and joy to wandering beings; these Buddhas also, with immeasurable, countless expedient means, various causal conditions, parables, and words, expound all dharmas for wandering beings; these dharmas are all for the sake of the One Buddha Vehicle. These wandering beings, hearing the Dharma from the Buddhas, ultimately all attain all-knowledge.
Śāriputra! These Buddhas only teach and transform Bodhisattvas, wishing to reveal the Buddha's knowledge and vision to wandering beings, wishing to awaken wandering beings to the Buddha's knowledge and vision, wishing to cause wandering beings to enter the Buddha's knowledge and vision.
Śāriputra! I now am also like this. Knowing that wandering beings have various desires deeply attached in their hearts, according to their original natures, by various causal conditions, parables, words, and the power of expedient means, I expound the Dharma for them. Śāriputra! All this is done in order to attain the One Buddha Vehicle and all-knowledge.
Śāriputra! In the worlds of the ten directions, there are not even two vehicles, much less three.
Śāriputra! The Buddhas appear in the evil world of the five turbidities: namely, kalpa turbidity, affliction turbidity, wandering being turbidity, view turbidity, and life turbidity. Thus, Śāriputra, during the chaotic time of kalpa turbidity, the defilements of wandering beings are heavy; stingy, greedy, and envious, they accomplish various unwholesome roots. Therefore, the Buddhas, by the power of expedient means, within the One Buddha Vehicle, distinguish and speak of three.
Śāriputra! If my disciples claim themselves to be arhats or pratyekabuddhas, yet do not hear and do not know that the Buddhas, the Tathāgatas, solely teach and transform Bodhisattvas, they are not disciples of the Buddha, not arhats, and not pratyekabuddhas.
Furthermore, Śāriputra! These bhikṣus and bhikṣuṇīs who claim they have already attained arhatship, that this is their final body and ultimate nirvāṇa, and thus no longer aspire to seek anuttarasamyaksambodhi—you must know that these people are all of arrogant pride.
For what reason? If there is a bhikṣu who has truly attained arhatship, if he does not believe this Dharma, there is no such possibility—except after the Buddha's extinction, when no Buddha is present. For what reason? After the Buddha's extinction, those who uphold, read, recite, and understand the meaning of such sūtras as this are difficult to find. If they encounter another Buddha, they will then attain clear resolution in this Dharma.
Śāriputra! You all should single-mindedly believe, understand, receive, and uphold the Buddha's words. The words of the Buddhas, the Tathāgatas, are without falsehood; there is no other vehicle, only the One Buddha Vehicle.
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Bhikṣus and bhikṣuṇīs
Harboring arrogant pride,
Upāsakas with self-grasping pride,
Upāsikās lacking faith—
Such members of the fourfold assembly,
Five thousand in number,
Do not see their own errors,
Having flaws in their morality,
Guarding their own defects.
These of small wisdom have already left;
The chaff among the assembly
Departed through the Buddha's majesty and virtue.
These persons have meager merit;
They cannot receive this Dharma.
This assembly has no branches and leaves,
Possessing only pure reality.
Śāriputra, listen well!
The Dharma attained by the Buddhas,
By immeasurable power of expedient means,
Is expounded for wandering beings.
The thoughts in the minds of wandering beings,
The various paths they practice,
Their diverse desires and natures,
Their wholesome and unwholesome karma of past lives—
The Buddha, fully knowing all these,
Uses various conditions, parables,
Words, and the power of expedient means,
To cause them all to rejoice.
He expounds sūtras,
Gāthās, and itivṛttakas,
Jātakas, and adbhutadharmas,
He also expounds nidānas,
Avadānas, and geyas,
As well as upadeśa scriptures.
Those of dull faculties who delight in small dharmas,
Greedily attached to birth and death,
Under immeasurable Buddhas
Do not practice the deep, subtle path;
Distressed by many sufferings,
For their sake he speaks of nirvāṇa.
I devised this expedient means
To cause them to enter the Buddha's wisdom;
I never said to you
That you will attain the Buddha path.
The reason I did not say it before
Was that the time to speak had not yet arrived;
Now is precisely the time
To definitively speak the Great Vehicle.
These nine divisions of Dharma of mine,
Expounded in accord with wandering beings,
Have entering the Great Vehicle as their root;
Therefore this sūtra is expounded.
There are Buddha-children with pure minds,
Gentle, and of sharp faculties,
Who under immeasurable Buddhas
Have practiced the deep, subtle path.
For these Buddha-children,
I expound this Great Vehicle sūtra;
I predict that such persons as these
Will in future lives attain the Buddha path,
Because with deep minds they are mindful of the Buddha
And cultivate pure morality.
These, hearing that they will attain Buddhahood,
Will have great joy pervade their bodies;
The Buddha, knowing their mental actions,
Therefore expounds the Great Vehicle for them.
Whether Śrāvakas or Bodhisattvas,
Hearing the Dharma I expound,
Even down to a single gāthā,
All will attain Buddhahood without doubt.
Within the Buddha lands of the ten directions,
There is only the Dharma of the One Vehicle,
There are neither two nor three,
Except for the expedient words of the Buddhas.
Merely using provisional names
To guide wandering beings,
Speaking of the Buddha's wisdom,
The Buddhas appear in the world.
Only this one matter is real;
The other two are not genuine;
Never by the Small Vehicle
Does he liberate wandering beings.
The Buddha himself dwells in the Great Vehicle;
According to the Dharma he attained,
Adorned with the power of meditation and wisdom,
By this he liberates wandering beings.
Having realized the unexcelled path himself,
The equal Dharma of the Great Vehicle,
If he transformed through the Small Vehicle,
Even down to a single person,
I would fall into stinginess and greed;
This matter cannot be.
If a person faithfully takes refuge in the Buddha,
The Tathāgata does not deceive them,
Nor has he thoughts of greed or jealousy;
He severs evil from among all dharmas.
Therefore the Buddha in the ten directions
Is uniquely free of fear.
Adorning my body with marks,
My radiant light illuminates the world;
Revered by immeasurable multitudes,
I speak for them the seal of the genuine mark.
Śāriputra, you must know:
I originally made a vow,
Wishing to cause all multitudes
To be equal to me without difference.
As I originally vowed in the past,
Now it has already been fulfilled;
Transforming all wandering beings,
I cause them all to enter the Buddha path.
If I encounter wandering beings,
Teaching them entirely with the Buddha path,
Those without wisdom, confused and errant,
Deluded, will not receive the teaching.
I know these wandering beings
Have never cultivated roots of virtue,
Firmly attached to the five desires,
Giving rise to afflictions through foolish attachment.
By causal conditions of various desires,
They fall into the three evil destinies,
Transmigrating within the six realms,
Fully undergoing painful torments,
Taking subtle shapes within the womb,
Increasing life after life.
Persons of meager virtue and little merit,
Oppressed by many sufferings,
Enter the dense forest of false views,
Such as existence or non-existence.
Relying on these various views,
Complete in sixty-two varieties,
Deeply attached to false dharmas,
Holding firmly and unable to let go,
Haughty with self-grasping pride,
Flattering, crooked, with ungenuine minds,
Through thousands of millions of kalpas,
They do not hear the name of the Buddha,
Nor do they hear the correct Dharma;
Such persons as these are difficult to liberate.
Therefore, Śāriputra!
I establish expedient means for them,
Speaking of the path that ends suffering,
Showing them nirvāṇa.
Although I speak of nirvāṇa,
This is also not genuine extinction;
All dharmas from the very beginning
Are constantly of the mark of tranquil extinction.
When Buddha-children have practiced the path,
In future lives they will become Buddhas;
I possess the power of expedient means
To open and reveal the Dharma of the three vehicles.
All the World-Honored Ones
All speak the path of the One Vehicle.
Now this great assembly
Should completely eliminate doubts;
The words of the Buddhas do not differ:
There is only the One, no second vehicle.
Throughout countless kalpas of the past,
Immeasurable Buddhas who passed into extinction,
Hundreds of thousands of millions of kinds,
Their numbers beyond calculation—
Such World-Honored Ones as these,
With various conditions and parables,
And countless powers of expedient means,
Expounded the marks of all dharmas.
These World-Honored Ones
All spoke the Dharma of the One Vehicle,
Transforming immeasurable wandering beings
To cause them to enter the Buddha path.
Furthermore, the Great Holy Lords,
Knowing what all in the world,
Devas, humans, and groups of living beings,
Desire deep within their hearts,
Further used distinct expedient means
To help illuminate the primary truth.
If there were classes of wandering beings
Who met past Buddhas,
If they heard the Dharma and practiced giving,
Or upheld precepts, patience,
Vigor, dhyāna, and wisdom,
Variously cultivating merit and wisdom—
Such persons as these
Have all attained the Buddha path.
After the Buddhas passed into extinction,
If a person had a wholesome, gentle mind,
Such wandering beings as these
Have all attained the Buddha path.
After the Buddhas passed into extinction,
Those who made offerings to relics,
Erecting thousands of millions of stūpas,
Of gold, silver, and crystal,
Mother-of-pearl and agate,
Rose quartz, lapis lazuli, and pearls,
Purely and widely adorned,
Decorating these stūpas;
Or those who erected stone shrines,
Of sandalwood and aloeswood,
Deodar and other timber,
Bricks, tiles, clay, and earth;
Or if in an open wilderness,
They heaped earth to make a Buddha shrine,
Even down to children at play
Gathering sand to make a Buddha stūpa—
Such persons as these
Have all attained the Buddha path.
If a person, for the Buddha's sake,
Erected various images,
Carving and sculpting them into various forms,
They have all attained the Buddha path.
Or if made of the seven treasures,
Brass, red and white copper,
Pewter, lead, and tin,
Iron, wood, and clay,
Or with lacquered cloth,
Adorning and making Buddha statues—
Such persons as these
Have all attained the Buddha path.
Those who painted Buddha images,
Adorned with the marks of a hundred merits,
Whether painted themselves or having others do so,
Have all attained the Buddha path.
Even down to children at play,
With blades of grass, wood, or brushes,
Or with their own fingernails,
Drawing Buddha images—
Such persons as these,
Gradually accumulating merits,
Complete with the great compassionate mind,
Have all attained the Buddha path.
Merely transforming the Bodhisattvas,
They liberate immeasurable multitudes.
If a person, at stūpas and shrines,
Before precious images or painted images,
With flowers, incense, banners, and canopies,
Reverently makes offerings;
Or causes people to make music,
Striking drums, blowing horns and conches,
Flutes, pipes, harps, lutes,
Pīpās, cymbals, and gongs,
Such wondrous sounds as these,
All offered up as worship;
Or with a joyful mind,
Sings hymns in praise of the Buddha's virtues,
Even down to a single faint sound,
They have all attained the Buddha path.
If a person with distracted mind,
Even with a single flower,
Makes an offering to a painted image,
They will gradually see countless Buddhas.
Or if a person bows down in homage,
Or merely joins palms,
Even raising a single hand,
Or slightly bowing the head,
By this offering to the image,
They will gradually see immeasurable Buddhas.
Themselves attaining the unexcelled path,
They will widely liberate countless multitudes,
Entering nirvāṇa without remainder,
As fire dies out when fuel is exhausted.
If a person with distracted mind
Enters into a stūpa or shrine,
And utters but once: "Homage to the Buddha!"
They have all attained the Buddha path.
Under the past Buddhas,
Whether while they were in the world or after their extinction,
If there were those who heard this Dharma,
They have all attained the Buddha path.
The future World-Honored Ones,
Their numbers having no measure,
These Tathāgatas
Will also expound the Dharma through expedient means.
All the Tathāgatas,
With immeasurable expedient means,
Liberate wandering beings,
Causing them to enter the Buddha's uncontaminated wisdom.
If there are those who hear the Dharma,
Not one will fail to attain Buddhahood.
The original vow of the Buddhas,
The Buddha path that I practice,
I wish universally to cause wandering beings
Also to attain this same path.
Although the Buddhas of future ages
Will speak hundreds of thousands of millions
Of countless Dharma gates,
In reality they are for the One Vehicle.
The Buddhas, Honored Among Two-Legged Beings,
Know that dharmas are ever without inherent nature;
The Buddha-seeds arise from conditions;
Therefore they speak the One Vehicle.
These dharmas abide in their Dharma-positions;
The worldly marks are ever-abiding.
Having known this at the place of awakening,
The Guides expound it through expedient means.
Revered by devas and humans,
The present Buddhas of the ten directions,
Their numbers like Ganges sands,
Appearing within the world
To bring peace and safety to wandering beings,
Also speak such a Dharma as this.
Knowing the foremost tranquil extinction,
Through the power of expedient means,
Although they display various paths,
In reality they are for the Buddha Vehicle.
Knowing the conduct of wandering beings,
The thoughts deep within their hearts,
The karma practiced in the past,
Their desires, natures, and powers of vigor,
As well as whether faculties are sharp or dull,
By various causal conditions,
Parables, and words,
They speak according to what is appropriate through expedient means.
Now I am also like this;
To bring peace and safety to wandering beings,
Through various Dharma gates,
I proclaim the Buddha path.
By the power of my wisdom,
Knowing the natures and desires of wandering beings,
I expound all dharmas through expedient means,
Causing them all to attain joy.
Śāriputra, you must know!
Observing with the Buddha eye,
I see wandering beings in the six destinies,
Impoverished, lacking merit and wisdom,
Entering the perilous path of birth and death,
Their sufferings continuing without break,
Deeply attached to the five desires,
Like a yak cherishing its tail,
Blinding themselves with greed and love,
Blind and dark, seeing nothing.
They do not seek the Buddha of great power,
Nor the Dharma that severs suffering,
Entering deeply into false views,
Wishing to shed suffering through suffering;
For the sake of these wandering beings,
I give rise to a great compassionate mind.
When I first sat at the place of awakening,
Gazing at the tree and walking about,
Throughout three times seven days,
I contemplated such matters as this:
The wisdom I have attained
Is subtle, sublime, and foremost.
Wandering beings have dull faculties,
Attached to pleasure, blinded by delusion;
Classes of beings such as these,
How could they be liberated?
At that time all the Brahmā Kings,
As well as Śakra, Lord of Devas,
The Four Heavenly Kings guarding the world,
And the Great Sovereign Deva Maheśvara,
Together with the other assemblies of devas,
Attendants numbering hundreds of thousands of millions,
Reverently joined palms and bowed,
Requesting me to turn the wheel of Dharma.
I then thought to myself:
If I merely praise the Buddha Vehicle,
Wandering beings submerged in suffering
Will not be able to believe this Dharma;
Because they contravene the Dharma and do not believe,
They will fall into the three evil destinies.
Better that I not speak the Dharma
And quickly enter into nirvāṇa.
Then I recalled the past Buddhas,
The power of expedient means they practiced;
The path I have now attained
Should also be expounded as three vehicles.
When I had this thought,
The Buddhas of the ten directions all appeared,
Comforting me with Brahma-sounds:
Excellent, Śākyamuni!
Foremost Guide,
Having attained this unexcelled Dharma,
Following all the Buddhas,
You will use the power of expedient means.
We too have all attained
The most wondrous, foremost Dharma.
For the sake of classes of wandering beings,
We distinguish and speak of three vehicles.
Those of small wisdom delight in small dharmas,
Not believing they themselves will become Buddhas;
Therefore through expedient means,
We distinguish and speak of various fruits.
Although they speak of three vehicles,
It is only for the sake of teaching Bodhisattvas.
Śāriputra, you must know!
When I heard the holy lions'
Deep, pure, subtle, and sublime sounds,
Joyfully I proclaimed: Homage to the Buddhas!
Again I had this thought:
I have appeared in a turbid and evil world;
As the Buddhas have spoken,
I too will follow and act accordingly.
Having contemplated this matter,
I immediately set out for Vārāṇasī.
The marks of tranquil extinction of all dharmas
Cannot be proclaimed in words;
Through the power of expedient means,
I expounded it for the five bhikṣus.
This is named the turning of the wheel of Dharma.
Then there were the sounds of nirvāṇa,
As well as of arhat,
And distinct names of Dharma and Saṅgha.
From distant kalpas onward,
Praising and showing the Dharma of nirvāṇa,
Birth and death's suffering forever exhausted—
I have constantly spoken thus.
Śāriputra, you must know!
I see the Buddha-children
Aspiring to seek the Buddha path,
Immeasurable thousands of millions,
All with reverent minds,
All coming to where the Buddha dwells,
Having heard from the Buddhas
The Dharma expounded through expedient means.
I immediately had this thought:
The reason the Tathāgata appears
Is to speak the Buddha's wisdom;
Now is precisely the proper time.
Śāriputra, you must know!
People of dull faculties and small wisdom,
Attached to marks, proud and arrogant,
Cannot believe this Dharma.
Now I, joyful and fearless,
Amidst the Bodhisattvas,
Directly abandon expedient means,
Speaking only the unexcelled path.
When Bodhisattvas hear this Dharma,
Their nets of doubts are all eliminated;
The twelve hundred arhats
Will also all become Buddhas.
As is the ritual manner of speaking the Dharma
Of the Buddhas of the three periods of time,
I now am also like this,
Speaking the Dharma free of discrimination.
The Buddhas' appearing in the world
Is distant and difficult to encounter;
Even when appearing in the world,
Speaking this Dharma is also difficult.
Through immeasurable, countless kalpas,
Hearing this Dharma is also difficult;
Those persons who can listen to this Dharma,
Such persons are also rare.
Just like the uḍumbara flower,
Loved and delighted in by all,
A rarity to devas and humans,
Appearing once in a great while.
If one hears the Dharma, rejoices and praises it,
Even uttering but a single word,
One has already made offerings
To all Buddhas of the three periods of time;
Such a person is exceedingly rare,
Surpassing even the uḍumbara flower.
Do not harbor any doubts;
I am the King of all dharmas.
I universally announce to the great assembly:
Solely by the path of the One Vehicle
Do I teach and transform Bodhisattvas;
There are no Śrāvaka disciples.
You, Śāriputra,
Both Śrāvakas and Bodhisattvas,
Should know that this wonderful Dharma
Is the secret essential of all Buddhas.
In the evil world of the five turbidities,
Beings merely delight in and are attached to desires;
Such wandering beings as these
Ultimately do not seek the Buddha path.
Evil people in future ages,
Hearing the Buddha speak the One Vehicle,
Deluded, will not believe or receive it;
Contravening the Dharma, they will fall into evil destinies.
For those with shame, remorse, and purity,
Who aspire to seek the Buddha path,
For such as these,
You should widely praise the path of the One Vehicle.
Śāriputra, you must know!
The Dharma of the Buddhas is like this:
With tens of billions of expedient means,
Expounding the Dharma according to what is appropriate;
Those who do not practice and study it
Cannot understand this.
Since you all already know
That the Buddhas, the Teachers of the World,
Act through appropriate expedient means,
You will have no further doubts;
With great joy arising in your hearts,
You know that you will certainly become Buddhas.
