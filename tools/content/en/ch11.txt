At that time, before the Buddha, there appeared a seven-jeweled stūpa, five hundred yojanas in height, two hundred and fifty yojanas in length and breadth, springing up from the earth and suspended in open space, adorned and decorated with various precious objects.
It had five thousand railings, thousands of millions of niches and chambers, adorned with countless banners and streamers, draped with jewel garlands, with tens of billions of jewel bells suspended above it. On all four sides poured forth the fragrance of tamālapatra-candana, pervading all worlds. Its banners and canopies were made of the seven treasures: gold, silver, lapis lazuli, mother-of-pearl, agate, pearls, and carnelian, rising as high as the palaces of the Four Heavenly Kings.
The devas of Trāyastriṃśa rained down heavenly māndārava flowers to make offerings to the jewel stūpa. The other devas, Naga kings, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, and non-humans, tens of billions of multitudes, with all flowers, incense, necklaces, banners, canopies, and music, made offerings to the jewel stūpa, revering, honoring, and praising it.
At that time, from within the jewel stūpa came forth a great voice, praising: "Excellent, excellent! Śākyamuni World-Honored One! Able, through equal great wisdom, to expound for the great assembly the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma, a teaching for Bodhisattvas and guarded in the mindfulness of the Buddhas. Thus it is, thus it is! Śākyamuni World-Honored One! All that you have expounded is the genuine reality."
Then the fourfold assembly, seeing the great jewel stūpa suspended in open space and hearing the voice coming forth from within the stūpa, all attained Dharma joy, wondering at what they had never seen before. Rising from their seats, reverently joining palms, they withdrew to stand on one side.
At that time, there was a Bodhisattva Mahāsattva named Mahāpratibhāna, who, knowing the doubts in the minds of all devas, humans, and asuras of the world, addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! By what causal condition has this jewel stūpa sprung up from the earth, and why does this voice issue from within it?"
Then the Buddha addressed Mahāpratibhāna Bodhisattva: "Within this jewel stūpa is the complete body of the Tathāgata. Long ago in the past, in the eastern direction across immeasurable thousands of billions of incalculable worlds, there was a land named Ratnaviśuddha. In that land was a Buddha named Prabhūtaratna.
When that Buddha was practicing the Bodhisattva path, he made a great vow: 'If, after I become a Buddha and pass into extinction, in lands of the ten directions there is a place where the Lotus Sūtra is expounded, my stūpa shrine, for the sake of hearing this scripture, will spring forth before it, bearing witness and praising: "Excellent!"'
After that Buddha accomplished the path, approaching extinction, amidst the great assembly of devas and humans he addressed the bhikṣus: 'After my extinction, those who wish to make offerings to my complete body should erect a great stūpa.' That Buddha, through the power of his spiritual vows, throughout the worlds of the ten directions, wherever the Lotus Sūtra is expounded, has his jewel stūpa spring forth before it, with his complete body within the stūpa, praising: 'Excellent, excellent!'
Mahāpratibhāna! Now the stūpa of Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata, hearing the Lotus Sūtra expounded, has sprung up from the earth, praising: 'Excellent, excellent!'"
Then Mahāpratibhāna Bodhisattva, through the spiritual power of the Tathāgata, addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! We wish to behold this Buddha's body."
The Buddha addressed Mahāpratibhāna Bodhisattva Mahāsattva: "This Prabhūtaratna Buddha has a deep and heavy vow: 'When my jewel stūpa, for the sake of hearing the Lotus Sūtra, emerges before the Buddhas, if anyone wishes to display my body to the fourfold assembly, all the emanation Buddhas of that Buddha expounding the Dharma in worlds of the ten directions must gather together in one place; only then will my body appear.'
Mahāpratibhāna! All the emanation bodies of myself expounding the Dharma in worlds of the ten directions should now be assembled."
Mahāpratibhāna addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! We also wish to behold the emanation Buddhas of the World-Honored One, to bow down and make offerings."
Then the Buddha emitted a light from the white curl between his eyebrows, immediately revealing the Buddhas of lands equal to five hundred million billion inconceivable numbers of Ganges sands in the eastern direction. All those lands had crystal as their ground, adorned with jewel trees and precious garments, filled with countless thousands of billions of Bodhisattvas, with jewel curtains hung all around and jewel nets spread above.
The Buddhas of those lands expounded dharmas with great, subtle sounds, and immeasurable thousands of billions of Bodhisattvas were seen filling those lands, expounding the Dharma for the multitudes. The southern, western, northern directions, the four intermediate directions, zenith, and nadir, wherever the light of the white curl illuminated, were also like this.
At that time, the Buddhas of the ten directions each addressed their assemblies of Bodhisattvas: "Good children! I should now go to the Sahā world, to where Śākyamuni Buddha dwells, and make offerings to the jewel stūpa of Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata."
Then the Sahā world was immediately transformed into purity: with lapis lazuli as its ground, adorned with jewel trees, golden cords bordering eight crossroads, free of villages, towns, cities, great oceans, rivers, streams, mountains, forests, and thickets. Great precious incense was burned, and māndārava flowers covered the ground; covered above with jewel nets and curtains, hanging with jewel bells. Only this assembled gathering was retained, while the devas and humans were relocated to other lands.
At that time, the Buddhas each brought a great Bodhisattva as an attendant, arrived in the Sahā world, and each went beneath a jewel tree. Each jewel tree was five hundred yojanas high, successively adorned with branches, leaves, flowers, and fruits. Beneath each jewel tree was a lion seat, five yojanas high, also decorated with great jewels.
Then the Buddhas each sat cross-legged upon these seats. Progressively filling the great three-thousand-fold worlds, the emanation bodies of Śākyamuni Buddha from just one direction were not yet exhausted.
Then Śākyamuni Buddha, wishing to accommodate all his emanation Buddhas, further transformed two hundred million billion inconceivable lands in each of the eight directions, causing them all to become pure, free of hells, hungry ghosts, animals, and asuras, and relocated the devas and humans to other lands.
The transformed lands also had lapis lazuli as their ground, adorned with jewel trees five hundred yojanas high, successively adorned with branches, leaves, flowers, and fruits, with jewel lion seats five yojanas high beneath each tree, adorned with various treasures. They were also free of great oceans, rivers, and mountain kings like Mount Mucilinda, Mahāmucilinda, the Iron Ring Mountains, the Great Iron Ring Mountains, and Mount Sumeru, united into a single Buddha land. The jewel ground was flat, covered with jeweled netting; banners and canopies hung, great precious incense burned, and heavenly jewel flowers covered the ground.
Śākyamuni Buddha, to provide seats for the arriving Buddhas, further transformed another two hundred million billion inconceivable lands in each of the eight directions, causing them all to become pure, free of hells, hungry ghosts, animals, and asuras, relocating devas and humans to other lands. The transformed lands were also of lapis lazuli ground, adorned with jewel trees five hundred yojanas high, with lion seats beneath each tree, united into a single Buddha land, covered with jewel curtains, banners, incense, and heavenly flowers.
Then the emanation bodies of Śākyamuni Buddha, Buddhas from lands numbering hundreds of thousands of millions of inconceivable numbers of Ganges sands in the eastern direction, each expounding the Dharma, gathered here. In this manner successively, the Buddhas of the ten directions all arrived and sat in the eight directions. At that time, in each direction, four hundred million billion inconceivable lands were filled with Buddhas, Tathāgatas.
Then the Buddhas, each seated upon a lion seat beneath a jewel tree, dispatched their attendants to greet Śākyamuni Buddha, each offering a handful of jewel flowers, saying: "Good son! Go to Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa to where Śākyamuni Buddha dwells, and speak as I say: 'Are you free of sickness and affliction? Are your physical energies at ease, and are the assemblies of Bodhisattvas and Śrāvakas at peace?' Scatter these jewel flowers to make offerings to the Buddha, and say: 'That Buddha wishes to join in opening this jewel stūpa.'" All the Buddhas dispatched messengers in this manner.
Then Śākyamuni Buddha, seeing that all his emanation Buddhas had gathered, each seated upon a lion seat, and hearing that the Buddhas all wished to open the jewel stūpa together, immediately arose from his seat and stood in open space. The entire fourfold assembly rose, joined their palms, and single-mindedly gazed upon the Buddha.
Thereupon, Śākyamuni Buddha, with the finger of his right hand, opened the door of the seven-jeweled stūpa; it emitted a great sound, like unbarring a lock and throwing open a great city gate. Immediately the entire assembly beheld Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata seated upon the lion seat within the jewel stūpa, his complete body whole and unscattered, as if entering dhyāna meditation.
They also heard him say: "Excellent, excellent! Śākyamuni Buddha! Joyfully expound this Lotus Sūtra; I have come here for the sake of hearing this scripture."
At that time, the fourfold assembly, seeing a Buddha who had passed into extinction immeasurable thousands of billions of kalpas ago speak such words, praised what they had never seen before, and scattered heaps of heavenly jewel flowers over Prabhūtaratna Buddha and Śākyamuni Buddha.
Then Prabhūtaratna Buddha, from within the jewel stūpa, offered half of his seat to Śākyamuni Buddha, saying: "Śākyamuni Buddha! Take this seat." Immediately Śākyamuni Buddha entered the stūpa, sat upon half of the seat, and sat cross-legged.
Then the great assembly, seeing the two Tathāgatas sitting cross-legged upon the lion seat within the seven-jeweled stūpa, each thought: "The Buddha seats are lofty and distant; we pray the Tathāgata will use spiritual powers to cause us all to abide in open space."
Immediately Śākyamuni Buddha, by spiritual power, drew the great assembly up into open space, and with a great voice universally addressed the fourfold assembly:
"Who can widely expound the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma in this Sahā world? Now is precisely the time. The Tathāgata will before long enter nirvāṇa; the Buddha wishes to entrust this Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma so that it may endure."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
The Holy Lord, the World-Honored One,
Though long passed into extinction,
Within the jewel stūpa,
Still comes for the sake of the Dharma.
How could people
Not exert diligence for the Dharma?
This Buddha passed into extinction
Countless kalpas ago;
Everywhere he listens to the Dharma,
Because it is difficult to encounter.
That Buddha's original vow:
"After my extinction,
Wherever I go,
It is constantly to hear the Dharma."
Furthermore, my emanation
Immeasurable Buddhas,
Countless as Ganges sands,
Have come wishing to hear the Dharma,
And to behold the extinct
Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata.
Each abandoned their wonderful lands,
Disciples,
Devas, humans, Nagas, spirits,
And all articles of offering,
To cause the Dharma to abide long,
Therefore they arrived here.
To provide seats for the Buddhas,
By spiritual powers,
I relocated immeasurable multitudes,
Causing the lands to become pure.
The Buddhas each
Went beneath jewel trees,
Like pure ponds
Adorned with lotus flowers.
Beneath the jewel trees,
Upon the lion seats,
The Buddhas sat,
Radiant and adorned,
As in the dark of night,
A great torch is kindled.
Their bodies emitted wondrous fragrance,
Pervading lands of the ten directions;
Wandering beings receiving this incense,
Were filled with boundless joy.
Like a great wind
Blowing small tree branches;
Through this expedient means,
Causing the Dharma to abide long.
I tell the great assembly:
"After my extinction,
Who can protect, uphold,
Read, and recite this scripture?
Now before the Buddha,
Declare your own vows!
That Prabhūtaratna Buddha,
Though long passed into extinction,
Through his great vow,
Roars the lion's roar.
Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata,
And my own body,
Along with the assembled transformed Buddhas,
You should understand this intent.
Children of the Buddhas,
Who can protect the Dharma?
You should make a great vow,
To cause it to abide long.
Those who are able to protect
The Dharma of this scripture,
Will thereby make offerings
To me and to Prabhūtaratna.
This Prabhūtaratna Buddha,
Dwelling within the jewel stūpa,
Constantly travels the ten directions
For the sake of this scripture;
One also makes offerings
To the arriving transformed Buddhas,
Who adorn and illuminate
All the worlds.
If one expounds this scripture,
One beholds me,
Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata,
And all the transformed Buddhas.
Good children!
Each contemplate carefully:
This is a difficult matter;
You should make a great vow.
Other scriptures,
Are numerous as the sands of the Ganges;
Even if expounding these,
It is not yet difficult.
If one picked up Mount Sumeru,
And hurled it to other directions,
Across countless Buddha lands,
That would not be difficult.
If with a toe,
One moved a great three-thousand-fold worlds,
Hurling it far to other countries,
That would not be difficult.
If standing on the Peak of Existence,
One expounded for the multitude
Immeasurable other scriptures,
That would not be difficult.
If after the Buddha's extinction,
In an evil world,
One is able to expound this scripture,
That indeed is difficult!
Even if a person
Grasped empty space in their hand,
And wandered about with it,
That would not be difficult.
If after my extinction,
One copies and upholds it oneself,
Or causes others to copy it,
That indeed is difficult!
If placing the great earth
Upon a toenail,
One ascended to the Brahmā Heaven,
That would not be difficult.
After the Buddha's extinction,
In an evil world,
To read this scripture for an instant,
That indeed is difficult!
Even if at the kalpa's end-fire,
Carrying dry grass on one's back,
One entered it without being burned,
That would not be difficult.
After my extinction,
If one upholds this scripture,
And speaks it for even one person,
That indeed is difficult!
If one upholds the eighty-four thousand
Treasuries of Dharma,
The twelve divisions of scriptures,
And expounds them to people,
Causing all listeners
To attain the six spiritual superknowledges;
Though able to do this,
It is not yet difficult.
After my extinction,
To listen to and accept this scripture,
And inquire into its meaning,
That indeed is difficult!
If a person expounds the Dharma,
Causing thousands of billions,
Immeasurable, countless
Ganges sands of wandering beings,
To attain arhatship,
Endowed with the six spiritual superknowledges;
Though bringing such benefit,
It is not yet difficult.
After my extinction,
If one can uphold
Such a scripture as this,
That indeed is difficult!
For the sake of the Buddha path,
In immeasurable lands,
From the beginning until now,
I have widely expounded scriptures;
Yet among them,
This scripture is foremost!
If one can uphold it,
One upholds the Buddha's body.
Good children!
After my extinction,
Who can receive, uphold,
Read, and recite this scripture?
Now before the Buddha,
Declare your own vows!
This scripture is difficult to uphold;
If one upholds it for an instant,
I will rejoice,
And all Buddhas will also rejoice.
Such a person as this,
Is praised by all Buddhas.
This is heroism,
This is vigor,
This is named upholding precepts,
And practicing the dhūta austerities;
One will swiftly attain
The unexcelled Buddha path.
Able in future ages
To read and uphold this scripture,
One is a genuine Buddha-child,
Dwelling on the ground of pure goodness.
After the Buddha's extinction,
Able to understand its meaning,
One is the eye of devas and humans
In the world.
In a terrifying world,
Able to expound it for an instant,
All devas and humans
Should make offerings to them."
