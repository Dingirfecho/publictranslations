At that time, the Buddha addressed the great assembly: "In the ancient past, immeasurable, boundless, inconceivable incalculable kalpas ago, there was a Buddha named Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha. His land was named Prabhāsa-maṇḍita, and his kalpa was named Priyadarśana.
Within the Dharma of that Buddha, there was a king named Śubhavyūha. The king's consort was named Vimaladattā. He had two sons: one named Vimalagarbha, the second named Vimalanetra.
These two sons possessed great spiritual powers, merit, and wisdom, having long cultivated the path practiced by Bodhisattvas: namely, dāna-pāramitā, śīla-pāramitā, kṣānti-pāramitā, vīrya-pāramitā, dhyāna-pāramitā, prajñā-pāramitā, upāya-pāramitā, love, compassion, joy, and equanimity, down to the thirty-seven factors of awakening, all clearly penetrated and understood. Furthermore, they had attained the Bodhisattva's Pure samādhi, Constellation samādhi, Pure Light samādhi, Pure Color samādhi, Pure Illumination samādhi, Long Adornment samādhi, and Great Majestic Virtue Treasury samādhi, thoroughly penetrating all these samādhis.
At that time, that Buddha, wishing to guide King Śubhavyūha and out of compassion for wandering beings, expounded this Lotus Sūtra.
Then the two sons, Vimalagarbha and Vimalanetra, went to their mother, joined their palms, and said: 'We pray our mother will go to where Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha is; we too will follow in attendance, to draw near, make offerings, and bow down. For what reason? This Buddha expounds the Lotus Sūtra amidst all assemblies of devas and humans; it is fitting to listen and receive it.'
The mother said to her sons: 'Your father believes and accepts non-Buddhist paths, deeply attached to brāhmaṇa dharmas. You should go speak to your father, so that we may go together.'
Vimalagarbha and Vimalanetra joined their palms and said to their mother: 'We are sons of the Dharma King, yet born into this household of false views.'
The mother said to her sons: 'You should feel concern for your father, and manifest spiritual transformations for him; if he sees them, his heart will certainly become pure, and perhaps he will permit us to go to the Buddha's abode.'
Thereupon the two sons, mindful of their father, sprang into open space to the height of seven tāla trees, and displayed various spiritual transformations: walking, standing, sitting, and lying down in open space; emitting water from the upper body and fire from the lower body; emitting water from the lower body and fire from the upper body; manifesting huge bodies filling open space, and then appearing small; appearing small, and then appearing large; vanishing in open space, and suddenly appearing on the ground; entering the ground as if into water, and walking on water as if on dry ground.
Displaying various spiritual transformations such as these, they caused their royal father's heart to become pure with faith and understanding. At that time, the father, seeing his sons possess such spiritual powers, rejoiced greatly, having attained what he had never had before, joined his palms toward his sons, and said: 'Who is your teacher? Whose disciples are you?'
The two sons said: 'Great King! That Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha is now seated upon the Dharma seat beneath the seven-jeweled Bodhi tree, widely expounding the Lotus Sūtra amidst assemblies of devas and humans of all the world; he is our teacher, and we are his disciples.'
The father said to his sons: 'I now also wish to see your teacher; we may go together.'
Thereupon the two sons descended from open space, went to their mother, joined their palms, and said: 'Our royal father has now attained faith and understanding, capable of generating the mind of anuttarasamyaksambodhi. We have performed Buddha activities for our father; we pray our mother will grant permission for us to leave home and cultivate the path under that Buddha.'
Then the two sons, wishing to restate their intention, spoke to their mother in gāthās:
Pray let our mother release us,
To leave home and become śramaṇas;
The Buddhas are exceedingly difficult to encounter,
We will follow the Buddha to learn.
Like the uḍumbara flower,
Encountering a Buddha is more difficult than this;
Escaping tribulations is also difficult;
Pray permit us to leave home!
The mother immediately said: 'I permit you to leave home. For what reason? Because the Buddha is difficult to encounter.'
Thereupon the two sons said to their parents: 'Excellent, father and mother! Pray go in due time to where Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha is, to draw near and make offerings. For what reason? The Buddha is difficult to encounter, like the uḍumbara flower, and like a one-eyed turtle encountering a hole in a floating log. Yet our past merit is deep and heavy, born to encounter the Buddha-Dharma; therefore our parents should permit us to leave home. For what reason? The Buddhas are difficult to encounter, and the time is difficult to meet.'
At that time, eighty-four thousand people in King Śubhavyūha's inner palace were all capable of receiving and upholding this Lotus Sūtra. Vimalanetra Bodhisattva had long penetrated the Lotus of Dharma samādhi; Vimalagarbha Bodhisattva had throughout immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of kalpas penetrated the samādhi of Abandoning Evil Destinies, wishing to cause all wandering beings to abandon evil destinies. The king's consort had attained the samādhi of the Gathering of Buddhas, able to know the secret treasuries of the Buddhas.
The two sons, through the power of expedient means, skillfully transformed their father in this manner, causing his heart to believe, understand, and delight in the Buddha-Dharma.
Thereupon King Śubhavyūha, together with his ministers and attendants; Queen Vimaladattā, together with the court ladies of the inner palace; and the king's two sons, together with forty-two thousand people, all went together to where the Buddha was. Having arrived, they bowed with heads touching his feet, circumambulated the Buddha three times, and withdrew to stand on one side.
Then that Buddha expounded the Dharma for the king, instructing, benefiting, and delighting him. The king was exceedingly glad.
Then King Śubhavyūha and his queen unfastened pearl necklaces from their necks worth hundreds of thousands, scattering them over the Buddha; in open space they transformed into a four-pillared jewel tower, within which was a great precious couch spread with hundreds of thousands of heavenly robes, upon which sat a Buddha cross-legged, emitting great light.
Then King Śubhavyūha thought: 'The Buddha's body is rare, upright, extraordinary, accomplishing foremost subtle color.'
Then Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha addressed the fourfold assembly: 'Do you see this King Śubhavyūha standing with joined palms before me? This king, within my Dharma, will become a bhikṣu, diligently cultivating and aiding the Buddha path, and will become a Buddha named Śālendrarāja, his land named Prabhāsa, and his kalpa named Mahāprabhāvarāja. That Śālendrarāja Buddha will have an immeasurable assembly of Bodhisattvas and immeasurable Śrāvakas, his land level and flat, his merits like this.'
The king immediately entrusted his kingdom to his younger brother, and together with his queen, two sons, and retinues, left home to cultivate the path within the Buddha-Dharma.
Having left home, throughout eighty-four thousand years, the king constantly exerted diligence and vigor in practicing the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma. After this had passed, he attained the samādhi of All Pure Virtue Adornments, immediately ascended into open space to the height of seven tāla trees, and addressed the Buddha:
'World-Honored One! These two sons of mine have already performed Buddha activities, through spiritual transformations turning my false mind, enabling me to abide peacefully within the Buddha-Dharma and behold the World-Honored One. These two sons are my good friends; wishing to awaken my wholesome roots of past lives and benefit me, they were born into my household.'
Then Jaladharagarjitasvaranakṣatrarājasaṃkusumitābhijña Buddha addressed King Śubhavyūha: 'Thus it is, thus it is! Just as you have said. If a good man or good woman plants roots of virtue, life after life they attain good friends; their good friends can perform Buddha activities, instructing, benefiting, and delighting them, causing them to enter anuttarasamyaksambodhi.
Great King! You should know that a good friend is a great causal condition: that is to say, guiding and leading so that one attains beholding the Buddha and generating the mind of anuttarasamyaksambodhi.
Great King! Do you see these two sons? These two sons have already made offerings to Buddhas equal to sixty-five hundred thousand billion inconceivable numbers of Ganges sands, drawing near and revering them, receiving and upholding the Lotus Sūtra under those Buddhas, pitying wandering beings of false views and causing them to abide in right view.'
King Śubhavyūha immediately descended from open space and addressed the Buddha: 'World-Honored One! The Tathāgata is exceedingly rare; through merit and wisdom, the flesh-topknot on his crown shines brightly, his eyes are long, broad, and deep blue, the curl of hair between his eyebrows is white as a gleaming moon, his teeth are white, even, and closely set, constantly radiant, and his lips are red and beautiful like the bimba fruit.'
Then King Śubhavyūha, having praised the Buddha's immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of merits, before the Tathāgata single-mindedly joined his palms and addressed the Buddha again: 'World-Honored One! It is unprecedented. The Tathāgata's Dharma is fully endowed with inconceivable, subtle merits; the conduct taught by his precepts brings peace and swift goodness. From this day forward, I will no longer follow the impulses of my own mind, nor give rise to false views, arrogance, anger, or evil thoughts.' Having spoken these words, he bowed to the Buddha and departed."
The Buddha addressed the great assembly: "What do you think: could King Śubhavyūha have been someone else? It was Padmaśrī Bodhisattva himself. Queen Vimaladattā was Vairocanaraśmipratimaṇḍitadhvajā Bodhisattva, who now stands before the Buddha, born in that realm out of compassion for King Śubhavyūha and his retinues.
The two sons were Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva and Bhaiṣajyasamudgata Bodhisattva. These Bodhisattvas Bhaiṣajyarāja and Bhaiṣajyasamudgata have accomplished such great merits, having planted roots of virtue under immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of Buddhas, accomplishing inconceivable wholesome merits. If someone knows the names of these two Bodhisattvas, devas and humans of all the world should bow down to them in homage."
When the Buddha expounded this Former activities of King Śubhavyūha Chapter, eighty-four thousand persons departed from dust and defilement, attaining the pure Dharma eye amidst all dharmas.
