At that time, the Buddha addressed Nityodyukta Bodhisattva Mahāsattva: "If a good man or good woman receives and upholds this Lotus Sūtra, whether reading, reciting, explaining, or copying it, that person will attain eight hundred eye merits, twelve hundred ear merits, eight hundred nose merits, twelve hundred tongue merits, eight hundred body merits, and twelve hundred mind merits. With these merits, they adorn the six sense faculties, causing them all to become pure.
This good person, using the pure, natural eyes they were born with, sees all mountains, forests, rivers, and oceans within and without the great three-thousand-fold worlds, downward to the Avīci Hell and upward to the Peak of Existence; and sees all wandering beings within them, as well as their karmic causes and conditions, and the rebirth places of their karmic retributions, completely seeing and knowing them all."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
If amidst the great assembly,
With a fearless mind,
One expounds this Lotus Sūtra,
Listen to their merits!
This person attains eight hundred
Extraordinary eye merits;
Adorned through these,
Their eyes are exceedingly pure.
With the eyes given by their parents,
They see the three-thousand-fold worlds,
Inner and outer Mount Merus,
Sumeru and the Iron Ring Mountains,
As well as other mountains and forests,
Waters of great oceans, rivers, and streams;
Downward to the Avīci Hell,
Upward to the Peak of Existence;
All wandering beings within them,
All are fully seen.
Although not having attained the divine eye,
The power of their flesh eye is like this.
"Furthermore, Nityodyukta! If a good man or good woman receives and upholds this scripture, whether reading, reciting, explaining, or copying it, they attain twelve hundred ear merits.
With this pure ear, they hear the various languages and sounds within and without the great three-thousand-fold worlds, downward to the Avīci Hell and upward to the Peak of Existence: elephant sounds, horse sounds, ox sounds, carriage sounds, weeping sounds, lamenting sounds, conch sounds, drum sounds, bell sounds, chime sounds, laughing sounds, speaking sounds, men's sounds, women's sounds, boys' sounds, girls' sounds, Dharma sounds, non-Dharma sounds, sounds of suffering, sounds of joy, common mortals' sounds, sages' sounds, joyful sounds, unpleasant sounds;
Sounds of devas, Nagas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, fire sounds, water sounds, wind sounds, hell sounds, animal sounds, hungry ghost sounds, bhikṣus' sounds, bhikṣuṇīs' sounds, Śrāvakas' sounds, Pratyekabuddhas' sounds, Bodhisattvas' sounds, and Buddhas' sounds.
In essence: all sounds within and without the great three-thousand-fold worlds, although they have not yet attained the divine ear, with the pure, ordinary ear given by their parents, they hear and know them all, distinguishing various sounds like this without destroying the ear faculty."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
With the ears given by their parents,
Pure and free of turbidity,
With this ordinary ear they hear
The sounds of the three-thousand-fold worlds:
Sounds of elephants, horses, carriages, and oxen,
Bells, chimes, conches, and drums,
Lutes, harps, pipes, and flutes,
Pure and fine songs;
Listening without attachment,
Countless kinds of human speech,
Hearing, they can understand it all.
Also hearing the sounds of devas,
Subtle and sublime songs,
Hearing the sounds of men and women,
And the voices of boys and girls.
In mountains, rivers, and perilous ravines,
Sounds of kalaviṅka birds,
Jīvaṃjīvaka birds and others,
Hearing all their voices.
The various agonizing torments of hells,
Sounds of sharp suffering,
Hungry ghosts pressed by hunger and thirst,
Sounds of seeking food and drink;
Asuras,
Dwelling by the shores of the great ocean,
When speaking among themselves,
Uttering loud voices.
The expounder of Dharma,
Abiding peacefully here,
Hears all these sounds from afar,
Without damaging the ear faculty.
In worlds of the ten directions,
Birds and beasts calling to one another,
This expounder of Dharma,
Hears them all from here.
In the Brahmā heavens above,
Light-Sound and Universal Purity,
Upward to the Peak of Existence,
The sounds of their speech,
The Dharma teacher dwelling here,
Is completely able to hear.
All assemblies of bhikṣus,
And bhikṣuṇīs,
Whether reading and reciting scriptures,
Or speaking them for others,
The Dharma teacher dwelling here,
Is completely able to hear.
Furthermore, Bodhisattvas,
Reading and reciting the scriptural Dharma,
Or speaking it for others,
Compiling and explaining its meaning,
All such sounds as these,
He is completely able to hear.
The Buddhas, Great Holy Honored Ones,
Who teach and transform wandering beings,
Amidst great assemblies,
Expounding the subtle, wondrous Dharma;
One who upholds this Lotus,
Is completely able to hear.
All sounds within and without
The great three-thousand-fold worlds,
Downward to the Avīci Hell,
Upward to the Peak of Existence,
Hearing all their sounds,
Without damaging the ear faculty;
Because the ear is sharp,
Able to distinguish and know them all.
One who upholds this Lotus,
Though not yet having attained the divine ear,
Merely using the ear given through birth,
Their merits are already like this.
"Furthermore, Nityodyukta! If a good man or good woman receives and upholds this scripture, whether reading, reciting, explaining, or copying it, they accomplish eight hundred nose merits.
With this pure nose faculty, they smell various fragrances above, below, within, and without the great three-thousand-fold worlds: sumanā flower fragrance, jātī flower fragrance, mallikā flower fragrance, campaka flower fragrance, pāṭala flower fragrance, red lotus flower fragrance, blue lotus flower fragrance, white lotus flower fragrance, flowering tree fragrance, fruit tree fragrance, sandalwood fragrance, aloeswood fragrance, tamālapatra fragrance, tagara fragrance, and thousands of kinds of blended incenses, whether powdered, pellets, or paste. One who upholds this scripture, dwelling here, can distinguish them all.
Furthermore, they distinguish the fragrances of wandering beings: elephant scent, horse scent, ox and sheep scent, man scent, woman scent, boy scent, girl scent, and the scents of grasses, trees, thickets, and forests—whether near or far, all fragrances are smelled and distinguished without error.
One who upholds this scripture, though dwelling here, also smells the fragrances of heavenly devas: pārijātaka and kovidāra tree fragrances, māndārava flower fragrance, mahā-māndārava flower fragrance, mañjūṣaka flower fragrance, mahā-mañjūṣaka flower fragrance, sandalwood, aloeswood, various powdered incenses, and various flower fragrances—all such blended heavenly fragrances are smelled and known.
They also smell the body scents of devas: the scent of Śakra Devānām Indra enjoying the pleasures of the five desires in his palace; the scent when expounding the Dharma for the Trāyastriṃśa devas in the Hall of Good Dharma; the scent when playing in gardens; and the body scents of other devas, male and female, all smelled from afar. In this manner successively reaching the Brahmā worlds, upward to the body scents of devas of the Peak of Existence, all are smelled, as well as the incenses burned by devas.
And the scent of Śrāvakas, Pratyekabuddhas, Bodhisattvas, and the body scents of Buddhas are all smelled from afar, knowing where they dwell. Although smelling these scents, the nose faculty is not impaired or confused; if wishing to distinguish and explain them for others, their memory is without error."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
This person's nose is pure;
Within this world,
Whether fragrant or foul objects,
They smell and know them all.
Sumanā, jātī,
Tamālapatra, sandalwood,
Aloeswood, and cassia fragrance,
Fragrances of various flowers and fruits,
And knowing the scents of wandering beings,
Scents of men and women;
The expounder of Dharma dwelling far away,
Smells the scent and knows where they are.
Great wheel-turning kings,
Lesser wheel-turning kings and their sons,
Ministers and palace attendants,
Smelling the scent, knows where they are.
Jewels worn upon the body,
Treasures buried in the earth,
And the wheel-turning king's precious women,
Smelling the scent, knows where they are.
People's personal ornaments,
Clothing, necklaces,
And various paste incenses,
Smelling the scent, knows their bodies.
Devas walking or sitting,
Sporting and displaying spiritual transformations;
One who upholds this Lotus,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Flowers and fruits of trees,
And the fragrance of ghee,
The upholder of the scripture dwelling here,
Knows where they all are.
In deep, perilous mountain places,
Where sandalwood trees blossom,
Wandering beings dwelling within them,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
The Iron Ring Mountains, the great ocean,
And wandering beings within the earth,
The upholder of the scripture smelling the scent,
Knows where they all are.
Asura men and women,
And their retinues,
When fighting or sporting,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
In wildernesses and narrow passes,
Lions, elephants, tigers, wolves,
Wild oxen, and water buffaloes,
Smelling the scent, knows where they are.
If there are pregnant women,
Whose child's sex is not yet known,
Deformed or non-human,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Through the power of smelling scent,
Knowing the initial pregnancy,
Whether it will be brought to term or not,
And if she will safely bear a blessed child.
Through the power of smelling scent,
Knowing the thoughts of men and women,
Minds of lust, delusion, or anger,
And knowing those who cultivate goodness.
Hidden treasuries within the earth,
Gold, silver, and precious gems,
Stored within bronze vessels,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Various necklaces,
Whose value none can know;
Smelling the scent, knows their value,
Their origin, and location.
All flowers in the heavens,
Māndārava, mañjūṣaka,
And the pārijātaka tree,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
All palaces in the heavens,
Superior, middling, and inferior,
Adorned with various jewel flowers,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Heavenly gardens and excellent palaces,
Pavilions and the Hall of Good Dharma,
Sporting within them,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
When devas listen to the Dharma,
Or indulge in the five desires,
Coming, going, walking, sitting, or lying down,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Robes worn by heavenly maidens,
Adorned with fine flower scents,
When whirling and sporting,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Successively ascending in this manner,
Reaching the Brahmā worlds,
Those entering or emerging from dhyāna,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Light-Sound and Universal Purity heavens,
Upward to the Peak of Existence,
Newly born or passing away,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Assemblies of bhikṣus,
Constantly vigorous in the Dharma,
Whether sitting or walking,
Reading and reciting scriptural Dharma,
Or beneath forest trees,
Concentrated in seated meditation;
The upholder of the scripture smelling the scent,
Knows where they all are.
Bodhisattvas firm in resolve,
Sitting in meditation or reading and reciting,
Or speaking the Dharma for others,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
The World-Honored Ones in every direction,
Revered by all,
Pitying beings and expounding the Dharma,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Wandering beings before the Buddha,
Rejoicing upon hearing the scripture,
Practicing according to the Dharma,
Smelling the scent, can know them all.
Although not yet having attained the Bodhisattva's
Nose born of uncontaminated Dharma,
This upholder of the scripture,
First attains such marks of the nose as these.
"Furthermore, Nityodyukta! If a good man or good woman receives and upholds this scripture, whether reading, reciting, explaining, or copying it, they attain twelve hundred tongue merits.
Whether fine or repulsive, delicious or unpalatable, and all bitter and astringent substances, when placed upon their tongue faculty, all transform into superior flavors, like heavenly amṛta, none that are not delicious.
If with the tongue faculty amidst the great assembly they proclaim anything, uttering a deep, wondrous sound, it can enter their hearts, causing them all to attain joy and delight.
Furthermore, sons of devas, heavenly maidens, Śakra, Brahmā, and devas, hearing this deep, wondrous sound and the orderly sequence of speech expounded, will all come to listen. And Nagas, Naga maidens, yakṣas, yakṣa maidens, gandharvas, gandharva maidens, asuras, asura maidens, garuḍas, garuḍa maidens, kiṃnaras, kiṃnara maidens, mahoragas, and mahoraga maidens, for the sake of hearing the Dharma, will all come to draw close, revering and making offerings.
And bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, upāsikās, kings, princes, ministers, retinues, lesser wheel-turning kings, great wheel-turning kings, with their seven treasures, thousand sons, and inner and outer retinues, riding their palaces, will come together to listen to the Dharma, because this Bodhisattva skillfully expounds the Dharma. Brāhmaṇas, householders, and the people of the land will, for the rest of their lives, attend upon and make offerings to him.
Furthermore, Śrāvakas, Pratyekabuddhas, Bodhisattvas, and Buddhas constantly delight in seeing him. Wherever this person resides, the Buddhas all face that direction to expound the Dharma; he is able fully to receive and uphold all Buddha-dharmas, and can also emit deep, wondrous Dharma sounds."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
This person's tongue faculty is pure,
Never receiving bad tastes;
Whatever is eaten,
All turns into amṛta.
With a deep, pure, wondrous sound,
Expounding the Dharma amidst the great assembly,
With causal conditions and parables,
Guiding the minds of wandering beings;
Listeners all rejoice,
Setting forth superior offerings.
Devas, Nagas, yakṣas,
And asuras,
All with reverent minds,
Come together to listen to the Dharma;
This expounder of Dharma,
If wishing with a wondrous sound
To fill the three-thousand-fold worlds,
Can reach it as desired.
Great and lesser wheel-turning kings,
With their thousand sons and retinues,
With palms joined and reverent minds,
Constantly come to listen and receive the Dharma.
Devas, Nagas, yakṣas,
Rākṣasas, and piśācas,
Also with joyful minds,
Constantly delight in coming to make offerings.
Brahmā Kings, Mara Kings,
Īśvara, and Maheśvara,
Such assemblies of devas,
Constantly come to where he is.
The Buddhas and their disciples,
Hearing his sound of expounding the Dharma,
Constantly remember and guard him,
And at times manifest their bodies for him.
"Furthermore, Nityodyukta! If a good man or good woman receives and upholds this scripture, whether reading, reciting, explaining, or copying it, they attain eight hundred body merits.
They attain a pure body, like pure lapis lazuli, which wandering beings delight in seeing. Because of the purity of their body, wandering beings of the great three-thousand-fold worlds—at the time of birth, at the time of death, high or low, fair or ugly, born in good or evil destinies—all appear within it. And the Iron Ring Mountains, Great Iron Ring Mountains, Mount Meru, Mahāmeru, and other mountains, as well as the wandering beings within them, all appear within it. Downward to the Avīci Hell and upward to the Peak of Existence, all things and wandering beings appear within it. If Śrāvakas, Pratyekabuddhas, Bodhisattvas, or Buddhas expound the Dharma, all manifest their forms and images within his body."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
If one upholds the Lotus,
Their body is exceedingly pure,
Like pure lapis lazuli,
Delighted in by all wandering beings.
Like a pure, bright mirror,
Fully reflecting all forms and images;
The Bodhisattva within his pure body,
Sees all that exists in the world;
He alone clearly understands,
While other people cannot see.
Within the three-thousand-fold worlds,
All living creatures,
Devas, humans, asuras,
Hells, ghosts, and animals,
All such forms and images,
All manifest within his body.
The palaces of devas,
Upward to the Peak of Existence,
The Iron Ring and Mount Meru,
And Mahāmeru,
Waters of great oceans,
All manifest within his body.
The Buddhas and Śrāvakas,
Buddha-children, Bodhisattvas,
Whether solitary or amidst assemblies,
Expounding the Dharma, all manifest.
Although not having attained the uncontaminated,
Wondrous body of Dharma-nature,
Within his pure, ordinary body,
All things manifest within it.
"Furthermore, Nityodyukta! If a good man or good woman, after the Tathāgata's extinction, receives and upholds this scripture, whether reading, reciting, explaining, or copying it, they attain twelve hundred mind merits.
With this pure mind faculty, hearing even a single gāthā or a single phrase, they penetrate immeasurable, boundless meanings. Having understood this meaning, they can expound a single phrase or a single gāthā for one month, four months, or up to a year; all the dharmas expounded, accordant with their meaning, will not conflict with the genuine mark.
If speaking of worldly books, governing speech, or livelihoods, all accord with the correct Dharma. Wandering beings of the six destinies in the great three-thousand-fold worlds, the movements of their minds, the actions of their minds, and the discursive play of their minds, they know them all.
Although they have not yet attained uncontaminated wisdom, their mind faculty is pure like this. Whatever this person contemplates, ponders, or speaks, all is the Buddha-Dharma, without falsehood, and was also spoken in the scriptures of past Buddhas."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
This person's mind is pure,
Bright, sharp, and free of defilement;
With this wondrous mind faculty,
Knowing superior, middling, and inferior dharmas;
Hearing even a single gāthā,
Penetrating immeasurable meanings;
Successively expounding according to the Dharma,
For a month, four months, up to a year.
Within and without this world,
All wandering beings,
Devas, Nagas, humans,
Yakṣas, ghosts, and spirits,
Those in the six destinies,
Whatever they contemplate,
As the reward for upholding the Lotus,
All at once he fully knows.
Countless Buddhas of the ten directions,
Adorned with marks of a hundred merits,
Expounding the Dharma for wandering beings,
He hears and can uphold it all.
Contemplating immeasurable meanings,
Expounding the Dharma immeasurably,
From beginning to end never forgetting or erring,
Because of upholding the Lotus.
Fully knowing the marks of all dharmas,
Recognizing sequences according to the meaning,
Penetrating names and language,
Expounding as he knows.
Whatever this person speaks,
Is all the Dharma of past Buddhas;
Because of expounding this Dharma,
Dwelling amidst the assembly without fear.
One who upholds the Lotus Sūtra,
Has a mind faculty pure like this;
Though not yet having attained the uncontaminated,
First possesses such marks as these.
This person upholding this scripture,
Abides peacefully on a rare ground;
By all wandering beings,
Joyfully loved and revered.
Able with thousands of kinds
Of skillful words,
Distinguishing and expounding the Dharma,
Because of upholding the Lotus Sūtra.
