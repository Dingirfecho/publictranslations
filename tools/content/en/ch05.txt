At that time, the World-Honored One addressed Mahākāśyapa and the other great disciples: "Excellent, excellent! Kāśyapa has skillfully spoken of the genuine virtues and merits of the Tathāgata. Truly it is as you have said: the Tathāgata furthermore possesses immeasurable, boundless incalculable virtues and merits; even if you were to speak of them for immeasurable billions of kalpas, you could not exhaust them.
Kāśyapa! You should know that the Tathāgata is the King of all dharmas; whatever he speaks is not false. Regarding all dharmas, he expounds them with wisdom and expedient means; the Dharma he expounds all reaches the ground of all-knowledge. The Tathāgata observes and knows the final destination of all dharmas, and also knows the movements deep within the hearts of all wandering beings, penetrating without obstruction. Furthermore, he fully and clearly understands all dharmas, revealing all wisdom to wandering beings.
Kāśyapa! Suppose in a great three-thousand-fold worlds, the mountains, rivers, valleys, and earth give birth to plants, trees, thickets, forests, and various medicinal herbs, of many varieties, with differing names and forms. A dense cloud spreads wide, completely covering the great three-thousand-fold worlds, raining down equally all at once, its moisture pervading everywhere. The plants, trees, thickets, forests, and medicinal herbs—those with small roots, small stems, small branches, and small leaves; those with medium roots, medium stems, medium branches, and medium leaves; those with large roots, large stems, large branches, and large leaves; trees great and small, according to whether superior, middling, or inferior—each receives a share. Rained upon by a single cloud, each grows according to its nature, blossoming with flowers and bearing fruit. Although born of the same earth and moistened by the same rain, yet the various grasses and trees have differences.
Kāśyapa! You should know that the Tathāgata is also like this. Appearing in the world like the rising of a great cloud, with a great voice he universally reaches the devas, humans, and asuras of the world, just as that great cloud completely covers the great three-thousand-fold worlds. Amidst the great assembly, he proclaims these words: 'I am the Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha, Vidyācaraṇasampanna, Sugata, Lokavid, Anuttara, Puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstā Devamanuṣyāṇām, Buddha, Bhagavān. Those who are not yet liberated I cause to be liberated; those who are not yet awakened I cause to be awakened; those who are not yet at peace I cause to be at peace; those who have not yet attained nirvāṇa I cause to attain nirvāṇa. The present world and future worlds, I know them as they truly are. I am the all-knowing one, the all-seeing one, the knower of the path, the opener of the path, the proclaimer of the path. You assemblies of devas, humans, and asuras should all come here to hear the Dharma.'
At that time, countless thousands of millions of varieties of wandering beings come to the Buddha's abode to hear the Dharma. The Tathāgata then observes whether the faculties of these wandering beings are sharp or dull, whether they are vigorous or negligent, and expounds the Dharma for them according to what they can bear, in various immeasurable ways, causing them all to rejoice and quickly attain wholesome benefits.
These wandering beings, having heard this Dharma, are at peace in this present life and reborn in good destinies in future lives, experiencing joy through the path and also hearing the Dharma. Having heard the Dharma, they are free from obstacles; amidst all dharmas, according to their capacity, they gradually enter the path. Just like that great cloud raining upon all plants, trees, thickets, forests, and medicinal herbs, according to their nature, they fully receive moisture and each grows.
The Dharma expounded by the Tathāgata is of one mark and one flavor—that is to say: the mark of liberation, the mark of detachment, and the mark of cessation, ultimately reaching all-knowledge. If there are wandering beings who hear the Tathāgata's Dharma, whether upholding, reading, reciting, or practicing it as taught, the virtues and merits they attain they do not perceive or know themselves.
For what reason? Only the Tathāgata knows the types, marks, substances, and natures of these wandering beings: what they are mindful of, what they contemplate, what they cultivate; how they are mindful, how they contemplate, how they cultivate; with what dharma they are mindful, with what dharma they contemplate, with what dharma they cultivate; and by what dharma they attain what dharma. Wandering beings dwell upon various levels, and only the Tathāgata sees them as they truly are, clearly and without hindrance. Just as those plants, trees, thickets, forests, and medicinal herbs do not themselves know whether their nature is superior, middling, or inferior.
The Tathāgata knows this Dharma of one mark and one flavor—namely, the mark of liberation, the mark of detachment, and the mark of cessation—the mark of ultimate nirvāṇa, constantly tranquil and extinguished, returning ultimately to emptiness. The Buddha, having known this, observes the desires in the hearts of wandering beings and protects them; therefore, he does not immediately expound all-knowledge for them.
All of you, Kāśyapa, are exceedingly rare, able to know that the Tathāgata expounds the Dharma according to what is appropriate, able to believe and accept it. For what reason? The Dharma expounded by the Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, according to what is appropriate, is difficult to understand and difficult to know."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
The Dharma King who shatters existence,
Appears within the world;
According to the desires of wandering beings,
He expounds the Dharma in various ways.
The Tathāgata is revered and honored,
His wisdom profound and far-reaching;
Long silent on this essential,
He did not hasten to speak it.
If those with wisdom hear it,
They can believe and understand;
Those without wisdom doubt and regret,
Thereby losing it forever.
Therefore, Kāśyapa!
According to their capacities he speaks for them,
By various conditions,
Causing them to attain right view.
Kāśyapa, you should know!
Just like a great cloud,
Arising within the world,
Universally covering all;
The cloud of wisdom contains moisture,
Lightning flashes gleam and shine,
Thunder rumbles afar,
Causing the multitude to rejoice.
The light of the sun is veiled,
Coolness descends upon the earth,
Dense vapor hangs and spreads,
As if it could be gathered in hand.
Its rain falls equally and universally,
Pouring down from the four quarters together,
Streaming down immeasurably,
Saturating all the land.
Mountains, rivers, perilous ravines,
And deep recesses where there grow
Plants, trees, and medicinal herbs,
Trees both large and small,
The sprouts of the hundred grains,
Sugar cane and grapes,
Moistened by the rain,
All without exception are enriched and filled;
The dry ground is universally soaked,
Herbs and trees flourish together.
What pours forth from that cloud
Is water of a single flavor;
Grasses, trees, thickets, and forests
Receive moisture according to their portion.
All the various trees,
Superior, middling, and inferior,
According to their size,
Each attains growth;
Roots, stems, branches, and leaves,
The luster and color of flowers and fruits,
Reached by the single rain,
All become fresh and lustrous.
According to their substance and marks,
The size of their nature and capacity,
That which moistens them is one,
Yet each flourishes distinctly.
The Buddha is also like this,
Appearing within the world,
Just like a great cloud,
Universally covering all.
Having appeared in the world,
For the sake of wandering beings,
He distinguishes and expounds
The reality of all dharmas.
The Great Sage, the World-Honored One,
Amidst devas, humans,
And all the assemblies,
Proclaims these words:
"I am the Tathāgata,
The Honored Among Two-Legged Beings;
Appearing within the world,
Just like a great cloud,
Moistening all.
Withered wandering beings,
I cause them all to abandon suffering,
Attaining peaceful joy—
Worldly joy,
And the joy of nirvāṇa.
Assemblies of devas and humans,
Listen well with one mind;
You should all come here
To behold the Unexcelled Honored One."
I am the World-Honored One,
None can equal me;
To bring peace to wandering beings,
Therefore I appear in the world.
Expounding for the great assembly
The pure Dharma of amṛta;
Its Dharma is of one flavor,
Liberation and nirvāṇa.
With a single wondrous voice,
Proclaiming this meaning,
Constantly for the Great Vehicle,
Creating causal conditions.
I observe all beings,
Universally equal,
Without this or that,
Without thoughts of love or hate.
I have no greedy attachment,
Nor any limit or obstruction;
Constantly for all,
I expound the Dharma equally.
As for a single person,
So it is for multitudes;
Constantly expounding the Dharma,
Never having any other concern.
Going, coming, sitting, or standing,
Never weary or fatigued,
Filling the world,
Like rain universally moistening.
Noble or base, high or low,
Upholding precepts or breaking precepts,
Endowed with proper deportment,
Or not endowed with it,
Right views or false views,
Sharp faculties or dull faculties,
Equally I rain down the Dharma rain,
Without weariness or indolence.
All wandering beings
Who hear my Dharma,
Receive it according to their capacity,
Dwelling upon various levels.
Whether dwelling among humans or devas,
As wheel-turning sage kings,
Or kings like Śakra and Brahmā,
These are the small medicinal herbs.
Knowing uncontaminated dharmas,
Able to attain nirvāṇa,
Giving rise to six spiritual superknowledges,
And attaining the three knowledges,
Dwelling alone in mountain forests,
Constantly practicing dhyāna meditation,
Attaining the realization of Conditioned Awakening,
These are the middling medicinal herbs.
Seeking the state of the World-Honored One:
"I will become a Buddha,"
Practicing vigor and meditation,
These are the superior medicinal herbs.
Furthermore, Buddha-children,
Whose minds are devoted to the Buddha path,
Constantly practicing love and compassion,
Knowing of themselves they will become Buddhas,
Definitive and without doubt,
These are named small trees.
Dwelling at peace in spiritual powers,
Turning the irreversible wheel,
Liberating immeasurable billions,
Hundreds of thousands of wandering beings,
Such Bodhisattvas as these
Are named great trees.
The Buddha expounds equally,
Like rain of a single flavor;
According to the nature of wandering beings,
What they receive differs,
Just as those herbs and trees
Receive their endowment differently.
The Buddha, through this parable,
Expediently opens and reveals;
With various words,
Expounding the One Dharma,
Which to the Buddha's wisdom
Is like a single drop in the ocean.
I rain down the Dharma rain,
Filling the world;
The Dharma of a single flavor
Is cultivated according to capacity.
Like those thickets, forests,
Medicinal herbs, and trees,
According to their size,
Gradually increase in lush beauty.
The Dharma of all Buddhas
Is constantly of a single flavor,
Causing all in the world
Universally to be fulfilled;
Gradually cultivating,
All attain the fruits of the path.
Śrāvakas and Conditioned Buddhas,
Dwelling in mountain forests,
Abiding in their final bodies,
Hearing the Dharma and attaining the fruit,
Are named medicinal herbs,
Each attaining growth.
If Bodhisattvas
Possess firm wisdom,
Penetrating the three realms,
Seeking the supreme vehicle,
These are named small trees,
Attaining growth.
Again, there are those who dwell in meditation,
Attaining spiritual powers,
Hearing of the emptiness of all dharmas,
Their hearts rejoicing greatly,
Emitting countless lights,
Liberating wandering beings,
These are named great trees,
Attaining growth.
Thus, Kāśyapa!
The Dharma expounded by the Buddha
Is just like a great cloud;
With rain of a single flavor,
It moistens the flowers among humans,
Each attaining fruition.
Kāśyapa, you should know!
Through various causal conditions
And various parables,
Opening and revealing the Buddha path,
This is my expedient means;
All Buddhas are also thus.
Now for all of you,
I speak the most real matter:
"The assemblies of Śrāvakas
Have none of them attained extinction.
What you practice
Is the Bodhisattva path;
Gradually cultivating and learning,
You will all become Buddhas."
