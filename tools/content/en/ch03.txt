At that time, Śāriputra leaped up in joy. Immediately rising, he joined his palms, gazed up at the venerable countenance, and addressed the Buddha: Now, hearing this Dharma sound from the World-Honored One, my heart leaps with joy, having attained what I never experienced before.
For what reason? In the past, I heard such Dharma from the Buddha and saw the Bodhisattvas receive prophecies of attaining Buddhahood, yet we were not included in this matter. I deeply grieved and lamented losing the Tathāgata's immeasurable knowledge and vision.
World-Honored One! I was constantly dwelling alone in mountain forests or under trees, whether sitting or walking, and always had this thought: "We equally enter into Dharma-nature. Why does the Tathāgata liberate us through the Small Vehicle?" This was our own fault, not that of the World-Honored One.
For what reason? If we had waited for the explanation of the causes for accomplishing anuttarasamyaksambodhi, we would certainly have attained liberation through the Great Vehicle. Yet we did not understand the expedient means expounded according to what is appropriate. Upon first hearing the Buddha-Dharma, encountering it, we immediately believed, accepted, contemplated, and realized it.
World-Honored One! From the past onward, throughout the day and through the night, I constantly blamed myself. Yet now, hearing from the Buddha what I had never heard before, this unprecedented Dharma, I have severed all doubts and regrets. My body and mind are at ease, happily attaining peaceful security. Today I finally know that I am truly a Buddha-child, born from the Buddha's mouth, born from the transformation of the Dharma, obtaining a share of the Buddha-Dharma.
Thereupon, Śāriputra, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Hearing this sound of Dharma,
I have attained what I never had before;
My heart harbors great joy,
The net of doubts is completely eliminated.
From the past, receiving the Buddha's teaching,
I have not lost the Great Vehicle.
The Buddha's sound is exceedingly rare,
Able to eliminate the afflictions of wandering beings;
I have already attained the exhaustion of outpourings,
Yet hearing it also eliminates grief and affliction.
Dwelling in mountain valleys,
Or beneath forest trees,
Whether sitting or walking,
I constantly contemplated this matter,
Alas, deeply reproaching myself:
Why have I deceived myself?
We too are Buddha-children,
Equally entering uncontaminated dharmas,
Yet unable in future ages
To proclaim the unexcelled path.
The golden color, the thirty-two marks,
The ten powers, and the liberations,
Together within the single Dharma,
Yet we did not attain these matters.
The eighty subtle marks,
The eighteen distinct dharmas,
Virtues and merits such as these,
I have completely lost.
When walking alone,
I saw the Buddha amidst the great assembly,
His fame filling the ten directions,
Extensively benefiting wandering beings.
Reflecting on having lost this benefit,
I considered that I was deceiving myself.
Constantly, through day and night,
I always contemplated this matter,
Wishing to ask the World-Honored One:
Have I lost it or not?
I constantly saw the World-Honored One
Praising the Bodhisattvas;
Therefore, through day and night,
I pondered matters such as this.
Now hearing the Buddha's voice,
Expounding the Dharma according to what is appropriate,
Uncontaminated and difficult to conceive,
Leading the assembly to the place of awakening.
Originally attached to false views,
I was a master to brahmacārins;
The World-Honored One, knowing my mind,
Uprooted the false and spoke of nirvāṇa.
Completely eliminating false views,
I attained realization in the Dharma of emptiness.
At that time, I considered in my mind
That I had reached tranquil extinction;
Yet now I realize
That it was not genuine extinction.
If one can become a Buddha,
Complete with the thirty-two marks,
Revered by devas, humans, yakṣa hosts,
Nāgas, and spirits,
Only at that time can it be said
To be eternal extinction without remainder.
The Buddha amidst the great assembly
Proclaims that I will become a Buddha;
Hearing such a sound of Dharma,
Doubts and regrets are completely eliminated.
When first hearing what the Buddha spoke,
In my heart was great alarm and doubt:
Could it be that Mara is acting as the Buddha,
Disturbing and confusing my mind?
The Buddha, with various conditions,
Parables, and skillful words,
With mind serene as the ocean,
Spoke, and my net of doubts was severed.
The Buddha spoke of past eras,
Immeasurable Buddhas who entered extinction,
Dwelling peacefully in expedient means,
Who also all spoke this Dharma.
The Buddhas of the present and future,
Their numbers having no measure,
Also by various expedient means
Expound such Dharma as this.
Just as the present World-Honored One,
From birth and leaving home,
Attaining the path and turning the Dharma wheel,
Also speaks through expedient means.
The World-Honored One speaks the genuine path;
Pāpīyān has no such matter.
By this I definitively know
That it is not Mara acting as the Buddha.
Because I fell into a net of doubts,
I thought it was the work of Mara;
Hearing the Buddha's gentle sound,
Profound, far-reaching, subtle, and sublime,
Expressing the pure Dharma,
My mind rejoiced greatly.
Doubts and regrets are forever exhausted,
Dwelling peacefully in genuine wisdom.
I will definitively become a Buddha,
Revered by devas and humans,
Turning the unexcelled wheel of Dharma,
Teaching and transforming Bodhisattvas.
Then the Buddha addressed Śāriputra: I now proclaim amidst the great assembly of devas, humans, śramaṇas, and brāhmaṇas: in the past, under twenty thousand billion Buddhas, for the sake of the unexcelled path, I constantly taught and transformed you. You also, throughout the long night, followed me to receive the teachings.
Because I guided you through expedient means, you were born into my Dharma. Śāriputra! In the past, I taught you to resolve upon the Buddha path. You have now completely forgotten this, and thus claimed that you had already attained extinction. Now, wishing to cause you to recall the path of your original vow, for the sake of the Śrāvakas I expound this Great Vehicle sūtra named the Lotus Flower of the Wonderful Dharma, a teaching for Bodhisattvas and guarded in the mindfulness of the Buddhas.
Śāriputra! In a future age, after immeasurable, boundless, inconceivable kalpas have passed, having made offerings to several tens of billions of Buddhas, having upheld the correct Dharma, and having perfected the path practiced by Bodhisattvas, you will become a Buddha named Padmaprabha Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha, Vidyācaraṇasampanna, Sugata, Lokavid, Anuttara, Puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstā Devamanuṣyāṇām, Buddha, Bhagavān.
His land will be named Virajā. That land will be level and upright, pure and adorned, peaceful, abundant, and joyful, flourishing with devas and humans. Lapis lazuli will form the ground, with eight intersecting roadways bordered with gold cords. Along their sides will stand rows of trees of the seven treasures, constantly bearing flowers and fruits. Padmaprabha Tathāgata will also teach and transform wandering beings through the three vehicles.
Śāriputra! When that Buddha appears, although it will not be an evil world, because of his original vow, he will expound the Dharma of the three vehicles. His kalpa will be named Mahāratnapratimaṇḍita. For what reason will it be named Mahāratnapratimaṇḍita? Because in that land, Bodhisattvas will be regarded as the great treasures.
Those Bodhisattvas will be immeasurable, boundless, and inconceivable, beyond the reach of calculation or parables; none can know them except through the power of the Buddha's wisdom. When they wish to walk, jewel flowers will receive their feet. These Bodhisattvas will not be newly resolved; they will have long planted roots of virtue, purely cultivating Brahma conduct under immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of Buddhas, constantly praised by the Buddhas. Always cultivating the Buddha's wisdom, endowed with great spiritual superknowledges, well understanding the gates of all dharmas, upright and unfeigned, their resolve and mindfulness firm: such Bodhisattvas will fill that land.
Śāriputra! The lifespan of Padmaprabha Buddha will be twelve minor kalpas, excluding the time before becoming a Buddha while still a prince. The lifespan of the people of that land will be eight minor kalpas.
After twelve minor kalpas have passed, Padmaprabha Tathāgata will confer upon Dhṛtiparipūrṇa Bodhisattva the prophecy of anuttarasamyaksambodhi, addressing the bhikṣus: "This Dhṛtiparipūrṇa Bodhisattva will next become a Buddha, named Padmacaraṇasupratiṣṭhita Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha. His Buddha land will also be like this."
Śāriputra! After this Padmaprabha Buddha passes into extinction, the Correct Dharma will abide in the world for thirty-two minor kalpas; the Semblance Dharma will also abide in the world for thirty-two minor kalpas.
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Śāriputra in a future life
Will become a Buddha, the Universally Wise Honored One,
Named Padmaprabha,
Who will liberate immeasurable multitudes.
Making offerings to countless Buddhas,
Perfecting the Bodhisattva practices,
Endowed with the ten powers and other merits,
He will realize the unexcelled path.
After immeasurable kalpas have passed,
The kalpa named Mahāratnapratimaṇḍita,
The world named Virajā,
Pure and free of flaws and defilements.
With lapis lazuli as its ground,
Golden cords bordering its roads,
Variegated trees of the seven treasures,
Constantly bearing flowers and fruits.
The Bodhisattvas in that land,
With resolve and mindfulness ever firm,
Endowed with spiritual powers and pāramitās,
All completely fulfilled.
Under countless Buddhas,
Skillfully learning the Bodhisattva path,
Such great beings as these
Will be transformed by Padmaprabha Buddha.
When the Buddha was a prince,
Abandoning the kingdom and worldly glory,
In his final bodily existence,
He left home and attained the Buddha path.
Padmaprabha Buddha dwelling in the world
Will have a lifespan of twelve minor kalpas;
The multitude of people in that land
Will have lifespans of eight minor kalpas.
After the Buddha passes into extinction,
The Correct Dharma will abide in the world
For thirty-two minor kalpas,
Widely liberating wandering beings.
When the Correct Dharma has ended,
The Semblance Dharma will abide for thirty-two;
His relics widely distributed,
Devas and humans universally making offerings.
The deeds of Padmaprabha Buddha,
His activities will all be like this;
That holy Honored One Among Two-Legged Beings
Will be supreme and without equal.
He is none other than yourself;
You should rejoice and celebrate.
At that time, the fourfold assembly of bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, and upāsikās, along with devas, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, and mahoragas, seeing Śāriputra receive the prophecy of anuttarasamyaksambodhi before the Buddha, were filled with great joy and leaped boundlessly. Each removed their upper garments to offer them to the Buddha.
Śakra Devānām Indra, Brahmā Kings, and countless sons of devas also offered heavenly subtle garments, heavenly māndārava flowers, and mahā-māndārava flowers to the Buddha. The scattered heavenly robes remained suspended in open space, whirling about of themselves. Hundreds of thousands of kinds of heavenly instruments sounded simultaneously in open space, and a rain of heavenly flowers fell, as they said: In the past at Vārāṇasī, the Buddha first turned the wheel of Dharma; now he turns again the unexcelled, greatest wheel of Dharma!
Thereupon, the sons of devas, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
In the past at Vārāṇasī,
You turned the Dharma wheel of the Four Truths,
Distinguishing and expounding all dharmas,
The arising and ceasing of the five aggregates.
Now you turn again the most wondrous,
Unexcelled, great wheel of Dharma;
This Dharma is exceedingly profound and abstruse,
Few are able to believe it.
From the past, we
Have frequently heard the World-Honored One speak,
Yet never heard such
Profound, subtle, and supreme Dharma.
When the World-Honored One speaks this Dharma,
We all rejoice in accord.
The great wise Śāriputra
Now receives the honored prophecy;
We too are like this,
And will certainly become Buddhas.
In all the worlds,
Most honored and without superior,
The Buddha path is inconceivable,
Expounded through expedient means as appropriate.
Whatever meritorious karma we possess,
In this life or past lives,
And the merit of beholding the Buddha,
We dedicate entirely to the Buddha path.
At that time, Śāriputra addressed the Buddha: World-Honored One! I now have no further doubts or regrets, having personally received the prophecy of anuttarasamyaksambodhi before the Buddha. Yet these twelve hundred whose minds have attained mastery previously dwelt on the level of training, and the Buddha constantly taught and transformed them, saying: "My Dharma can lead away from birth, old age, sickness, and death, to ultimate nirvāṇa."
These persons in training and beyond training also each considered that having abandoned the self-view, the view of existence, the view of non-existence, and the like, they had attained nirvāṇa. Now, hearing before the World-Honored One what they had never heard before, they have all fallen into doubt and hesitation. Excellent, World-Honored One! We pray you will speak the causal conditions for the sake of the fourfold assembly, causing them to abandon doubts and regrets.
Then the Buddha addressed Śāriputra: Did I not say before: "The Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones, expound the Dharma through various causal conditions, parables, words, and expedient means, all for the sake of anuttarasamyaksambodhi"? All these teachings are for the sake of transforming Bodhisattvas.
However, Śāriputra! I will now use a parable to further clarify this meaning. Those who have wisdom attain understanding through parables.
Śāriputra! Suppose in a country, city, or village there is a great elder, advanced in years, of immeasurable wealth, possessing many fields, dwellings, and servants. His mansion is vast and extensive, with only a single gate. Many people—one hundred, two hundred, or even five hundred persons—dwell within it.
The halls and chambers are decaying and old, the walls crumbling, the bases of the pillars rotted, the beams and ridgepoles tilted and precarious. Suddenly, all at once on every side, a fire breaks out, burning the dwelling.
The sons of the elder—ten, twenty, or up to thirty—are inside this dwelling. The elder, seeing this great fire erupting from all four directions, is immediately startled and terrified, thinking: "Although I can safely escape through this burning gate, my children inside the burning house are joyfully attached to games and amusements. Unaware and uncomprehending, not startled and not terrified, the fire approaches pressing upon their bodies, sharp suffering cutting close, yet their hearts feel no dread, having no intention of seeking escape."
Śāriputra! That elder considers: "I have strength in body and hands. I could carry them out of the house in garments or upon a table."
He further considers: "This house has only a single gate, and it is narrow. The children are young and foolish, possessing no understanding. Attached to their play places, they might fall and be burned by the fire. I must speak to them of terrifying matters: this house is already burning; you should come out quickly, do not let yourselves be burned and harmed by the fire."
Having had this thought, just as he considered, he fully warns the children: "All of you, come out quickly!" Although the father pities them and uses good words to coax and instruct them, the children are joyfully attached to their games and are unwilling to believe and accept. Not startled and not afraid, they have no mind to emerge; neither do they know what fire is, what the house is, or what loss means. They merely run about playing to the east and west, looking back at their father.
Then the elder has this thought: "This house is already being burned by a great fire; if I and my children do not emerge in time, we will certainly be incinerated. I must now devise an expedient means to enable the children to escape this harm."
The father knows what each child originally favored among various precious playthings and rare curiosities, things their minds would certainly delight in. He addresses them: "The playthings you cherish are rare and difficult to find; if you do not take them, you will certainly grieve and regret it later. Such things as sheep carts, deer carts, and ox carts are now outside the gate, ready for you to play with! Come out of this burning house quickly; whatever you desire, I will give to all of you!"
Then the children, hearing their father speak of the precious playthings that suited their wishes, were each emboldened in heart. Pushing and jostling one another, competing in running, they scrambled to escape the burning house.
At that time, the elder, seeing his children safely emerge, all sitting in the open clearing at the crossroads without further obstruction, felt his heart at ease, rejoicing and leaping.
Then the children each addressed their father: "Father, the playthings you promised earlier—the sheep carts, deer carts, and ox carts—pray bestow them upon us in due time!"
Śāriputra! At that time, the elder bestowed upon each of his children a great cart. The cart was lofty and broad, adorned with many treasures, surrounded by railings, with bells hanging on all four sides. Above it was pitched a canopy, also adorned with rare and precious jewels; jewel cords were intertwined, hanging with garlands of flowers, spread with layers of fine mats, and furnished with crimson pillows.
It was yoked to a white ox of plump, clean hide, handsome form, and great muscular power, walking evenly and paced as swift as the wind, with many servants attending and guarding it.
For what reason? This great elder had immeasurable wealth, his various storehouses completely overflowing. He thought: "My riches are limitless; I should not give inferior small carts to my children. Now these youths are all my sons, loved without partiality. Having such great carts of the seven treasures, immeasurable in number, I should give them equally to each with an even mind, without making distinctions. For what reason? Even if I supplied an entire country with these possessions, they would not be depleted, how much less my own children!"
At that time, the children each rode the great carts, attaining what they had never had before, surpassing their original expectations.
Śāriputra! What do you think: when this elder gave the precious great carts equally to his children, was he guilty of falsehood or not?
Śāriputra said: No, World-Honored One! This elder merely enabled his children to escape the calamity of fire and preserved their physical lives; he was not guilty of falsehood.
Why is this? If their lives were preserved, they already gained the playthings, how much more so being rescued from that burning house through expedient means! World-Honored One! Even if this elder had not given them even the smallest cart, he still would not be guilty of falsehood.
Why is this? Because this elder originally intended: "I will use expedient means to cause my children to escape." For this reason, there is no falsehood. How much more so when the elder, knowing his wealth was limitless and wishing to benefit his children, gave them great carts equally!
The Buddha addressed Śāriputra: Excellent, excellent! It is just as you have said. Śāriputra! The Tathāgata is also like this; he is the father of all the world. Regarding all terrors, decline, afflictions, worries, and the darkness of ignorance, he has exhausted them without remainder. He has fully accomplished immeasurable knowledge and vision, powers, and fearlessnesses, possessing great spiritual power and wisdom power, fully endowed with expedient means and the pāramitā of wisdom, of great love and great compassion, constantly unwearied, ever seeking wholesome deeds to benefit all.
He is born into the decaying burning house of the three realms to liberate wandering beings from the fires of birth, old age, sickness, death, sorrow, grief, suffering, affliction, foolishness, darkness, and the three poisons, teaching and transforming them to attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi.
He sees wandering beings burned and boiled by birth, old age, sickness, death, sorrow, grief, suffering, and affliction; and because of the five desires and material gain, they undergo various sufferings. Furthermore, because of greedy attachment and seeking, they presently undergo many sufferings, and later undergo the sufferings of hells, animals, and hungry ghosts. Even if born in the heavens or amidst humans, they experience poverty, destitution, the suffering of parting from what is loved, and the suffering of encountering what is hated—various sufferings such as these.
Wandering beings are submerged in their midst, joyfully playing, unaware and uncomprehending, not startled and not afraid, giving rise to no aversion, not seeking liberation. Running east and west within the burning house of the three realms, although encountering great suffering, they do not consider it an affliction.
Śāriputra! The Buddha, having seen this, had this thought: "I am the father of wandering beings; I should rescue them from their sufferings and tribulations, and give them the joy of immeasurable, boundless Buddha wisdom with which to play."
Śāriputra! The Tathāgata further thought: "If I merely use spiritual power and wisdom power, abandoning expedient means, and praise for wandering beings the Tathāgata's knowledge and vision, powers, and fearlessnesses, wandering beings will not be able to attain liberation by this.
For what reason? These wandering beings have not yet escaped birth, old age, sickness, death, sorrow, grief, suffering, and affliction, and are burned by the burning house of the three realms; how could they understand the Buddha's wisdom?"
Śāriputra! Just as that elder, although having strength in body and hands, did not use it, but merely through earnest expedient means diligently rescued his children from the disaster of the burning house, and afterward gave each of them precious great carts:
The Tathāgata is also like this. Although possessing powers and fearlessnesses, he does not use them, but merely through wisdom and expedient means rescues wandering beings from the burning house of the three realms. He expounds for them the three vehicles—Śrāvaka, Pratyekabuddha, and Buddha Vehicle—saying:
"You must not delight in dwelling within the burning house of the three realms; do not crave crude and base sights, sounds, scents, tastes, and tangibles! If you greedily attach to them and give rise to craving, you will be burned. Come out of the three realms quickly, and you will attain the three vehicles: Śrāvaka, Pratyekabuddha, and Buddha Vehicle. I now guarantee this matter for you; it will never be false. You need only cultivate diligence and vigor."
The Tathāgata, through this expedient means, coaxes and guides wandering beings forward, further saying: "You should know that these three vehicle teachings are all praised by the sages, sovereign and unattached, free of dependence or seeking. Riding these three vehicles, by uncontaminated faculties, powers, factors of awakening, paths, dhyāna meditations, liberations, and samādhis, you will amuse yourselves and attain immeasurable peaceful security and joy."
Śāriputra! If there are wandering beings who internally possess the nature of wisdom, who hear the Dharma from the Buddha, the World-Honored One, believe and accept it, and with earnest vigor wish to quickly emerge from the three realms, seeking nirvāṇa for themselves—this is named the Śrāvaka Vehicle, like those children who, seeking sheep carts, came out of the burning house.
If there are wandering beings who hear the Dharma from the Buddha, the World-Honored One, believe and accept it, with earnest vigor seek spontaneous wisdom, delight in solitary tranquility, and deeply understand the causal conditions of all dharmas—this is named the Pratyekabuddha Vehicle, like those children who, seeking deer carts, came out of the burning house.
If there are wandering beings who hear the Dharma from the Buddha, the World-Honored One, believe and accept it, diligently cultivate vigor, seeking all-knowledge, Buddha-knowledge, spontaneous knowledge, unguided knowledge, the Tathāgata's knowledge and vision, powers, and fearlessnesses, pitying and bringing joy to immeasurable wandering beings, benefiting devas and humans, and liberating all—this is named the Great Vehicle. Because Bodhisattvas seek this vehicle, they are named Mahāsattvas, like those children who, seeking ox carts, came out of the burning house.
Śāriputra! Just as that elder, seeing his children safely emerge from the burning house and reach a place of fearlessness, reflected that his wealth was immeasurable, and bestowed great carts equally upon his children:
The Tathāgata is also like this. Being the father of all wandering beings, when he sees immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of wandering beings emerge through the Buddha's teaching gate from the suffering of the three realms, from the perilous path of terror, attaining the joy of nirvāṇa:
The Tathāgata then has this thought: "I possess the treasuries of all Buddha-dharmas—immeasurable, boundless wisdom, powers, and fearlessnesses. These wandering beings are all my children. I will give them the Great Vehicle equally; I will not allow anyone to attain extinction alone, but will liberate them all with the Tathāgata's extinction."
To all these wandering beings who escape the three realms, he gives the playthings of the Buddhas' dhyāna meditations, liberations, and the like, all of one mark and one kind, praised by the sages, able to generate pure, subtle, foremost joy.
Śāriputra! Just as that elder initially coaxed his children with three carts, and afterward gave them only great carts adorned with treasures, foremost in safety; yet that elder had no fault of falsehood:
The Tathāgata is also like this, without falsehood. First he speaks of three vehicles to guide wandering beings, and afterward liberates them solely by the Great Vehicle.
Why is this? The Tathāgata possesses the treasuries of immeasurable wisdom, powers, and fearlessnesses, able to bestow the Dharma of the Great Vehicle upon all wandering beings, though not all are able to receive it.
Śāriputra! By this causal condition, you should know that by the power of expedient means, the Buddhas distinguish and speak of three within the One Buddha Vehicle.
The Buddha, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Suppose an elder
Has a great mansion;
That mansion is long decayed
And extremely dilapidated.
The halls are high and perilous,
The bases of pillars shattered and rotted,
Beams and ridgepoles slanting,
Foundations and steps collapsing,
Walls crumbling and cracking,
Plaster peeling away,
Thatch roofs falling in disorder,
Rafters slipping out of joint,
Enclosures crooked and distorted,
Filth filling it all around.
Five hundred persons
Dwell inside it.
Kites, owls, eagles, vultures,
Crows, magpies, pigeons, doves,
Vipers, snakes, adders, scorpions,
Centipedes, millipedes,
Geckos, multipedes,
Weasels, wildcats, and mice,
All evil vermin,
Scampering crosswise in confusion.
Feces and urine stench,
Impure things overflowing,
Dung beetles and insects
Gathering upon them.
Foxes, wolves, and jackals,
Chewing and trampling,
Gnawing on corpses,
Bones and flesh scattered about.
Thereupon packs of dogs
Compete to grab and snatch,
Starved, emaciated, and alarmed,
Seeking food everywhere.
Fighting, snarling, and pulling,
Growling, snapping, and barking,
That house is terrifying,
Its altered state like this.
Everywhere there are
Sprites, goblins, specters,
Yakṣas, and evil ghosts,
Devouring human flesh.
Poisonous insects of various kinds,
And evil beasts and birds,
Brooding, giving birth,
Each hiding and protecting their young.
Yakṣas compete to come,
Struggling to seize and devour them;
Having eaten their fill,
Their evil hearts become fiercer;
The sounds of their fighting
Are exceedingly terrifying.
Kumbhāṇḍa ghosts,
Squatting on mounds of earth,
Sometimes rising above the ground
One or two feet,
Wandering back and forth,
Indulging in sport,
Seizing dogs by two feet,
Striking them till they lose their voices,
Pinning their necks with feet,
Terrifying dogs for their own amusement.
There are also ghosts,
Their bodies tall and large,
Naked, black, and emaciated,
Constantly dwelling within,
Uttering loud, harsh sounds,
Crying out in search of food.
There are also ghosts
Whose throats are like needles.
There are also ghosts
With heads like oxen,
Some devouring human flesh,
Some consuming dogs,
Their hair disheveled,
Cruel and treacherous,
Pressed by hunger and thirst,
Crying out as they run.
Yakṣas and hungry ghosts,
And evil birds and beasts,
Famished, gazing in all directions,
Peering through windows.
Perils such as these,
Terrifying beyond measure.
This decaying house
Belongs to a single man.
That man had gone out,
Not long before;
Subsequently, in the dwelling,
Suddenly a fire broke out;
On all four sides simultaneously,
Its flames flared up fiercely.
Ridgepoles, beams, rafters, and pillars,
Exploding with cracking sounds,
Shattered and falling down,
Walls collapsing.
The ghosts and spirits
Cried out loudly.
Eagles, vultures, and various birds,
Kumbhāṇḍas and the like,
Frantic and terrified,
Unable to escape.
Evil beasts and poisonous vermin
Fled into holes.
Piśāca ghosts
Also dwelled within;
Having meager merit,
Pressed by the fire,
They harmed one another,
Drinking blood and devouring flesh.
Packs of jackals
Had already died earlier;
Great evil beasts
Competed to devour them;
Foul smoke billowing,
Filled all four sides.
Centipedes and millipedes,
And poisonous snakes,
Burned by the fire,
Struggled to flee their holes;
Kumbhāṇḍa ghosts
Seized and devoured them as they appeared.
Hungry ghosts,
With fires burning on their heads,
Tormented by hunger, thirst, and heat,
Ran about in frantic distress.
That dwelling was like this,
Exceedingly terrifying;
Venomous harm and the fire disaster,
Many perils, not just one.
At that time the householder
Stood outside the gate,
Hearing someone say:
"Your children,
Earlier in play,
Entered this house;
Young and foolish,
They are merrily attached to sport."
The elder, hearing this,
Startled, entered the burning house,
Seeking to rescue them in time,
Lest they be burned and harmed.
He warned the children,
Speaking of the many tribulations:
Evil ghosts and poisonous vermin,
The spread of the disastrous fire,
Various sufferings following one another,
Continuing without break;
Vipers, snakes, adders,
And yakṣas,
Kumbhāṇḍa ghosts,
Jackals, foxes, dogs,
Eagles, vultures, kites, owls,
And centipedes,
Distressed by hunger and thirst,
Exceedingly terrifying.
These sufferings are hard to endure,
How much more so the great fire!
The foolish children,
Though hearing their father's admonition,
Still joyfully attached,
Played on without ceasing.
At that time the elder
Had this thought:
"The children being like this
Increases my grief and sorrow.
Now in this dwelling,
There is not a single thing to delight in,
Yet the children,
Submerged in play,
Do not accept my teaching,
And will be harmed by the fire."
Thereupon he considered,
Devising expedient means,
Addressing the children:
"I have various
Rare playthings,
Wondrous, precious fine carts:
Sheep carts, deer carts,
And great ox carts,
Now outside the gate.
Come out,
And I, for your sake,
Have made these carts;
According to your desire,
You can play with them."
The children, hearing him speak
Of such carts as these,
Immediately scrambled,
Running to come out,
Reaching the open ground,
Free of all sufferings and tribulations.
The elder, seeing his children
Escape the burning house,
Dwelling at the crossroads,
Sat on a lion seat
And rejoiced within himself:
"I am now joyful!
These children,
Raised with great difficulty,
Foolish, young, and without understanding,
Entered the perilous house.
Full of poisonous vermin,
Terrifying sprites,
Fierce flames of great fire,
Simultaneously rising on all sides;
Yet these children
Greedily delighted in play.
I have rescued them,
Enabling them to escape disaster.
Therefore, all of you,
I am now joyful."
At that time the children,
Knowing their father was seated peacefully,
All went to where the father was,
Addressing him:
"Pray bestow upon us
The three precious carts,
As promised before:
'If you come out,
I will give you three carts
According to your desires.'
Now is precisely the time;
Pray deign to give them to us."
The elder was very wealthy,
With many storehouses,
Gold, silver, lapis lazuli,
Mother-of-pearl, and agate;
With these various treasures,
He made great carts,
Adorned and decorated,
Surrounded with railings,
Bells hung on all four sides,
Gold cords intertwined,
Nets of pearls
Spread above them,
Golden flower tassels
Hanging everywhere,
Variegated adornments
Surrounding them all around,
Soft silk and cotton
Serving as cushions,
Superior, fine textiles,
Worth thousands of billions,
Pure and clean white,
Covering them above.
There were great white oxen,
Fat, robust, and strong,
Of handsome form,
Yoked to the precious carts,
With many attendants
Guarding them.
With these wonderful carts,
He gifted his children equally.
The children at that time
Rejoiced and leaped,
Riding these precious carts,
Traveling in the four directions,
Playing and joyful,
Sovereign and unobstructed.
I tell you, Śāriputra:
I too am like this,
Honored Among Sages,
The Father of the World.
All wandering beings
Are my children;
Deeply attached to worldly pleasures,
They lack wise minds.
The three realms have no peace,
Just like a burning house,
Filled with many sufferings,
Exceedingly terrifying.
Constantly there are birth, old age,
Sickness, death, grief, and worry;
Fires such as these
Blaze without ceasing.
The Tathāgata has already departed
The burning house of the three realms,
Dwelling quietly in leisure,
At peace in forest wilds.
Now these three realms
All belong to me;
The wandering beings within them
Are all my children.
Yet now this place
Has many afflictions;
I alone
Am able to rescue and protect them.
Although I teach and instruct them,
They do not believe and accept,
Because of passions and defilements,
Their greedy attachment being deep.
By this expedient means,
I expound the three vehicles for them,
Causing wandering beings
To know the suffering of the three realms,
Opening, revealing, and expounding
The supramundane path.
If these children
Have determined minds,
Endowed with the three knowledges
And the six spiritual superknowledges,
Some attain Conditioned Awakening,
Or irreversible Bodhisattvahood.
You, Śāriputra!
For the sake of wandering beings,
Through this parable,
I speak of the One Buddha Vehicle.
If you are able to
Believe and accept these words,
All of you will
Attain the Buddha path.
This vehicle is subtle, sublime,
Pure, and foremost;
In all the worlds,
It is unexcelled,
Delighted in by the Buddhas.
All wandering beings
Should praise it,
Make offerings, and bow in homage.
Immeasurable thousands of billions
Of powers, liberations,
Dhyāna meditations, and wisdom,
And other dharmas of the Buddha,
Are attained through such a vehicle as this.
Causing the children,
Day, night, and throughout kalpas,
Constantly to play with it,
With Bodhisattvas
And the Śrāvaka assembly,
Riding this precious vehicle,
Directly reaching the place of awakening.
By this causal condition,
Searching carefully in the ten directions,
There are no other vehicles,
Except for the Buddha's expedient means.
I address Śāriputra:
All of you
Are my children,
And I am your father.
Through successive kalpas,
Burned by many sufferings,
I have rescued you all,
Leading you out of the three realms.
Although I previously said:
"You have attained extinction,"
It was only ending birth and death,
Not genuine extinction;
What should now be practiced
Is solely the Buddha's wisdom.
If there are Bodhisattvas
Amidst this assembly,
Who can single-mindedly listen
To the genuine Dharma of the Buddhas:
The Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones,
Though using expedient means,
The wandering beings they transform
Are all Bodhisattvas.
If people of small wisdom
Are deeply attached to love and desire,
For their sake,
I expound the Truth of Suffering.
Wandering beings rejoice in their hearts,
Attaining what they never had before;
The Truth of Suffering spoken by the Buddha
Is genuine, real, and without difference.
If there are wandering beings
Who do not know the root of suffering,
Deeply attached to the causes of suffering,
Unable to abandon them for an instant,
For their sake,
I expound the path through expedient means.
The cause of all sufferings
Has greedy desire as its root;
If greedy desire is extinguished,
It has no support;
Extinguishing all sufferings
Is named the Third Truth.
For the sake of the Truth of Cessation,
One cultivates the path;
Free from the bonds of suffering,
It is named attaining liberation.
From what
Has this person attained liberation?
Merely departing from falsehood
Is named liberation;
In truth they have not yet attained
Complete liberation.
The Buddha states that this person
Has not truly attained extinction.
Because this person has not attained
The unexcelled path,
I do not intend
To let them reach extinction.
I am the King of Dharma,
At ease in the Dharma;
To bring peace to wandering beings,
I appear in the world.
You, Śāriputra!
This Dharma seal of mine
Is spoken to benefit
The world;
Wherever you travel,
Do not recklessly proclaim it.
If there are those who hear it,
Rejoice and receive it on their crowns,
You should know that such persons
Are avaivartika.
If there are those who believe and accept
The Dharma of this sūtra,
These persons have already
Seen past Buddhas,
Reverently made offerings,
And also heard this Dharma.
If a person is able to
Believe what you speak,
They have seen me,
And have also seen you,
And the Bhikṣu-Saṅgha,
And the Bodhisattvas.
This Lotus Sūtra
Is spoken for those of deep wisdom.
Those of shallow understanding hearing it
Are deluded and do not comprehend.
All Śrāvakas
And Pratyekabuddhas,
In this sūtra,
Their power cannot reach it.
You, Śāriputra!
Even in this sūtra,
You enter through faith;
How much more so other Śrāvakas!
The other Śrāvakas,
Because of believing the Buddha's words,
Follow this sūtra,
Not through the share of their own wisdom.
Furthermore, Śāriputra!
To those of arrogance, laziness,
And those calculating a self-view,
Do not speak this sūtra.
Common mortals of shallow understanding,
Deeply attached to the five desires,
Hearing it, cannot understand;
Do not speak it to them either.
If a person does not believe
And slanders this sūtra,
They sever all
Worldly Buddha-seeds.
Or if they frown
And harbor doubts,
You should listen
To the retributions of this person's transgressions.
Whether while the Buddha is in the world
Or after his extinction,
If there are those who slander
Such a sūtra as this,
Or seeing those who read, recite,
Write, and uphold this sūtra,
Despise, hate, and envy them,
Harboring grudges:
The retributions of this person's transgressions,
Listen to them now.
When that person's life ends,
They will enter the Avīci Hell,
Fulfilling a full kalpa;
When the kalpa ends, they are reborn there again.
Passing through successive cycles like this
For countless kalpas,
Emerging from hell,
They will fall among animals.
If as dogs or jackals,
Their forms emaciated,
Dark, scabby, and leprous,
Harassed by people.
Furthermore, by people
Despised and hated,
Constantly afflicted by hunger and thirst,
Bones and flesh withered,
Undergoing flogging in life,
Stoned with tiles and rocks in death.
Because of severing the Buddha-seeds,
They receive such retributions for their transgressions.
If born as camels,
Or born among donkeys,
Their bodies constantly bearing heavy burdens,
Subjected to sticks and whips,
Thinking only of water and grass,
Knowing nothing else.
Because of slandering this sūtra,
They obtain transgressions like this.
Some become jackals,
Coming into villages,
Their bodies scabby and leprous,
Lacking an eye.
By young boys
Beaten and pelted,
Undergoing many sufferings,
Sometimes meeting death.
Dying from this,
They then receive the body of a python,
Its form long and large,
Five hundred yojanas,
Deaf, dull, and without feet,
Wriggling on its belly,
Devoured by small vermin,
Day and night undergoing suffering,
Having no respite.
Because of slandering this sūtra,
They obtain transgressions like this.
If they attain human birth,
Their faculties are dull and dark,
Dwarfish, ugly, crippled, lame,
Blind, deaf, and hunchbacked.
Whatever they say,
People do not believe or accept;
Their breath constantly foul,
Possessed by ghosts and sprites.
Poor and lowly,
Employed by others,
Beset with many sicknesses, emaciated,
Having none to rely upon.
Although they attach themselves to others,
Others pay them no heed;
If they obtain anything,
They quickly forget and lose it.
If they study the art of medicine,
Treating illnesses according to formulas,
They only worsen other people's diseases,
Or even cause death.
If they fall ill themselves,
None come to rescue or treat them;
Even if taking good medicine,
It only worsens the condition.
If others rebel,
Rob, raid, or steal,
For such transgressions as these,
They wrongly incur the blame.
Such transgressors as these
Never see the Buddha,
The King of all Sages,
Expounding the Dharma to teach and transform.
Such transgressors as these
Are constantly born in difficult realms,
Mad, deaf, their minds confused,
Never hearing the Dharma.
Throughout countless kalpas
Like Ganges sands,
Born deaf and mute,
Their faculties incomplete.
Constantly dwelling in hells,
As if roaming in gardens;
In other evil destinies,
As if in their own homes;
Camels, donkeys, pigs, and dogs
Are their walking places.
Because of slandering this sūtra,
They obtain transgressions like this.
If they attain human birth,
Deaf, blind, and mute,
Poverty and all declines
Adorning themselves.
Dropsy, consumption,
Scabs, leprosy, and boils,
Such illnesses as these
Serving as their garments.
Their bodies constantly stinking,
Filthy and impure,
Deeply attached to the self-view,
Increasing their anger,
Their lust blazing,
Not discriminating even animals.
Because of slandering this sūtra,
They obtain transgressions like this.
I tell you, Śāriputra:
Those who slander this sūtra,
If I were to speak of their transgressions,
An entire kalpa would not exhaust them.
By this causal condition,
Therefore I say to you:
"Among persons lacking wisdom,
Do not speak this sūtra."
If there are those of sharp faculties,
Whose wisdom is clear,
Who are learned and have strong memory,
Seeking the Buddha path—
To such persons as these,
You may speak it.
If a person has seen
Hundreds of thousands of millions of Buddhas,
Planting roots of virtue,
Their deep minds firm—
To such persons as these,
You may speak it.
If a person is vigorous,
Constantly cultivating the mind of compassion,
Not begrudging body or life,
You may speak it.
If a person is reverent,
Without duplicity of mind,
Free from common fools,
Dwelling alone in mountains and marshes—
To such persons as these,
You may speak it.
Furthermore, Śāriputra!
If you see someone
Abandoning bad friends,
Drawing close to good friends—
To such persons as these,
You may speak it.
If you see a Buddha-child
Upholding pure precepts,
Like a pure bright jewel,
Seeking the Great Vehicle sūtras—
To such persons as these,
You may speak it.
If a person is free of anger,
Upright, direct, and gentle,
Constantly pitying all,
Reverencing all Buddhas—
To such persons as these,
You may speak it.
Furthermore, if there are Buddha-children
Amidst the great assembly,
Who with pure minds,
Through various conditions,
Parables, and words,
Expound the Dharma without hindrance—
To such persons as these,
You may speak it.
If there are bhikṣus
Who, for the sake of all-knowledge,
Seek the Dharma in the four directions,
Joining palms and receiving it on their crowns,
Only delighting in receiving and upholding
The Great Vehicle scriptures,
Not even receiving
A single verse of other sūtras—
To such persons as these,
You may speak it.
Like a person who with utmost mind
Seeks the Buddha's relics,
Seeking this sūtra in the same way,
Receiving it upon the crown when obtained,
That person no longer
Aspires to seek other sūtras,
Nor ever contemplates
The scriptures of non-Buddhists—
To such persons as these,
You may speak it.
I tell you, Śāriputra:
The marks of those who
Seek the Buddha path like this,
A kalpa would not exhaust describing them.
Persons such as these
Are able to believe and understand;
You should expound for them
The Sūtra of the Lotus of the Wonderful Dharma.
