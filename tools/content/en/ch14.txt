At that time, Mañjuśrī, Prince of the Dharma, Bodhisattva Mahāsattva, addressed the Buddha: "World-Honored One! These Bodhisattvas are exceedingly rare. Reverently obeying the Buddha, they have made great vows: in the future evil age, to protect, uphold, read, and expound this Lotus Sūtra. World-Honored One! How can Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas expound this scripture in the future evil age?"
The Buddha addressed Mañjuśrī: "If Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas wish to expound this scripture in the future evil age, they should abide peacefully in four dharmas. First, abiding peacefully in the Bodhisattva's sphere of practice and sphere of association, they will be able to expound this scripture for wandering beings.
Mañjuśrī! What is named the Bodhisattva Mahāsattva's sphere of practice? If a Bodhisattva Mahāsattva dwells on the ground of patience, gentle and compliant, not rash or violent, his mind not alarmed; furthermore, having no practice regarding dharmas, contemplating the genuine mark of all dharmas as they are, neither practicing nor discriminating—this is named the Bodhisattva Mahāsattva's sphere of practice.
What is named the Bodhisattva Mahāsattva's sphere of association? A Bodhisattva Mahāsattva does not draw close to kings, princes, ministers, or senior officials; he does not draw close to non-Buddhists, brahmacārins, Nirgranthas, and the like, nor to those who compose worldly literature, sing poems, or recite non-Buddhist books, nor to Lokāyatas and anti-Lokāyatas. He does not draw close to dangerous games, mutual boxing, wrestling, or the various illusionary entertainments of acrobats.
He does not draw close to caṇḍālas, nor to those who raise pigs, sheep, chickens, and dogs, or who hunt, fish, or engage in evil livelihoods. If such persons come at times, he expounds the Dharma for them without expectation.
He does not draw close to bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, or upāsikās who seek to be Śrāvakas, nor does he inquire after them. Whether in rooms, walkways, or lecture halls, he does not dwell together with them. If they come at times, he expounds the Dharma according to what is appropriate, without seeking anything.
Mañjuśrī! Furthermore, a Bodhisattva Mahāsattva should not, regarding the bodies of women, seize upon marks that give rise to lustful thoughts to expound the Dharma for them, nor does he delight in looking upon them. If entering the homes of others, he does not speak with young girls, virgins, or widows. He does not draw close to the five kinds of unmanly persons to make them friends. He does not enter the homes of others alone; if due to circumstances he must enter alone, he is solely and single-mindedly mindful of the Buddha.
If expounding the Dharma for women, he does not show his teeth in laughter, nor bare his chest. Even for the sake of Dharma he does not become intimate, how much less for other matters. He does not delight in keeping young disciples, śrāmaṇeras, or children, nor does he delight in sharing the same master with them. He constantly favors seated meditation, dwelling in leisure places to cultivate and collect his mind. Mañjuśrī! This is named the first sphere of association.
Furthermore, a Bodhisattva Mahāsattva contemplates all dharmas as empty, like the genuine mark: unperverted, unmoving, non-regressing, non-turning, like empty space, without inherent nature. All paths of language are severed; unborn, non-emerging, non-arising, nameless, signless, truly non-existent, immeasurable, boundless, unobstructed, unhindered, existing solely through causal conditions, spoken of because born from perversion. Constantly delighting in contemplating such marks of dharmas—this is named the Bodhisattva Mahāsattva's second sphere of association."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
If there are Bodhisattvas,
In the future evil world,
Without fear in mind,
Wishing to expound this scripture,
They should enter the sphere of practice,
And the sphere of association.
Constantly staying far from kings,
Princes of nations,
Ministers and senior officials,
Those engaged in dangerous games,
Caṇḍālas,
And non-Buddhist brahmacārins.
Neither should they draw close
To those of arrogant pride,
Who are greedily attached to the Small Vehicle,
Scholars of the Three Baskets,
Precept-breaking bhikṣus,
Nominal arhats,
And bhikṣuṇīs
Who are fond of frivolous laughter;
Those deeply attached to the five desires,
Who seek present extinction,
And all upāsikās—
Do not draw close to any of them.
If such persons as these,
Come with wholesome minds
To where the Bodhisattva dwells,
To hear the Buddha path,
The Bodhisattva then,
With a fearless mind,
Without harboring expectations,
Expounds the Dharma for them.
Widows, virgins,
And all unmanly persons,
Do not draw close to them,
To make them intimate friends.
Neither draw close to
Butchers, executioners,
Hunters, and fishermen,
Who kill for profit,
Or sell meat to sustain themselves,
Or peddle female charms;
Persons such as these,
Do not draw close to them.
Dangerous wrestling,
Various entertainments,
And prostitutes—
Do not draw close to any of them.
Do not in a secluded, screened place,
Expound the Dharma for women;
When expounding the Dharma,
Do not jest or laugh.
When entering a village to beg for food,
Take another bhikṣu along;
If there is no other bhikṣu,
Single-mindedly be mindful of the Buddha.
These are named
The sphere of practice and sphere of association;
Relying on these two spheres,
One can expound in peace and joy.
Furthermore, do not practice
Superior, middling, or inferior dharmas,
Conditioned or unconditioned,
Real or unreal dharmas;
Neither discriminate
Whether one is male or female.
Not apprehending all dharmas,
Not knowing and not seeing—
This is named
The Bodhisattva's sphere of practice.
All dharmas
Are empty and non-existent,
Without permanence,
And without arising or ceasing;
This is named
The wise person's sphere of association.
Perverted discriminations
Of dharmas as existent or non-existent,
Real or unreal,
Arising or non-arising.
Dwelling in a leisure place,
Cultivating and collecting the mind,
Peacefully abiding unmoving,
Like Mount Sumeru.
Contemplating all dharmas
As having no existence,
Just like empty space,
Without solidity.
Unborn, non-emerging,
Unmoving, non-regressing,
Constantly abiding in one mark;
This is named the sphere of association.
If there are bhikṣus,
After my extinction,
Who enter this sphere of practice
And sphere of association,
When expounding this scripture,
They will have no timidity.
When Bodhisattvas at times
Enter a quiet chamber,
With proper mindfulness,
Contemplating dharmas according to their meaning,
Arising from dhyāna meditation,
For the sake of kings,
Princes, ministers, citizens,
And brāhmaṇas,
Opening, transforming, and proclaiming,
Expounding this scripture,
Their minds will be peaceful and secure,
Free of all timidity.
Mañjuśrī!
This is named the Bodhisattva
Abiding peacefully in the first dharma,
Able in future ages
To expound the Lotus Sūtra.
"Furthermore, Mañjuśrī! After the Tathāgata's extinction, in the Final Dharma age, if one wishes to expound this scripture, one should abide in peaceful and joyful practice. When speaking with the mouth or reading the scripture, one should not delight in speaking of the faults of persons or scriptures. Neither should one slight other Dharma teachers, nor speak of other people's virtues or vices, strengths or weaknesses.
Regarding Śrāvakas, one should not name them to speak of their errors and evils, nor name them to praise their virtues, nor give rise to thoughts of resentment and dislike. Because one skillfully cultivates such a peaceful and joyful mind, listeners will not oppose one's intent; if questioned on difficult points, one does not answer with the Small Vehicle Dharma, but solely through the Great Vehicle explains it for them, causing them to attain all-knowledge."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
The Bodhisattva constantly delights
In expounding the Dharma in peace and security;
Upon a pure ground,
He sets out a seat.
Anointing his body with oil,
Bathing away dirt and grime,
Putting on fresh, clean robes,
Clean both inside and out,
Peacefully seated upon the Dharma seat,
He speaks according to what is asked.
If there are bhikṣus,
Bhikṣuṇīs,
Upāsakas,
Upāsikās,
Kings, princes,
Ministers, and citizens,
With subtle, profound meaning,
He speaks with a gentle countenance.
If there are difficult questions,
He answers according to the meaning,
With causal conditions and parables,
Setting forth distinctions.
Through this expedient means,
He causes them all to generate the resolve,
Gradually increasing,
Entering the Buddha path.
Eliminating lazy thoughts,
And notions of negligence,
Free from all sorrow and affliction,
Expounding the Dharma with a compassionate mind.
Day and night constantly expounding
The teachings of the unexcelled path;
With various causal conditions,
And immeasurable parables,
Opening and revealing to wandering beings,
Causing them all to rejoice.
Clothing, bedding,
Food, drink, and medicine—
Regarding these,
He has no expectation.
Solely with single mind,
Mindful of the causal conditions of expounding the Dharma,
Vowing to accomplish the Buddha path,
And cause the assembly to do likewise;
This is great benefit,
An offering of peace and joy.
After my extinction,
If there are bhikṣus,
Able to expound this
Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma,
Their minds free of jealousy and anger,
And all hindrances of affliction,
Free also of sorrow,
And of revilers,
And without fear
Of knives and sticks,
Never expelled,
Because abiding in patience.
The wise person being like this,
Skillfully cultivating his mind,
Can abide in peace and joy,
As I have spoken above.
The merits of this person,
Throughout thousands of billions of kalpas,
Beyond calculation and parables,
Cannot be fully told.
"Furthermore, Mañjuśrī! Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas, in the future Final Dharma age when the Dharma is about to perish, who receive, uphold, read, and recite this scripture, should harbor no mind of envy, flattery, or deceit, nor slight and revile those who study the Buddha path, seeking their strengths and weaknesses.
If there are bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, or upāsikās who seek to be Śrāvakas, who seek to be Pratyekabuddhas, or who seek the Bodhisattva path, one must not distress them, causing them doubt and regret by saying to them: 'You are very far from the path; you will never attain all-knowledge. For what reason? You are negligent persons, indolent regarding the path.'
Furthermore, one should not engage in discursive play regarding dharmas, nor engage in contention. Toward all wandering beings, one should give rise to the thought of great compassion; toward all Tathāgatas, give rise to the thought of a loving father; toward all Bodhisattvas, give rise to the thought of great teachers; toward the great Bodhisattvas of the ten directions, one should constantly bow and pay homage with deep reverence. Toward all wandering beings, one should expound the Dharma equally, in accordance with the Dharma, neither too much nor too little; even toward those who deeply love the Dharma, one does not speak too much.
Mañjuśrī! This Bodhisattva Mahāsattva, in the future Final Dharma age when the Dharma is about to perish, who accomplishes this third peaceful practice: when he expounds this Dharma, none will be able to disturb or confuse him; he will attain good fellow students to read and recite this scripture with him, and will also attain great assemblies who come to listen and receive it; having heard, they will be able to uphold it; having upheld, they will be able to recite it; having recited, they will be able to expound it; having expounded, they will be able to copy it or cause others to copy it, making offerings to the scriptural scrolls, revering, honoring, and praising them."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
If one wishes to expound this scripture,
One should abandon jealousy, anger, pride,
Flattery, deceit, falsehood, and hypocrisy,
Constantly cultivating upright conduct.
Do not look down upon people,
Nor engage in discursive play regarding dharmas;
Do not cause others doubt and regret,
Saying: "You will not become Buddhas."
When this Buddha-child expounds the Dharma,
He is constantly gentle and able to endure,
Compassionate toward all,
Not giving rise to indolence in mind.
Great Bodhisattvas of the ten directions,
Who practice the path out of pity for beings,
One should generate a reverent mind toward them:
"These are my great masters."
Toward the Buddhas, the World-Honored Ones,
Give rise to the thought of unexcelled fathers;
Shattering minds of haughty pride,
Expounding the Dharma without hindrance.
The third dharma is like this;
The wise person should guard it.
Single-mindedly in peaceful practice,
He is revered by immeasurable multitudes.
"Furthermore, Mañjuśrī! Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas, in the future Final Dharma age when the Dharma is about to perish, who uphold this Lotus Sūtra, should give rise to a mind of great love toward those at home and those who have left home, and give rise to a mind of great compassion toward those who are not Bodhisattvas, thinking:
'Such persons suffer a great loss. The Tathāgata, through expedient means, expounds the Dharma according to what is appropriate, yet they do not hear, do not know, do not perceive, do not ask, do not believe, and do not understand. Although these persons do not ask, do not believe, and do not understand this scripture, when I attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi, wherever they may be, through spiritual power and wisdom power I will guide them, causing them to abide in this Dharma.'
Mañjuśrī! This Bodhisattva Mahāsattva, after the Tathāgata's extinction, who accomplishes this fourth dharma: when he expounds this Dharma, he will be free of faults, and will constantly receive offerings, reverence, honor, and praise from bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, upāsikās, kings, princes, ministers, citizens, brāhmaṇas, and householders. The devas of open space, for the sake of hearing the Dharma, will also constantly follow and attend upon him.
If he is in villages, cities, or leisure forests, and people come wishing to pose difficult questions, the devas day and night will constantly guard and protect him for the sake of the Dharma, causing all listeners to attain joy. For what reason? Because this scripture is protected by the spiritual power of all Buddhas of the past, future, and present. Mañjuśrī! In immeasurable lands, one cannot even hear the name of this Lotus Sūtra, how much less behold, receive, uphold, read, and recite it!
Mañjuśrī! Suppose a powerful wheel-turning sage king wishes to subdue various lands by force, but petty kings do not obey his commands. The wheel-turning king mobilizes various troops to punish them. The king, seeing those among his soldiers who have achieved merit in battle, rejoices greatly and bestows rewards according to their merit: giving fields, houses, villages, and cities; or giving garments and personal ornaments; or giving various treasures: gold, silver, lapis lazuli, mother-of-pearl, agate, coral, amber, elephants, horses, carriages, and servants. Only the bright jewel in his topknot does he not give away.
For what reason? Only on the crown of the king's head is there this single jewel; if he were to give it away, the king's attendants would certainly be greatly startled and amazed.
Mañjuśrī! The Tathāgata is also like this. Through the power of dhyāna meditation and wisdom, he attains the Dharma realm and reigns over the three realms, yet the Mara kings refuse to submit. The Tathāgata's wise and holy generals battle with them; when they achieve merit, his heart also rejoices.
Amidst the fourfold assembly, he expounds various scriptures for them, causing their hearts to delight, bestowing upon them dhyāna meditation, liberation, uncontaminated faculties and powers, and the wealth of all dharmas; he also bestows upon them the city of nirvāṇa, telling them they have attained extinction, guiding their minds and causing them all to rejoice; yet he does not expound this Lotus Sūtra for them.
Mañjuśrī! Just as that wheel-turning king, seeing those among his troops who have achieved great merit, rejoices exceedingly, and with this jewel difficult to believe, which has long resided in his topknot and was never recklessly given to anyone, now gives it away:
The Tathāgata is also like this. Within the three realms, he is the Great King of Dharma, teaching and transforming all wandering beings through the Dharma. Seeing the army of the wise and holy battle with the skandha-maras, affliction-maras, and death-maras, achieving great merit, extinguishing the three poisons, escaping the three realms, and shattering the net of Maras, the Tathāgata also rejoices greatly.
This Lotus Sūtra, able to cause wandering beings to arrive at all-knowledge, is resented and difficult to believe in all the world; not having been spoken before, he now expounds it. Mañjuśrī! This Lotus Sūtra is the foremost teaching of the Tathāgatas, the most profound among all teachings, bestowed at the very end, just like that powerful king who long guarded the bright jewel, and now finally bestows it.
Mañjuśrī! This Lotus Sūtra is the secret treasury of all Buddhas, the Tathāgatas, standing supreme among all scriptures. Guarded through the long night, it has not been recklessly proclaimed; only beginning today is it set forth and expounded for all of you."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Constantly practicing patience,
Pitying all beings,
Only then can one proclaim
The scripture praised by the Buddhas.
In the future Final Dharma age,
Those who uphold this scripture,
Toward those at home, those who have left home,
And those who are not Bodhisattvas,
Should give rise to love and compassion:
"These who do not hear,
And do not believe this scripture,
Suffer a great loss.
When I attain the Buddha path,
Through various expedient means,
I will expound this Dharma for them,
Causing them to abide within it."
Just like a powerful
Wheel-turning king,
When troops have merit in battle,
Bestowing various gifts:
Elephants, horses, carriages,
Personal ornaments,
Fields, houses,
Villages, and cities;
Or giving clothing,
Various treasures,
Servants, and wealth,
Joyfully bestowing them.
If there is a heroic warrior,
Able to perform difficult feats,
The king unties from his topknot
The bright jewel to bestow upon him.
The Tathāgata is also like this;
As King of all dharmas,
With the great power of patience,
And treasury of wisdom,
Through great compassion,
Transforms the world according to Dharma.
Seeing all humans
Undergoing various sufferings,
Wishing to seek liberation,
Battling with the Maras.
For the sake of these wandering beings,
He speaks various dharmas;
Through great expedient means,
Expounding these scriptures.
Having known that wandering beings
Have attained their strength,
At the very end,
He expounds this Lotus for them,
Just as the king unties his topknot,
To bestow the bright jewel.
This scripture is supreme,
Foremost among all scriptures;
I have constantly guarded it,
Not recklessly revealing it;
Now is precisely the time
To expound it for you all.
After my extinction,
Those who seek the Buddha path,
Wishing to attain peace and security
To proclaim this scripture,
Should draw close to
Such four dharmas as these.
Those who read this scripture
Will constantly be free of sorrow and affliction,
Free also of illness and pain,
Their complexions fresh and bright;
Not born into poverty,
Baseness, or ugliness.
Wandering beings will delight in seeing them,
Admiring them as worthy sages;
Sons of devas
Will serve as their attendants.
Knives and sticks will not strike them,
Poisons cannot harm them;
If someone reviles them,
Their mouths will be shut.
They will travel without fear,
Like lion kings;
The light of their wisdom,
Like the shining of the sun.
If in dreams,
They will see only wonderful matters:
Beholding the Tathāgatas,
Seated upon lion seats,
Surrounded by assemblies of bhikṣus,
Expounding the Dharma.
Also seeing Nagas, spirits,
And asuras,
Equal to the sands of the Ganges,
Reverently joining palms;
Seeing their own bodies,
Expounding the Dharma for them.
Again beholding the Buddhas,
Their bodies golden in color,
Emitting immeasurable light,
Illuminating all,
With Brahma-sounds,
Expounding all dharmas.
The Buddha expounds the unexcelled Dharma
For the fourfold assembly;
They see themselves in their midst,
Joining palms and praising the Buddha,
Hearing the Dharma with joy,
Making offerings,
Attaining dhāraṇī,
Realizing irreversible wisdom.
The Buddha, knowing their minds
Have entered deeply into the Buddha path,
Immediately bestows a prophecy
Of accomplishing the most right awakening:
"You, good son!
In a future age,
Will attain immeasurable wisdom,
The great path of the Buddha.
Your land will be adorned and pure,
Vast and incomparable;
There will also be a fourfold assembly,
Joining palms to listen to the Dharma."
They also see themselves
In mountain forests,
Cultivating wholesome dharmas,
Realizing the genuine mark,
Deeply entering dhyāna meditation,
Beholding the Buddhas of the ten directions.
The bodies of the Buddhas are golden,
Adorned with marks of a hundred merits;
Hearing the Dharma and speaking it to others,
They constantly have such good dreams.
Again dreaming of being a king,
Abandoning palaces and attendants,
And superior pleasures of the five desires,
Going to the place of awakening.
Beneath the Bodhi tree,
Seated upon a lion seat,
Seeking the path for seven days,
Attaining the wisdom of the Buddhas.
Having accomplished the unexcelled path,
Arising to turn the wheel of Dharma,
Expounding the Dharma for the fourfold assembly
Throughout thousands of billions of kalpas,
Expounding the uncontaminated, wondrous Dharma,
Liberating immeasurable wandering beings.
Afterward entering nirvāṇa,
As smoke vanishes and a lamp goes out.
If in the future evil world,
One expounds this foremost Dharma,
That person will attain great benefit,
Such virtues and merits as spoken above.
