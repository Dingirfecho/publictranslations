At that time, when the great assembly heard the Buddha speak of his lifespan and kalpas being long and distant like this, immeasurable, boundless, incalculable wandering beings attained great benefit.
Then the World-Honored One addressed Maitreya Bodhisattva Mahāsattva: "Ajita! When I spoke of this Tathāgata's lifespan being long and distant, wandering beings numbering six hundred and eighty thousand billion inconceivable numbers of Ganges sands attained the forbearance of non-arising dharmas;
Furthermore, a thousand times more Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas attained the dhāraṇī door of hearing and upholding;
Furthermore, Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas equal to the dust motes of a single world attained joyful and unhindered eloquence;
Furthermore, Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas equal to the dust motes of a single world attained hundreds of thousands of millions of immeasurable rotating dhāraṇīs;
Furthermore, Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas equal to the dust motes of a great three-thousand-fold worlds became able to turn the irreversible wheel of Dharma;
Furthermore, Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas equal to the dust motes of two thousand middle universes became able to turn the pure wheel of Dharma;
Furthermore, Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas equal to the dust motes of a small universe will attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi after eight rebirths;
Furthermore, Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas equal to the dust motes of four four-continents will attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi after four rebirths;
Furthermore, Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas equal to the dust motes of three four-continents will attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi after three rebirths;
Furthermore, Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas equal to the dust motes of two four-continents will attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi after two rebirths;
Furthermore, Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas equal to the dust motes of one four-continent will attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi after a single rebirth;
Furthermore, wandering beings equal to the dust motes of eight worlds all generated the mind of anuttarasamyaksambodhi."
When the Buddha spoke of these Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas attaining great Dharma benefits, from open space rained down māndārava flowers and mahā-māndārava flowers, scattering over the Buddhas seated upon lion seats beneath immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of jewel trees, and scattering over Śākyamuni Buddha and Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata, who had long passed into extinction, seated upon lion seats within the seven-jeweled stūpa, and also scattering over all the great Bodhisattvas and the fourfold assembly.
There also rained fine powdered sandalwood and aloeswood incense. In open space, heavenly drums naturally sounded, their wondrous tones deep and far-reaching. There also rained a thousand kinds of heavenly garments, draped with necklaces of pearls, mani jewels, and wish-fulfilling jewels, pervading the nine directions. Censers of various jewels burned priceless incense, naturally pervading all around, making offerings to the great assembly.
Above each Buddha stood Bodhisattvas holding banners and canopies, ascending successively up to the Brahmā Heaven. These Bodhisattvas, with wondrous voices, sang immeasurable hymns praising the Buddhas.
Then Maitreya Bodhisattva arose from his seat, bared his right shoulder, joined his palms toward the Buddha, and spoke gāthās:
The Buddha speaks a rare Dharma,
Never heard in the past;
The World-Honored One has great power,
His lifespan beyond measure.
Countless Buddha-children,
Hearing the World-Honored One distinguish
And speak of attaining Dharma benefits,
Have joy pervading their bodies.
Some dwell on the ground of non-retrogression,
Some attain dhāraṇīs,
Some unhindered joyful eloquence,
Tens of billions of rotating total retentions.
Or Bodhisattvas equal to the dust motes
Of a great three-thousand-fold worlds,
Each able to turn
The irreversible wheel of Dharma.
Furthermore, Bodhisattvas equal to the dust motes
Of a middle universe,
Each able to turn
The pure wheel of Dharma.
Furthermore, Bodhisattvas equal to the dust motes
Of a small universe,
With eight rebirths remaining,
Will accomplish the Buddha path.
Furthermore, four, three, or two,
Such four-continents,
Bodhisattvas equal to their dust motes,
Will become Buddhas according to their number of births.
Or Bodhisattvas equal to the dust motes
Of a single four-continent,
With a single rebirth remaining,
Will accomplish all-knowledge.
Wandering beings such as these,
Hearing of the Buddha's long lifespan,
Attain immeasurable, uncontaminated,
Pure fruits and retributions.
Furthermore, wandering beings equal to the dust motes
Of eight worlds,
Hearing the Buddha speak of his lifespan,
All generate the unexcelled resolve.
The World-Honored One expounds immeasurable,
Inconceivable dharmas,
Bringing abundant benefit,
Boundless like empty space.
Raining heavenly māndārava
And mahā-māndārava flowers;
Śakras and Brahmās like Ganges sands,
Arrive from countless Buddha lands.
Raining sandalwood and aloeswood,
Falling profusely in confusion,
Like birds flying down from the sky,
Scattered as offerings to the Buddhas.
Heavenly drums in open space,
Naturally emit wondrous sounds;
Heavenly garments of thousands of kinds,
Whirling down as they descend.
Exquisite censers of various jewels,
Burning priceless incense,
Naturally pervading all around,
Making offerings to all the World-Honored Ones.
That great assembly of Bodhisattvas,
Holding seven-jeweled banners and canopies,
Lofty and wondrous in tens of billions of kinds,
Successively reaching the Brahmā Heaven.
Before each of the Buddhas,
Precious pillars suspend victory banners,
And with tens of millions of verses,
They sing praises to the Tathāgatas.
Various matters such as these,
Never seen in the past;
Hearing of the Buddha's immeasurable lifespan,
All rejoice.
The Buddha's fame pervades the ten directions,
Widely benefiting wandering beings,
All endowed with wholesome roots,
To aid the unexcelled resolve.
Then the Buddha addressed Maitreya Bodhisattva Mahāsattva: "Ajita! If there are wandering beings who, hearing of the Buddha's lifespan being long and distant like this, can give rise to even a single thought of faith and understanding, the virtues and merits they attain have no limit.
If there is a good man or good woman who, for the sake of anuttarasamyaksambodhi, throughout eighty billion inconceivable kalpas practices the five pāramitās—dāna-pāramitā, śīla-pāramitā, kṣānti-pāramitā, vīrya-pāramitā, and dhyāna-pāramitā—excluding prajñā-pāramitā: compared to the former merit, it does not equal one part in a hundred, one part in a thousand, or one part in hundreds of thousands of millions of billions; even calculation and parables cannot know it. If a good man or good woman possesses such merit, that they should regress from anuttarasamyaksambodhi is impossible."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
If a person seeks the Buddha's wisdom,
Throughout eighty billion
inconceivable kalpas,
Practicing the five pāramitās;
During all these kalpas,
Giving offerings to the Buddhas,
Pratyekabuddha disciples,
And assemblies of Bodhisattvas;
Rare food and drink,
Superior clothing and bedding,
Erecting vihāras of sandalwood,
Adorned with gardens and groves;
Givings such as these,
All subtle and wondrous,
Throughout all these kalpas,
Dedicating them to the Buddha path;
Or upholding the prohibitive precepts,
Pure and without flaw,
Seeking the unexcelled path,
Praised by all the Buddhas;
Or practicing patience,
Dwelling on the ground of gentle compliance,
Even if various evils are inflicted,
His mind is unswayed;
Those who have attained the Dharma,
Harboring arrogant pride,
By these being slighted and distressed,
He is able to endure this as well;
Or diligently vigorous,
His resolve and mindfulness constantly firm,
Throughout immeasurable billions of kalpas,
Single-minded without resting;
And throughout countless kalpas,
Dwelling in leisure places,
Whether sitting or walking,
Dispelling sleep and collecting his mind;
By these causal conditions,
Able to give rise to dhyāna meditations,
For eighty billion kalpas,
Peacefully abiding with mind undistracted;
Upholding the merit of this single-mindedness,
Vowing to seek the unexcelled path:
"May I attain all-knowledge,
Exhausting the limits of dhyāna meditation."
This person, throughout hundreds of thousands
Of billions of kalpas,
Practices these various merits,
As spoken above;
Yet if there are good men and women,
Who hear me speak of my lifespan,
And give rise to even a single thought of faith,
Their merit surpasses the former!
If a person is completely free
Of all doubts and regrets,
Believing with deep mind for an instant,
Their merit is like this.
Those Bodhisattvas
Who have practiced the path for immeasurable kalpas,
Hearing me speak of my lifespan,
Are able to believe and receive it.
Such persons as these,
Receive this scripture upon their crowns:
"May I in future ages,
Have a long lifespan to liberate wandering beings;
Just as today's World-Honored One,
King among the Śākyas,
Roars the lion's roar at the place of awakening,
Expounding the Dharma without fear.
May we in future worlds,
Revered by all,
When seated at the place of awakening,
Speak of our lifespans likewise!"
If there are those of deep mind,
Pure, upright, and direct,
Learned, able to retain all,
Understanding the Buddha's words according to their meaning,
Such persons as these,
Have no doubts in this matter.
"Furthermore, Ajita! If someone hears of the Buddha's lifespan being long and distant, and understands the intent of these words, the merits attained by this person have no limit, and can give rise to the unexcelled wisdom of the Tathāgata.
How much more so if one widely hears this scripture or teaches others to hear it; if one upholds it oneself or teaches others to uphold it; if one copies it oneself or teaches others to copy it; or if with flowers, incense, necklaces, banners, canopies, scented oil, and ghee lamps, one makes offerings to the scriptural scrolls—the virtues and merits of this person are immeasurable and boundless, able to generate all-knowledge!
Ajita! If a good man or good woman, hearing me speak of my lifespan being long and distant, believes and understands with a deep mind, they will see the Buddha constantly dwelling on Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, surrounded by great Bodhisattvas and Śrāvakas, expounding the Dharma.
They will also see this Sahā world: its ground of lapis lazuli, flat and level, bordered with Jāmbūnada gold along eight roads, with rows of jewel trees; towers and pavilions all made of jewels, with the Bodhisattva assemblies dwelling within them. If someone can contemplate in this manner, you should know that this is the mark of deep faith and understanding.
Furthermore, after the Tathāgata's extinction, if someone hears this scripture and does not slander it, but gives rise to a mind of rejoicing in accord, you should know that this is already the mark of deep faith and understanding; how much more so those who read, recite, receive, and uphold it—such a person bears the Tathāgata upon their crown!
Ajita! This good man or good woman does not need to erect stūpas and temples for me, nor build monasteries and make offerings of the four requisites to the Saṅgha.
For what reason? This good man or good woman, receiving, upholding, reading, and reciting this scripture, has already erected stūpas, built monasteries, and made offerings to the Saṅgha. They have already erected seven-jeweled stūpas for the Buddha's relics, lofty and broad, tapering gradually up to the Brahmā Heaven, hanging banners, canopies, and jewel bells; offering flowers, incense, necklaces, powdered incense, paste incense, burning incense, drums, music, flutes, pipes, harps, various dances, singing hymns with wondrous voices—they have already made such offerings throughout immeasurable thousands of billions of kalpas.
Ajita! If after my extinction, hearing this scripture, someone can receive and uphold it, whether copying it oneself or teaching others to copy it, they have already erected monasteries, making thirty-two halls of red sandalwood, eight tāla trees in height, lofty, broad, and adorned, where hundreds of thousands of bhikṣus dwell, complete with gardens, bathing ponds, walkways, meditation caves, clothing, food, drink, bedding, medicine, and all articles of joy filling them. Such monasteries, halls, and pavilions, hundreds of thousands of millions, immeasurable in number, have already been offered directly before me and the Bhikṣu-Saṅgha.
Therefore I say: 'After the Tathāgata's extinction, if someone receives, upholds, reads, recites, expounds it for others, copies it oneself, or teaches others to copy it, making offerings to the scriptural scrolls, they do not need to erect stūpas and temples, nor build monasteries and make offerings to the Saṅgha.'
How much more so if someone can uphold this scripture while concurrently practicing giving, morality, patience, vigor, single-mindedness, and wisdom—their virtue is supreme, immeasurable, and boundless!
Just as empty space in the east, west, south, north, four intermediate directions, zenith, and nadir is immeasurable and boundless; this person's virtues and merits are also like this, immeasurable and boundless, swiftly arriving at all-knowledge.
If a person reads, recites, receives, and upholds this scripture, expounds it for others, copies it oneself or teaches others to copy it; and furthermore can erect stūpas, build monasteries, make offerings and praises to the Śrāvaka-Saṅgha, praise the Bodhisattvas' merits with hundreds of thousands of millions of methods of praise, and for others' sake explain this Lotus Sūtra through various causal conditions according to its meaning; and furthermore can purely uphold the precepts, dwell together with the gentle, be patient and free of anger, firm in resolve and mindfulness, constantly prize seated meditation, attaining deep samādhis, vigorous and heroic, embracing all wholesome dharmas, of sharp faculties and wisdom, skillfully answering difficult questions:
Ajita! If after my extinction, good men and good women who receive, uphold, read, and recite this scripture possess such wholesome virtues as these, you should know that these persons have already set out for the place of awakening, drawing near to anuttarasamyaksambodhi, seated beneath the tree of the path.
Ajita! Wherever these good men and good women sit, stand, or walk, in that place one should immediately erect a stūpa; all devas and humans should make offerings to it as they would to a stūpa of the Buddha."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
If after my extinction,
One can uphold this scripture,
This person's merit is boundless,
As spoken above;
This is fully endowed with
All offerings.
Erecting a stūpa for relics,
Adorned with the seven treasures,
Its spires exceedingly high and broad,
Tapering up to the Brahmā Heaven,
Jewel bells in tens of billions,
Stirred by wind, emitting wondrous sounds.
And throughout immeasurable kalpas,
Making offerings to this stūpa:
Flowers, incense, necklaces,
Heavenly garments, and music,
Burning scented oil and ghee lamps,
Constantly illuminating all around.
In the evil world of the Final Dharma age,
Those who can uphold this scripture,
Have already, as spoken above,
Perfected all offerings.
If one can uphold this scripture,
It is as if the Buddha were present,
Using ox-head sandalwood,
To build monasteries as offerings.
Thirty-two halls,
Eight tāla trees high,
Superior delicacies and wondrous clothing,
Bedding fully provided,
Dwellings for hundreds of thousands,
Gardens and bathing ponds,
Walkways and meditation caves,
All adorned and fine.
If someone with a faithful mind,
Receives, upholds, reads, recites, and copies,
Or teaches others to copy,
And makes offerings to the scrolls,
Scattering flowers, incense, powdered incense,
With sumanā, campaka,
And atimuktaka,
Scented oils constantly burned:
Those who make offerings like this,
Attain immeasurable merit;
Just as empty space is boundless,
Their merit is also like this.
How much more so upholding this scripture,
Concurrently with giving and morality,
Patience, delighting in meditation,
Free of anger and evil speech,
Reverent toward stūpas and shrines,
Humble before bhikṣus,
Far removed from arrogance,
Constantly contemplating wisdom,
Not angry when questioned with difficulties,
Explaining in accord with the Dharma;
If one can practice such conduct,
Their merits are beyond measure.
If one sees this Dharma teacher,
Accomplished in such virtues,
One should scatter heavenly flowers,
Cover his body with heavenly robes,
Bowing with heads touching his feet,
Generating the thought of him as the Buddha.
And one should have this thought:
"Before long he will go to the tree of the path,
Attaining the uncontaminated and unconditioned,
Widely benefiting devas and humans."
In the place where he dwells,
Walking, sitting, or lying down,
Or even speaking a single verse,
In that place one should erect a stūpa,
Adorning it wonderfully,
Making various offerings.
When a Buddha-child dwells upon this ground,
It is enjoyed by the Buddha;
He is constantly within it,
Walking, sitting, and lying down.
