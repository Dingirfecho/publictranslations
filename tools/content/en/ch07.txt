The Buddha addressed the bhikṣus: "In the distant past, immeasurable, boundless, inconceivable incalculable kalpas ago, there was a Buddha named Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata, Arhat, Samyaksambuddha, Vidyācaraṇasampanna, Sugata, Lokavid, Anuttara, Puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstā Devamanuṣyāṇām, Buddha, Bhagavān. His land was named Sambhava, and his kalpa was named Mahārūpa.
Bhikṣus! Since that Buddha passed into extinction, an exceedingly long time has passed. Suppose someone ground into ink all the earth elements of a great three-thousand-fold worlds, and passing eastward across a thousand lands, dropped a single dot as small as a mote of dust; passing another thousand lands, dropped another dot; continuing in this manner until all the ink of the earth elements was exhausted. What do you think: could mathematicians or the disciples of mathematicians calculate the limits and know the number of all these lands?"
"No, World-Honored One!"
"Bhikṣus! If all the lands this person passed through, whether dotted or not dotted, were entirely pulverized into dust motes, and each dust mote was counted as a kalpa: since that Buddha passed into extinction, it exceeds even this number by immeasurable, boundless hundreds of thousands of millions of billions of incalculable kalpas. By the power of the Tathāgata's knowledge and vision, I view that distant past just as if it were today."
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
I recall the past world,
Immeasurable, boundless kalpas ago,
There was a Buddha, Honored Among Two-Legged Beings,
Named Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū.
Just as a person might use force to grind
The lands of a great three-thousand-fold worlds,
Exhausting all these earth elements,
Making them all into ink.
Passing across a thousand lands,
He drops a single dust speck;
Continuing to dot in this manner,
Exhausting all this dust-mote ink.
All such lands as these,
Both dotted and undotted alike,
Are again entirely pulverized into dust,
Each dust mote counted as a kalpa.
The number of these dust motes—
The kalpas surpass even this!
Since that Buddha passed into extinction,
Immeasurable kalpas have elapsed like this.
The Tathāgata's unobstructed wisdom
Knows that Buddha's extinction,
And his Śrāvakas and Bodhisattvas,
As if seeing extinction occurring today.
Bhikṣus, you should know!
The Buddha's wisdom is pure, subtle, and sublime,
Uncontaminated and unobstructed,
Penetrating immeasurable kalpas.
The Buddha addressed the bhikṣus: "The lifespan of Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Buddha was five million four hundred thousand billion inconceivable kalpas. When that Buddha originally sat at the place of awakening, having shattered the hosts of Maras, he was on the verge of attaining anuttarasamyaksambodhi, yet the dharmas of the Buddhas did not appear before him. Thus for one minor kalpa and up to ten minor kalpas, he sat cross-legged, body and mind unmoving, yet the dharmas of the Buddhas still did not appear before him.
At that time, the devas of Trāyastriṃśa had previously arranged for that Buddha a lion seat beneath the Bodhi tree, one yojana in height, upon which the Buddha was to attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi. As soon as he sat upon this seat, the Brahmā Kings rained down heavenly flowers covering a hundred yojanas around; fragrant breezes blew from time to time, sweeping away withered flowers and raining down fresh ones. In this manner without ceasing, for a full ten minor kalpas, they made offerings to the Buddha, and continuously rained these flowers down until his extinction. The devas of the Four Heavenly Kings constantly struck heavenly drums to make offerings to the Buddha, and other devas made heavenly music, for a full ten minor kalpas, and likewise down to his extinction.
Bhikṣus! After ten minor kalpas had passed, the dharmas of the Buddhas appeared before Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Buddha, and he accomplished anuttarasamyaksambodhi.
Before that Buddha left home, he had sixteen sons, the eldest of whom was named Jñānākara. The princes each had various rare playthings; hearing that their father had accomplished anuttarasamyaksambodhi, they all abandoned what they cherished and went to where the Buddha was. Their mothers wept as they saw them off. Their grandfather, the wheel-turning sage king, together with one hundred ministers and hundreds of thousands of millions of citizens surrounding them, followed them to the place of awakening. All wished to draw near to Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata, to make offerings, revere, honor, and praise him. Having arrived, they bowed with heads touching his feet; having circumambulated the Buddha, single-mindedly joining their palms, they gazed upon the World-Honored One, praising him in gāthās:
'Great Majestic, Virtuous World-Honored One,
For the sake of liberating wandering beings,
Throughout immeasurable billions of kalpas,
Only now have you accomplished Buddhahood;
All your vows are fulfilled,
Excellent, auspicious, and unexcelled!
The World-Honored One is exceedingly rare;
Sitting in one posture for ten minor kalpas,
Your body, hands, and feet
Quiet, still, and unmoving.
Your mind constantly tranquil and calm,
Never experiencing distraction,
Ultimately and forever tranquil and extinguished,
Abiding peacefully in uncontaminated dharmas.
Now beholding the World-Honored One,
Peacefully accomplishing the Buddha path,
We attain wholesome benefit,
Praising and celebrating with great joy!
Wandering beings are constantly afflicted by suffering,
Blind, dark, without a guide,
Not knowing the path that ends suffering,
Not knowing to seek liberation.
Throughout the long night, evil destinies increase,
While the heavenly hosts decrease;
Passing from darkness into darkness,
Never hearing the name of the Buddha.
Now the Buddha has attained the supreme,
Peaceful, uncontaminated path;
We, along with devas and humans,
Attain the greatest benefit.
Therefore we all bow our heads,
Taking refuge in the Unexcelled Honored One.'
Then the sixteen princes, having praised the Buddha in gāthās, urged and requested the World-Honored One to turn the wheel of Dharma, all saying: 'May the World-Honored One expound the Dharma, bringing peace, compassion, and benefit to devas and humans.' They spoke again in gāthās:
'Hero of the World without equal,
Adorning yourself with a hundred blessings,
Having attained unexcelled wisdom,
Pray expound it for the world.
Liberate us
And all kinds of wandering beings;
Distinguish and reveal it for us,
Causing us to attain this wisdom.
If we attain Buddhahood,
Wandering beings will also be thus.
The World-Honored One knows
What wandering beings contemplate deep in their hearts,
And knows the paths they practice;
He also knows the power of their wisdom,
Their desires, joys, cultivated merit,
And karma practiced in past lives.
The World-Honored One, having fully known all this,
Will turn the unexcelled wheel!'"
The Buddha addressed the bhikṣus: "When Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Buddha attained anuttarasamyaksambodhi, in each of the ten directions five hundred million billion Buddha worlds shook in six ways. In the dark recesses between those lands, where the majestic light of the sun and moon could not reach, all became brightly illuminated. The wandering beings within them were each able to see one another, all saying: 'How is it that wandering beings have suddenly appeared here?' Furthermore, the realms and heavenly palaces in those lands, up to the Brahmā palaces, shook in six ways, and a great light universally illuminated, filling the worlds, surpassing the light of devas.
At that time, in five hundred million billion lands in the eastern direction, the Brahmā palaces shone with light exceeding their normal brightness twofold. The Brahmā Kings each thought: 'Now the light of the palaces is unprecedented. For what causal condition has this sign appeared?' Then the Brahmā Kings visited one another to discuss this matter.
Among that assembly was a Great Brahmā King named Sarvatrāṇam, who spoke gāthās for the Brahmā multitudes:
'All of our palaces,
Shine with light never seen before;
What causal condition is this?
Together we should seek it out.
Is a deva of great virtue born?
Has a Buddha appeared in the world,
That this great radiant light
Universally illuminates the ten directions?'
Then the Brahmā Kings of five hundred million billion lands, together with their palaces, each carrying heavenly flowers in the folds of their robes, went to the west to search for this sign. They saw Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata at the place of awakening beneath the Bodhi tree, seated on the lion seat, reverently surrounded by devas, Naga kings, gandharvas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, and non-humans, and saw the sixteen princes requesting the Buddha to turn the wheel of Dharma.
Immediately the Brahmā Kings bowed their heads to the Buddha's feet, circumambulated him hundreds of thousands of times, and scattered heavenly flowers over the Buddha—the flowers scattered piling up like Mount Sumeru—and made offerings to the Buddha's Bodhi tree, which was ten yojanas high. Having made offerings of flowers, they presented their palaces to that Buddha, saying: 'Only deign to show compassion and benefit us! Pray accept the palaces we offer.'
Then the Brahmā Kings, before the Buddha, single-mindedly and with one voice praised him in gāthās:
'The World-Honored One is exceedingly rare,
Difficult to encounter;
Endowed with immeasurable merits,
Able to rescue and protect all.
Great Master of devas and humans,
Pitying the world;
Wandering beings of the ten directions
Universally receive benefit.
We have come from
Five hundred million billion lands,
Abandoning the bliss of deep dhyāna meditation,
For the sake of making offerings to the Buddha.
From our merit of past lives,
Our palaces are exquisitely adorned;
We now offer them to the World-Honored One,
We only pray you will compassionately accept them.'
The Brahmā Kings, having praised the Buddha in gāthās, said: 'We only pray that the World-Honored One will turn the wheel of Dharma, liberate wandering beings, and open the path of nirvāṇa.' Then the Brahmā Kings, single-mindedly and with one voice, spoke gāthās:
'Hero of the World, Honored Among Two-Legged Beings,
We only pray you will expound the Dharma;
By the power of great love and compassion,
Liberate wandering beings afflicted by suffering.'
At that time, Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata silently gave his assent.
Furthermore, bhikṣus! The Great Brahmā Kings of five hundred million billion lands in the southeastern direction, each seeing their palaces illuminated with unprecedented brightness, rejoiced and leaped, giving rise to rare thoughts. They visited one another to discuss this matter. Among that assembly was a Great Brahmā King named Mahākāruṇika, who spoke gāthās for the Brahmā multitudes:
'By what causal condition
Does such a sign appear?
All of our palaces
Shine with light never seen before.
Is a deva of great virtue born?
Has a Buddha appeared in the world?
Never having seen this sign,
Together we should single-mindedly seek it.
Passing across thousands of millions of lands,
Together tracing the light to find it;
Most likely a Buddha has appeared in the world,
To liberate suffering wandering beings.'
Then five hundred million billion Brahmā Kings, together with their palaces, carrying heavenly flowers in the folds of their robes, went to the northwest to search for this sign. They saw Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata at the place of awakening beneath the Bodhi tree, seated on the lion seat, reverently surrounded by devas, Naga kings, gandharvas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, and non-humans, and saw the sixteen princes requesting the Buddha to turn the wheel of Dharma.
The Brahmā Kings bowed their heads to the Buddha's feet, circumambulated him hundreds of thousands of times, scattered heavenly flowers over the Buddha—the scattered flowers piling up like Mount Sumeru—and made offerings to the Buddha's Bodhi tree. Having made offerings of flowers, they presented their palaces to that Buddha, saying: 'Only deign to show compassion and benefit us! Pray accept the palaces we offer.'
Then the Brahmā Kings, before the Buddha, single-mindedly and with one voice praised him in gāthās:
'Holy Lord, King among Devas,
With the voice of the kalaviṅka bird,
You who pity wandering beings,
We now reverently bow in homage.
The World-Honored One is exceedingly rare,
Appearing once in a great while;
For one hundred and eighty kalpas,
Time passed emptily without a Buddha.
The three evil destinies were filled,
While the heavenly hosts decreased;
Now the Buddha has appeared in the world,
Serving as eyes for wandering beings.
The refuge of the world,
Rescuing and protecting all,
The father of wandering beings,
Pitying and benefiting them.
Through the celebration of our past merit,
We now meet the World-Honored One.'
The Brahmā Kings, having praised the Buddha in gāthās, said: 'We only pray that the World-Honored One will pity all, turn the wheel of Dharma, and liberate wandering beings.' Then the Brahmā Kings, single-mindedly and with one voice, spoke gāthās:
'May the Great Sage turn the wheel of Dharma,
Revealing the marks of all dharmas,
Liberating wandering beings afflicted by suffering,
Causing them to attain great joy!
Wandering beings hearing this Dharma,
Attain the path or are born in heavens;
The evil destinies will decrease,
And those of patient goodness will increase.'
At that time, Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata silently gave his assent.
Furthermore, bhikṣus! The Great Brahmā Kings of five hundred million billion lands in the southern direction, each seeing their palaces illuminated with unprecedented brightness, rejoiced and leaped, giving rise to rare thoughts. They visited one another to discuss this matter: 'For what reason do our palaces possess this radiance?' Among that assembly was a Great Brahmā King named Sudharma, who spoke gāthās for the Brahmā multitudes:
'All of our palaces
Shine with great majestic brightness;
This is not without causal condition;
This sign should be sought out.
Across hundreds of thousands of kalpas,
We have never seen such a sign;
Is a deva of great virtue born?
Has a Buddha appeared in the world?'
Then five hundred million billion Brahmā Kings, together with their palaces, carrying heavenly flowers in the folds of their robes, went to the north to search for this sign. They saw Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata at the place of awakening beneath the Bodhi tree, seated on the lion seat, reverently surrounded by devas, Naga kings, gandharvas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, and non-humans, and saw the sixteen princes requesting the Buddha to turn the wheel of Dharma.
The Brahmā Kings bowed their heads to the Buddha's feet, circumambulated him hundreds of thousands of times, scattered heavenly flowers over the Buddha—the scattered flowers piling up like Mount Sumeru—and made offerings to the Buddha's Bodhi tree. Having made offerings of flowers, they presented their palaces to that Buddha, saying: 'Only deign to show compassion and benefit us! Pray accept the palaces we offer.'
Then the Brahmā Kings, before the Buddha, single-mindedly and with one voice praised him in gāthās:
'The World-Honored One is exceedingly difficult to see,
He who shatters all afflictions;
Passing one hundred and thirty kalpas,
Only now do we behold him once.
Wandering beings hungry and thirsty
Are filled with the Dharma rain;
One never seen before,
One endowed with immeasurable wisdom,
Like the uḍumbara flower,
We meet today at last.
All of our palaces,
Adorned through receiving your light;
World-Honored One of great love and compassion,
We only pray you will deign to accept them.'
The Brahmā Kings, having praised the Buddha in gāthās, said: 'We only pray that the World-Honored One will turn the wheel of Dharma, causing all devas, Maras, Brahmās, śramaṇas, and brāhmaṇas in all worlds to attain peace and liberation.' Then the Brahmā Kings, single-mindedly and with one voice, praised him in gāthās:
'We only pray the Honored Among Devas and Humans
Will turn the unexcelled wheel of Dharma,
Striking the great Dharma drum,
Blowing the great Dharma conch,
Universally raining the great Dharma rain,
Liberating immeasurable wandering beings.
We all take refuge and beseech you:
Pray proclaim the profound and far-reaching sound!'
At that time, Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata silently gave his assent. The southwestern direction down to the nadir were also like this.
At that time, in five hundred million billion lands in the zenith above, the Great Brahmā Kings all personally beheld the palaces where they dwelled shining with unprecedented brightness, and rejoiced and leaped, giving rise to rare thoughts. They visited one another to discuss this matter: 'For what reason do our palaces possess this light?' Among that assembly was a Great Brahmā King named Śikhin, who spoke gāthās for the Brahmā multitudes:
'Now by what causal condition
Do all our palaces
Shine with majestic, virtuous light,
Adorned as never before?
Such a wondrous sign as this
Was never heard or seen in the past;
Is a deva of great virtue born?
Has a Buddha appeared in the world?'
Then five hundred million billion Brahmā Kings, together with their palaces, carrying heavenly flowers in the folds of their robes, went to the nadir to search for this sign. They saw Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata at the place of awakening beneath the Bodhi tree, seated on the lion seat, reverently surrounded by devas, Naga kings, gandharvas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, and non-humans, and saw the sixteen princes requesting the Buddha to turn the wheel of Dharma.
The Brahmā Kings bowed their heads to the Buddha's feet, circumambulated him hundreds of thousands of times, scattered heavenly flowers over the Buddha—the scattered flowers piling up like Mount Sumeru—and made offerings to the Buddha's Bodhi tree. Having made offerings of flowers, they presented their palaces to that Buddha, saying: 'Only deign to show compassion and benefit us! Pray accept the palaces we offer.'
Then the Brahmā Kings, before the Buddha, single-mindedly and with one voice praised him in gāthās:
'How good it is to behold the Buddhas,
Holy Honored Ones who rescue the world,
Able, from the prison of the three realms,
To extract wandering beings.
Universally Wise Honored Among Devas and Humans,
Pitying the mass of living beings,
Able to open the gate of amṛta,
Widely liberating all.
In past immeasurable kalpas,
Time passed emptily without a Buddha;
Before the World-Honored One appeared,
The ten directions were constantly dark and murky;
The three evil destinies increased,
And asuras also flourished;
The heavenly hosts gradually decreased,
And at death, most fell into evil realms.
Not hearing the Dharma from the Buddha,
Constantly committing unwholesome deeds;
Physical strength and wisdom,
All these diminished.
Because of the causal conditions of sinful karma,
They lost joy and the perception of joy;
Abiding in dharmas of false views,
Not recognizing wholesome rules of deportment;
Not receiving the Buddha's transformation,
Constantly falling into evil destinies.
The Buddha is the eye of the world;
Appearing after a long age,
Out of compassion for wandering beings,
He appears within the world.
Transcending and accomplishing right awakening,
We deeply celebrate and rejoice;
And all other multitudes
Rejoice and praise what has never been before.
All of our palaces,
Adorned through receiving your light,
We now offer to the World-Honored One;
Pray deign compassionately to accept them.
We vow that by this merit,
Pervading universally to all,
We and wandering beings
May together accomplish the Buddha path!'
Then five hundred million billion Brahmā Kings, having praised the Buddha in gāthās, addressed the Buddha: 'We only pray that the World-Honored One will turn the wheel of Dharma, bringing abundant peace and abundant liberation.' Then the Brahmā Kings spoke gāthās:
'May the World-Honored One turn the wheel of Dharma,
Striking the amṛta Dharma drum,
Liberating wandering beings afflicted by suffering,
Opening and revealing the path of nirvāṇa.
We only pray you will accept our request;
With your great, subtle, and sublime voice,
Compassionately expounding
The Dharma cultivated across immeasurable kalpas!'
At that time, Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Tathāgata, receiving the requests of the Brahmā Kings of the ten directions and the sixteen princes, immediately turned the Dharma wheel of three turnings and twelve aspects—which cannot be turned by śramaṇas, brāhmaṇas, devas, Maras, Brahmās, or others in the world—namely: this is suffering, this is the origin of suffering, this is the cessation of suffering, this is the path to the cessation of suffering.
He also widely expounded the Twelve Links of Dependent Origination: ignorance conditions mental formations; mental formations condition consciousness; consciousness conditions name and form; name and form condition the six sense bases; the six sense bases condition contact; contact conditions feeling; feeling conditions craving; craving conditions grasping; grasping conditions becoming; becoming conditions birth; birth conditions aging, death, sorrow, lamentation, suffering, and distress.
Through the cessation of ignorance, mental formations cease; through the cessation of mental formations, consciousness ceases; through the cessation of consciousness, name and form cease; through the cessation of name and form, the six sense bases cease; through the cessation of the six sense bases, contact ceases; through the cessation of contact, feeling ceases; through the cessation of feeling, craving ceases; through the cessation of craving, grasping ceases; through the cessation of grasping, becoming ceases; through the cessation of becoming, birth ceases; through the cessation of birth, aging, death, sorrow, lamentation, suffering, and distress cease.
When the Buddha expounded this Dharma amidst the great assembly of devas and humans, six hundred million billion inconceivable numbers of people, by unclinging to all dharmas, attained liberation of their minds from outpourings; all attained deep and subtle dhyāna meditations, the three knowledges, the six spiritual superknowledges, and were endowed with the eight liberations.
When he expounded the Dharma the second, third, and fourth times, multitudes of wandering beings numbering tens of millions of Ganges sands of inconceivable number, also by unclinging to all dharmas, attained liberation of their minds from outpourings. From that time onward, the assemblies of Śrāvakas were immeasurable, boundless, and beyond reckoning.
At that time, the sixteen princes—who had all left home as youths to become śrāmaṇeras, whose faculties were sharp and clear, whose wisdom was luminous, who had already made offerings to hundreds of thousands of millions of Buddhas, purely cultivated Brahma conduct, and sought anuttarasamyaksambodhi—addressed the Buddha together: 'World-Honored One! All these immeasurable thousands of millions of great virtuous Śrāvakas have already accomplished their realization. May the World-Honored One also expound for our sake the Dharma of anuttarasamyaksambodhi, so that having heard it, we may all practice and learn it together. World-Honored One! We aspire to the knowledge and vision of the Tathāgata; what we contemplate deep in our hearts, the Buddha himself witnesses and knows.'
At that time, eighty thousand billion people in the retinue led by the wheel-turning sage king, seeing the sixteen princes leave home, also sought to leave home. The king immediately permitted them.
Then that Buddha, receiving the request of the śrāmaṇeras, after twenty thousand kalpas had passed, expounded amidst the fourfold assembly this Great Vehicle sūtra named the Lotus Flower of the Wonderful Dharma, a teaching for Bodhisattvas and guarded in the mindfulness of the Buddhas.
When he finished expounding this sūtra, the sixteen śrāmaṇeras, for the sake of anuttarasamyaksambodhi, all received and upheld it together, reciting and penetrating it thoroughly. When this sūtra was expounded, the sixteen Bodhisattva śrāmaṇeras all faithfully accepted it; among the Śrāvaka assembly, there were also those who believed and understood; but the remaining multitudes of wandering beings, thousands of millions of varieties, all gave rise to doubt. The Buddha expounded this sūtra for eight thousand kalpas without resting or pausing.
Having expounded this sūtra, he immediately entered a quiet room and remained in dhyāna meditation for eighty-four thousand kalpas. At that time, the sixteen Bodhisattva śrāmaṇeras, knowing the Buddha had entered the room in tranquil dhyāna, each ascended a Dharma seat and likewise for eighty-four thousand kalpas widely expounded and distinguished the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma for the fourfold assembly. Each of them liberated wandering beings numbering six hundred million billion inconceivable number of Ganges sands, instructing, benefiting, and delighting them, causing them to generate the mind of anuttarasamyaksambodhi.
After eighty-four thousand kalpas had passed, Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Buddha arose from samādhi, went to the Dharma seat, sat down serenely, and announced to the great assembly: 'These sixteen Bodhisattva śrāmaṇeras are exceedingly rare; their faculties are sharp, their wisdom clear. They have already made offerings to immeasurable thousands of millions of Buddhas. Under those Buddhas, they constantly cultivated Brahma conduct, received and upheld the Buddha's wisdom, and revealed it to wandering beings, causing them to enter within it. You should all frequently draw close to and make offerings to them.
For what reason? If Śrāvakas, Pratyekabuddhas, or Bodhisattvas are able to believe the sūtra teachings expounded by these sixteen Bodhisattvas, receiving, upholding, and not slandering them, these persons will all attain anuttarasamyaksambodhi and the wisdom of the Tathāgata.'"
The Buddha addressed the bhikṣus: "These sixteen Bodhisattvas constantly rejoiced in speaking this Sūtra of the Lotus Flower of the Wonderful Dharma. The wandering beings numbering six hundred million billion inconceivable number of Ganges sands transformed by each Bodhisattva were reborn life after life together with those Bodhisattvas, hearing the Dharma from them, all believing and understanding it. By this causal condition, they have encountered four hundred million billion Buddhas, World-Honored Ones, and down to the present day this has not ceased.
Bhikṣus! I now tell you: those sixteen śrāmaṇeras, disciples of that Buddha, have all now attained anuttarasamyaksambodhi, presently expounding the Dharma in lands of the ten directions, having immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of Bodhisattvas and Śrāvakas as their attendants.
Two śrāmaṇeras became Buddhas in the east: one named Akṣobhya, in the land Abhirati; the second named Merukūṭa.
Two Buddhas in the southeast: one named Siṃhaghoṣa, the second named Siṃhadhvaja.
Two Buddhas in the south: one named Ākāśapratiṣṭhita, the second named Nityaparinirvṛta.
Two Buddhas in the southwest: one named Indradhvaja, the second named Brahmadhvaja.
Two Buddhas in the west: one named Amitābha, the second named Sarvalokadhātūpadravodvegapratyuttīrṇa.
Two Buddhas in the northwest: one named Tamālapatracandanagandhābhijña, the second named Merukalpa.
Two Buddhas in the north: one named Meghasvararāja, the second named Meghasvararāja.
The Buddha of the northeast is named Sarvalokabhayāstambhitatvavidhvaṃsakāra.
The sixteenth is myself, Śākyamuni Buddha, who accomplished anuttarasamyaksambodhi in the Sahā world.
Bhikṣus! When we were śrāmaṇeras, each of us taught and transformed immeasurable hundreds of thousands of millions of Ganges sands of wandering beings, who heard the Dharma from me for the sake of anuttarasamyaksambodhi. Among these wandering beings, there are those who down to the present day dwell on the ground of Śrāvakas; I constantly teach and transform them in anuttarasamyaksambodhi. These persons will gradually enter the Buddha path through this Dharma.
For what reason? The Tathāgata's wisdom is difficult to believe and difficult to understand. Those wandering beings like immeasurable Ganges sands transformed at that time are you bhikṣus, as well as the Śrāvaka disciples in future ages after my extinction.
After my extinction, there will also be disciples who do not hear this sūtra, who do not know and do not perceive the practices of Bodhisattvas, and regarding the merits they have attained give rise to the thought of extinction, entering into nirvāṇa. When I become a Buddha in other lands, I will have other names. Although these persons give rise to the thought of extinction and enter nirvāṇa, in those lands they will seek the Buddha's wisdom and hear this sūtra; only through the Buddha Vehicle will they attain extinction; there are no other vehicles, except for the expedient teachings of the Tathāgatas.
Bhikṣus! When the Tathāgata knows of himself that the time for nirvāṇa has arrived, when the assembly is pure, their faith and understanding firm, penetrating the Dharma of emptiness, and deeply entering dhyāna meditation, he gathers the Bodhisattvas and Śrāvaka assemblies to expound this sūtra for them. In the world, there are no two vehicles through which extinction is attained; only by the One Buddha Vehicle is extinction attained.
Bhikṣus, you should know! The Tathāgata, through expedient means, penetrates deeply into the nature of wandering beings; knowing that their resolve delights in small dharmas and that they are deeply attached to the five desires, for their sake he speaks of nirvāṇa. When these people hear it, they believe and accept it.
Suppose there is a perilous, bad road of five hundred yojanas, desolate, devoid of inhabitants, a place of terror. If a large multitude wishes to cross this road to reach a cache of rare treasures, there is a guide, intelligent and clear-sighted, who thoroughly knows the passable and blocked features of the perilous road, leading the multitude wishing to cross this hardship.
The multitude being led becomes weary and discouraged midway, saying to the guide: 'We are utterly exhausted and terrified, unable to advance further; the road ahead is still distant, and we now wish to turn back.'
The guide, possessing many expedient means, has this thought: 'These people are to be pitied; how could they abandon the great treasure and wish to turn back?' Having had this thought, by the power of expedient means, at a distance of three hundred yojanas along the perilous road, he conjures a city. He says to the people: 'Do not fear, do not turn back! Now in this great city you may rest, doing as you please. If you enter this city, you will quickly attain peace. If you are later able to advance to the treasury, you may also depart.'
Then the exhausted multitude rejoiced greatly, praising what they had never had before: 'We now escape this evil road and happily attain peaceful security!'
Thereupon the people advanced and entered the conjured city, giving rise to the thought that they had already crossed over, giving rise to the thought of peaceful security.
Then the guide, knowing that this multitude had rested and were no longer weary, vanished the conjured city, and said to the people: 'Come, advance! The treasury is near. That great city earlier was conjured by me merely for you to rest.'
Bhikṣus! The Tathāgata is also like this; now, acting as a great guide for all of you, knowing that the evil road of birth, death, and afflictions is perilous, difficult, and long, requiring crossing and liberation. If wandering beings hear only of the One Buddha Vehicle, they will not wish to see the Buddha, nor wish to draw near, thinking: 'The Buddha path is long and distant; only after long and bitter toil can it be accomplished.'
The Buddha, knowing their minds are timid, weak, and base, through the power of expedient means speaks of two nirvāṇas to provide a resting place midway. If wandering beings dwell upon these two grounds, the Tathāgata then says to them: 'What you have to do is not yet finished. The ground where you dwell is near to the Buddha's wisdom; you should observe and calculate: the nirvāṇa you have attained is not real. It is merely through the Tathāgata's power of expedient means that within the One Buddha Vehicle he distinguishes and speaks of three.'
Just as that guide, for the sake of rest, conjured a great city, and knowing they were rested, addressed them: 'The treasury is near; this city is not real, it was merely conjured by me.'"
Thereupon, the World-Honored One, wishing to restate this meaning, spoke gāthās:
Mahābhijñājñānābhibhū Buddha,
Sat at the place of awakening for ten kalpas;
The dharmas of the Buddhas did not appear before him,
And he could not accomplish the Buddha path.
Devas, spirits, Naga kings,
And asura assemblies,
Constantly rained heavenly flowers,
To make offerings to that Buddha.
Devas struck heavenly drums,
And played various kinds of music;
Fragrant breezes blew away withered flowers,
And rained down fresh and fine ones.
After ten minor kalpas had passed,
He finally accomplished the Buddha path;
Devas and worldly humans,
Their hearts all leaped with joy.
That Buddha's sixteen sons,
All together with their retinues,
Surrounded by thousands of millions,
Went together to where the Buddha was,
Bowing heads to the Buddha's feet,
And requesting him to turn the wheel of Dharma:
"May the Dharma rain of the holy lion,
Fill me and all beings!"
The World-Honored One is exceedingly difficult to meet,
Appearing once in a great while;
To awaken the multitude of living beings,
He shook all realms.
The worlds of the eastern direction,
Five hundred million billion lands,
Their Brahmā palaces shone with light,
As never experienced in the past.
The Brahmās, seeing this sign,
Traced it to where the Buddha was;
Scattering flowers to make offerings,
And presenting their palaces,
Requesting the Buddha to turn the wheel of Dharma,
Praising him in gāthās.
The Buddha, knowing the time had not arrived,
Accepted their request and sat in silence.
The other three directions, four intermediates,
Zenith and nadir were also like this;
Scattering flowers, offering palaces,
Requesting the Buddha to turn the wheel of Dharma:
"The World-Honored One is exceedingly difficult to meet;
We pray that with great love and compassion,
You will widely open the gate of amṛta,
And turn the unexcelled wheel of Dharma!"
The World-Honored One of immeasurable wisdom,
Accepted the requests of those multitudes,
Proclaiming various dharmas for them:
"The Four Truths and Twelve Links of Conditions;
From ignorance down to aging and death,
All exist through the condition of birth.
Faults and tribulations such as these,
You all should understand."
When he proclaimed this Dharma,
Six hundred million billion beings,
Exhausted the limits of suffering,
All becoming arhats.
When he expounded the Dharma the second time,
Multitudes like thousands of millions of Ganges sands,
Unclinging to all dharmas,
Also became arhats.
Those who attained the path after this,
Their numbers were without measure;
Calculations through billions of kalpas,
Could not reach their limits.
At that time the sixteen princes,
Left home to become śrāmaṇeras;
Together they requested that Buddha,
To expound the Great Vehicle Dharma:
"We and our retinues,
Will all accomplish the Buddha path;
We wish to attain, like the World-Honored One,
The foremost pure wisdom eye."
The Buddha knew the minds of the youths,
And what they practiced in past lives;
Through immeasurable causal conditions,
And various parables,
He expounded the Six Pāramitās,
And matters of spiritual superknowledges.
Distinguishing the genuine reality of Dharma,
The path practiced by Bodhisattvas,
He expounded this Lotus Sūtra,
In verses like Ganges sands.
That Buddha, having spoken the sūtra,
Entered dhyāna meditation in a quiet chamber,
Sitting with one mind in one place,
For eighty-four thousand kalpas.
All these śrāmaṇeras,
Knowing the Buddha had not emerged from meditation,
For immeasurable billions of multitudes,
Expounded the Buddha's unexcelled wisdom.
Each seated upon a Dharma seat,
Expounded this Great Vehicle sūtra,
After the Buddha entered tranquil silence,
Proclaiming and aiding the Dharma transformation.
Each of the śrāmaṇeras,
Liberated wandering beings,
Numbering six hundred million billion,
Multitudes like the sands of the Ganges.
After that Buddha passed into extinction,
All those who heard the Dharma,
In various Buddha lands,
Were constantly born together with their masters.
These sixteen śrāmaṇeras,
Fully practiced the Buddha path;
Now in the ten directions,
Each has accomplished right awakening.
Those who heard the Dharma at that time,
Are each in the abodes of those Buddhas;
Those who dwell as Śrāvakas,
Are gradually taught the Buddha path.
I was among the sixteen,
And in the past also spoke to you;
Therefore through expedient means,
I guide you toward the Buddha's wisdom.
By this original causal condition,
I now speak the Lotus Sūtra,
Causing you to enter the Buddha path;
Take care not to harbor alarm or fear!
Suppose there is a perilous, evil road,
Desolate, remote, with many poisonous beasts,
And furthermore devoid of water or grass,
A place dreaded by people.
A countless multitude of tens of millions,
Wishes to cross this perilous road;
The way is exceedingly vast and distant,
Spanning five hundred yojanas.
At that time there is a guide,
Of strong memory and wisdom,
Clear-sighted and resolute of mind,
Rescuing the multitude from peril and disaster.
The people all become weary and exhausted,
And address the guide:
"We are now utterly exhausted;
From here we wish to turn back."
The guide has this thought:
"These people are deeply to be pitied;
How could they wish to turn back,
And lose the great treasure?"
Immediately he ponders an expedient means:
"I should deploy spiritual power."
He conjures a great walled city,
With adorned dwellings,
Surrounded by gardens and groves,
Canals, streams, and bathing ponds,
Double gates and lofty towers,
Filled with men and women.
Having made this conjuration,
He comforts the multitude, saying: "Do not fear!
Enter this city,
Where each may do as you please."
The people, having entered the city,
All rejoice greatly in their hearts,
Giving rise to thoughts of peaceful security,
Considering that they have already been delivered.
The guide, knowing they are rested,
Gathers the multitude and announces:
"You should advance;
This is merely a conjured city!
Seeing that you were utterly exhausted,
And wished to turn back midway,
I therefore by the power of expedient means,
Provisionally conjured this city.
Exert diligence and vigor;
Together we will reach the treasury."
I too am like this,
The Guide of all beings.
Seeing those who seek the path,
Become negligent and exhausted midway,
Unable to cross the perilous road
Of birth, death, and afflictions.
Therefore by the power of expedient means,
For the sake of their rest, I speak of nirvāṇa.
Saying: "Your suffering is extinguished,
What was to be done is already accomplished."
Knowing they have reached nirvāṇa,
And all attained arhatship,
Only then do I gather the great assembly,
To expound the genuine Dharma for them.
Through the expedient power of the Buddhas,
They distinguish and speak of three vehicles;
There is only the One Buddha Vehicle;
For the sake of a resting place, two are spoken.
"I now speak the truth to you:
What you attained is not extinction;
For the sake of the Buddha's all-knowledge,
You must arouse great vigor!
When you realize all-knowledge,
The ten powers and other Buddha-dharmas,
Endowed with the thirty-two marks,
Only that is genuine extinction!"
The Guides among the Buddhas,
Speak of nirvāṇa for the sake of rest;
Knowing they have rested,
They lead them into the Buddha's wisdom.
