First: Clarifying the Exhortation to Practice the Lotus Repentance Liturgy for Twenty-One Days

After the Tathāgata’s extinction, in the turbid and evil age of the final five hundred years, if bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, or upāsikās who recite Great Vehicle scriptures wish to cultivate Great Vehicle practices, resolve upon the Great Vehicle mind, behold the physical body of Bodhisattva Samantabhadra, behold Śākyamuni Buddha, the jewel stūpa of Prabhūtaratna, his emanation Buddhas, and the Buddhas of the ten directions; if they wish to attain the purity of the six sense faculties, enter the Buddha’s realm, and penetrate unhindered; if they wish to hear what the Buddhas of the ten directions expound and in a single thought-moment receive, uphold, penetrate, remember without forgetting, and explain and expound without hindrance; if they wish to become companions of Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, and other great Bodhisattvas.

And if they wish to attain universally manifesting physical bodies, and in a single thought-moment without arising from the concentration of cessation pervade all Buddha lands of the ten directions to make offerings to all Buddhas; if they wish in a single thought-moment to reach all Buddha lands of the ten directions, manifesting various physical bodies, performing various spiritual transformations, radiating great light, and expounding the Dharma to liberate all wandering beings and lead them into the inconceivable Single Vehicle; if they wish to crush the four maras, purify all afflictions, extinguish all negativities obstructing the Path, enter the Bodhisattva’s correct position in this present body, and become endowed with all sovereign virtues of the Buddhas—they should first, in a quiet and empty place, single-mindedly and diligently enter the Lotus Samādhi for twenty-one days.

If there are those who in this present body have committed the five inexpiable offenses or the four grave violations, losing the bhikṣu dharma, and wish to attain purity, regain the complete rules of conduct of a śramaṇa, and attain the various superior and wondrous merits expounded above, they too should single-mindedly and diligently cultivate the Lotus Samādhi for twenty-one days.

Why is this? This Lotus Sūtra is the secret treasury of all Tathāgatas, supreme among all scriptures, traversing the great direct path without impediment. It is like the bright jewel in the topknot of the wheel-turning king, not given carelessly to anyone; if one obtains it, all diverse rare treasures one desires are fully supplied. The Lotus Samādhi is also like this, able to bestow upon all wandering beings the rare treasures of the Buddha-Dharma. Therefore, Bodhisattva practitioners should disregard body and life, cultivating this scripture to the end of future time—how much more so for twenty-one days!

Question: The Buddha Path is vast and distant; what benefit can there be in cultivating for twenty-one days?

Answer: There are three kinds of benefits, which will be expounded below.

Second: Clarifying the Preliminary Expedients for the Twenty-One Day Practice

There are two kinds of cultivation:

First, the practice in the beginning. Second, the long-standing practice. Teach the beginning practitioner to use this method. Teach the long-standing practitioner according to the Chapter on Peaceful Practices.

All repentance practices require preliminary expedients. Why is this? If one does not first adorn and purify body and mind but abruptly enters the bodhimaṇḍa, the mind for the Path will not arise, practice will not accord with the Dharma, and no holy response will be induced.

Therefore, prior to the formal repentance, during one period of seven days, one should first subdue one’s mind, cease all entangled activities, make offerings to the Three Jewels, adorn the bodhimaṇḍa, cleanse one’s garments, and single-mindedly bind one’s thoughts. Recall all evil karma committed from this present body back into past lives, give rise to deep shame and remorse, bow to the Buddhas in repentance, circumambulate while chanting scriptures, practice seated meditation and contemplation, and make focused and diligent vows. This is done so that when engaging in the formal practice of samādhi, body and mind will be pure and free of hindrances, and all aspirations of the heart will be fulfilled. One must also recite the various repentance texts below, causing them to be completely fluent.

Third: Clarifying the Method of Single-Minded Diligence in the Twenty-One Day Practice upon Formally Entering the Bodhimaṇḍa

Formally enter the bodhimaṇḍa on one of the six fasting days. On these days, the crown prince, messengers of the Four Heavenly Kings, and other virtuous heavenly spirits descend to the human realm to inspect good and evil. Seeing those cultivating good, they register them in the ledger of goodness, comfort and protect them, and manifest auspicious omens, causing the practitioner’s heart to rejoice and increasing wholesome roots.

When the practitioner first wishes to enter the bodhimaṇḍa, they should settle their own mind:

From this present moment until twenty-one days are fulfilled, during this period, I will follow the Buddha’s teaching with single-minded diligence.

Why is this? If the mind has discordant thoughts, it becomes mixed with afflictions and is named impure. The mind being impure, how could it accord with the correct path of samādhi? Therefore, bind the mind with a vow, not sparing body or life, with single-minded diligence to complete twenty-one days.

Question: The mental marks of wandering beings shift with diverse conditions; how can one attain single-minded diligence?

Answer: There are two ways to cultivate the single mind: first, cultivating the single mind within phenomena; second, cultivating the single mind within principle.

Cultivating the single mind within phenomena: when the practitioner first enters the bodhimaṇḍa, form this thought: During the twenty-one days, when bowing to the Buddha, I will single-mindedly bow to the Buddha without wandering to other conditions; even down to repentance, circumambulation, chanting scriptures, and seated meditation, all will be single-minded, free of scattered thoughts during the ritual. Persisting like this for twenty-one days is named single-minded diligence cultivated within phenomena.

Second, cultivating single-minded diligence within principle: when the practitioner first enters the bodhimaṇḍa, form this thought: From this moment until the twenty-one days are fulfilled, in all actions performed, I will constantly illuminate that the mind that acts and the nature of mind are non-dual.

Why is this? Just as when bowing to the Buddha the nature of mind is unborn and unceasing, one should know that in all diverse activities performed, the nature of mind is unborn and unceasing. When contemplating in this manner, one sees that all minds are a single mind, because from the beginning the nature of mind has ever been of a single mark.

If the practitioner can reflect back on the source of mind in this manner, thought-moment following thought-moment in succession, throughout twenty-one days without apprehending any mental marks, this is named the method of cultivating single-minded diligence within principle.

Fourth: Clarifying the Method of Actual Practice upon First Entering the Bodhimaṇḍa

When the practitioner first enters the bodhimaṇḍa, they must be fully endowed with ten dharmas:

  1. Adorning and purifying the bodhimaṇḍa
  2. Purifying the body
  3. Making offerings with the three karmas
  4. Reverently inviting the Three Jewels
  5. Praising the Three Jewels
  6. Bowing to the Buddhas
  7. Repentance
  8. Circumambulation
  9. Reciting the Lotus Sūtra
  10. Contemplating the realm of the Single Reality

During the twenty-one days, across the six periods of day and night, in the single period of first entering the bodhimaṇḍa, one must fully cultivate these ten dharmas. In each of the subsequent periods, one may omit the single dharma of inviting the Buddhas; the remaining nine dharmas are practiced identically without difference.

Within these ten dharmas, some merely explain procedures of action, some instruct mental visualization, and some instruct the oral recitation of phrases. The practitioner should grasp their meaning and apply them appropriately; it is not necessary to chant this explanatory text.

First: Clarifying the Practitioner’s Method of Adorning and Purifying the Bodhimaṇḍa

In an empty and quiet place, prepare and adorn a room as the bodhimaṇḍa. Arrange a separate personal seating place, partitioned from the bodhimaṇḍa. Within the bodhimaṇḍa, erect a fine high seat and enshrine a copy of the Lotus Sūtra. It is not necessary to enshrine statues, relics, or other scriptures; enshrine only the Lotus Sūtra, and arrange banners, canopies, and diverse offering implements.

At dawn on the day of entering the bodhimaṇḍa, sweep the ground clean, sprinkle fragrant water, and coat the floor with fragrant mud. Light various fragrant oil lamps, scatter diverse flowers and powdered incense, and burn renowned incense to make offerings to the Three Jewels, prepared according to one’s means, exhausting one’s sincerity to make it completely solemn and pure.

Why is this? Inwardly, the practitioner’s veneration for the Three Jewels surpasses the three realms. Now wishing to invite them and make offerings, how could one have a light or casual mind? If one cannot dedicate one’s wealth to make offerings to the Great Vehicle, one will ultimately be unable to invite the wise and move the holy; heavy negativities will not be eliminated, and how could samādhi arise?

Second: Clarifying the Practitioner’s Method of Purifying the Body

Upon first entering the bodhimaṇḍa, bathe with fragrant water and put on clean garments—either the great robe or newly dyed garments. If lacking these, take one’s finest garments as garments for entering the bodhimaṇḍa.

Afterward, when leaving the bodhimaṇḍa to visit impure places, take off the clean garments and put on old garments. When the task is done, bathe again, put on the clean garments, and enter the bodhimaṇḍa to resume practice.

Third: Clarifying the Practitioner’s Method of Making Offerings with the Three Karmas

Upon first entering the bodhimaṇḍa, approach before the Dharma seat and first spread the practice mat. Standing upright, first mindfully generate compassion for all wandering beings, wishing to rescue and liberate them. Next, give rise to earnest shame, remorse, and deep sincerity, visualizing the Tathāgata and the Three Jewels filling the empty space of the ten directions and reflecting into the bodhimaṇḍa. At this time, hold the incense burner, burn renowned incense, scatter diverse flowers to make offerings to the Three Jewels, cast the five limbs to the ground, and chant aloud:

With all reverence! Single-mindedly and reverently bowing to all ever-abiding Buddhas of the ten directions!

The mind follows the body and speech in single-minded prostration, free of scattered thoughts. Understand that this body is like a shadow, unreal; in the one who bows and that which is bowed to, the mind apprehends nothing. All wandering beings also enter into this sea of the Dharma-realm of bowing to the Buddhas, universally bowing to the Buddhas of the ten directions. Having performed one bow, stand upright with proper deportment, and chant aloud:

Single-mindedly and reverently bowing to all ever-abiding Dharma of the ten directions!

Having performed one bow, stand upright with proper deportment, and chant aloud:

Single-mindedly and reverently bowing to all ever-abiding Saṅgha of the ten directions!

Having performed one bow, the mental method does not differ from that explained above for bowing to the Buddhas. Having bowed to the Three Jewels, kneel upright with the right knee on the ground, maintain proper deportment, single-mindedly burn incense and scatter flowers, with upright body and correct thoughts, and chant aloud:

Solemnly holding incense and flowers, making offerings according with Dharma. May this cloud of incense and flowers pervade the worlds of the ten directions, making offerings to the Buddha, scriptural Dharmas, Bodhisattvas, assemblies of Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas, and all heavenly beings and ṛṣis, accepted and utilized to perform Buddha activities!

Next, visualize thinking: “May these incense and flowers, thought-moment by thought-moment, reach all Buddha lands of the ten directions, creating diverse jeweled terraces, towers, and superior forms; creating diverse instrumental music, wondrous sounds, songs, hymns, and praises; creating diverse sandalwood and aloeswood incenses; creating diverse dishes, drinks, medicines, and superior flavors; creating diverse robes, necklaces, springs, bathing pools, and superior textures; creating diverse dhyānas, wisdoms, pure genuine marks, and immeasurable superior Dharma gates, all filling the Dharma-realm to perform Buddha activities, making offerings to all Buddhas of the ten directions and three periods of time and to the Three Jewels. May the Three Jewels receive them, and may they perfume all wandering beings to generate the bodhicitta. Before each Buddha, see one’s own body making offerings in this manner, equal without difference. Furthermore, may beings of the six destinies and four modes of birth enter into this sea of the Dharma-realm of my offerings. Understand that such offerings are born from the mind, devoid of self-nature, and do not grasp at them.”

Having formed this thought, bring the five limbs to the ground, and chant aloud:

With reverence, I make offerings!

Fourth: Clarifying the Practitioner’s Method of Inviting the Three Jewels

Having completed the offerings of the three karmas, burn incense and scatter flowers again. With single-minded correct mindfulness, turn the prior offering mind to reverently inviting the Three Jewels. When inviting the Three Jewels, direct the mind to face those invited, chant their names, and invite each according to Dharma, without a scattered or careless mind.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Śākyamuni Buddha!

Understand that the Dharma-body is like empty space, without marks of coming or going; all other Buddhas are also like this. As the mind visualizes: from Mount Gṛdhrakūṭa, surrounded by the great assembly, he comes to the bodhimaṇḍa, receiving my earnest invitation and offerings.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Prabhūtaratna World-Honored One of the past!

Visualize: the jewel stūpa of Prabhūtaratna wells forth from the earth, manifesting its reflection in the bodhimaṇḍa, receiving my offerings.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo emanations of Śākyamuni Buddha of the ten directions!

Visualize: the emanation Buddhas all gather like clouds beneath jewel trees, receiving my offerings.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo all Buddhas in the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma!

Visualize: Candrasūryapradīpa Buddhas of the past, Kamaladalavimalanakṣatrarājasaṅkusumitābhijña Buddhas of the present, Padmaprabha Buddhas of the future, all manifest before me, receiving my offerings.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo all ever-abiding Buddhas of the ten directions!

Visualize: the Buddhas of the eastern direction, yellow-golden in body, complete with marks and signs, radiating great light, surrounded by great assemblies, come to the bodhimaṇḍa filling empty space to receive my offerings; the southern, western, northern directions, intermediate directions, zenith, and nadir are also like this.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Great Vehicle Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma!

Visualize: the profound and secret Dharma treasury all manifests before me, receiving my offerings.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo all ever-abiding Dharma of the ten directions!

Visualize: all Dharma treasures of all Buddhas of the ten directions manifest in the bodhimaṇḍa, receiving my offerings.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Mañjuśrī Bodhisattva Mahāsattva!

Visualize: surrounded by immeasurable Bodhisattvas, receiving my offerings.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Maitreya Bodhisattva Mahāsattva!

Visualize as before.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Bhaiṣajyarāja Bodhisattva and Bhaiṣajyasamudgata Bodhisattva Mahāsattva!

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva and Akṣayamati Bodhisattva Mahāsattva!

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Gadgadasvara Bodhisattva and Padmaśrī Bodhisattva Mahāsattva!

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Nityodyukta Bodhisattva and Mahāsthāmaprāpta Bodhisattva Mahāsattva!

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Mahāpratibhāna Bodhisattva and Prajñākūṭa Bodhisattva Mahāsattva!

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Nakṣatrarājasaṅkusumitābhijña Bodhisattva, Pradānaśūra Bodhisattva, and Dharaṇīṃdhara Bodhisattva Mahāsattva!

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Viśiṣṭacāritra and the boundless asaṃkhyeya Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas welling forth from below!

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Bodhisattva Samantabhadra and all great Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas in the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma!

Visualize: Bodhisattva Samantabhadra, riding the six-tusked white elephant king, manifesting a body that all wandering beings delight in seeing, comes with immeasurable attendants into the bodhimaṇḍa, receiving my offerings.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo Śāriputra and all great Śrāvaka assemblies in the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma!

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: Namo all ever-abiding Saṅgha of the ten directions!

Visualize: all great Bodhisattvas, Śrāvakas, and Pratyekabuddhas of the ten directions radiate great light, surrounded by great retinues, coming to the bodhimaṇḍa to receive my offerings.

Single-mindedly and reverently inviting: All devas, nāgas, yakṣas, gandharvas, asuras, garuḍas, kiṃnaras, mahoragas, humans, non-humans, and all spirits of the unseen space, each with their retinues, in the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma!

Having invited once in this order, cast the five limbs to the ground. Kneel upright again, burn incense, scatter flowers, and invite by name from the beginning. Having completed three repetitions of invitation, proclaim aloud:

May our Fundamental Teacher, Śākyamuni World-Honored One, Prabhūtaratna Tathāgata, and the emanation Buddhas, out of great love and compassion, accept my invitation and come to the bodhimaṇḍa!

May the truly pure Dharma gate of the Great Vehicle Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma, pitying and sheltering us, accept my invitation and come to the bodhimaṇḍa!

May Mañjuśrī Bodhisattva, Maitreya Bodhisattva, the Bodhisattvas welling forth from below such as Viśiṣṭacāritra, Bodhisattva Samantabhadra, and all great Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas in the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma, out of great love and compassion, accept my invitation and come to the bodhimaṇḍa!

May Śāriputra and all great Śrāvakas, out of compassion, accept my invitation and come to the bodhimaṇḍa!

May all Three Jewels of the ten directions, pitying and sheltering us, accept my invitation and come to the bodhimaṇḍa!

May all the eightfold assembly of devas, nāgas, and spirits give rise to pity, accept my invitation, and come to the bodhimaṇḍa! May all these holy assemblies bear witness: for the sake of all wandering beings of the six destinies throughout the ten directions, I today wish to cultivate unexcelled Great Vehicle bodhi and destroy all heavy negativities obstructing the Path. I vow to attain the Lotus Samādhi and universally manifest physical bodies, in a single thought-moment making offerings to the Three Jewels of the ten directions, and in a single thought-moment universally liberating all wandering beings of the six destinies throughout the ten directions, leading them to enter the equal great wisdom of the Single Vehicle.

For this reason, during twenty-one days with single-minded diligence, I will cultivate as expounded in the scripture. May all Buddhas, Bodhisattvas, and Great Master Samantabhadra, through the power of their fundamental vows, receive my repentance, causing my practice decisively to destroy all sinful obstacles and the Dharma gates to manifest as expounded in the scripture!

The practitioner should express the intent of inviting the Buddhas according to their own capacity.

Fifth: Clarifying the Method of Praising the Three Jewels

Having invited the Three Jewels, cast the five limbs to the ground. Standing upright with proper deportment, face the Dharma seat, burn incense and scatter flowers, contemplate the subtle merits of the Three Jewels, and chant gāthās of praise and prayer:

Your countenance is extraordinarily wondrous,

Your light illuminates the ten directions;

A moment ago I made offerings to you,

Now, again I return in person to behold you.

Holy Lord, King among Devas,

With the voice of the kalaviṅka bird,

You who pity wandering beings,

We now reverently bow in homage!

By this merit of praising the Buddha and cultivating unexcelled Great Vehicle wholesome roots, I dedicate blessings to the devas, nāgas, and eightfold assembly above, Mahābrahmā, the thirty-three devas, Yama, those of the five destinies, the eight kings of the six fasting days, the ghost kings of epidemics, each with their retinues, the local deities of this land, and protectors of the Genuine Dharma within the saṅghārāma; also for kings, rulers, and all people of the realm, teachers, monks, parents, good and evil spiritual friends, temple benefactors, donors of the ten directions, extending universally to wandering beings of the Dharma-realm. Relying on these wholesome roots, may we equally perfume and cultivate the two adornments of merit and wisdom, and together meet in the unborn Path of Omniscience!

Understand that the karma of body, speech, and mind fills the Dharma-realm, praising the Three Jewels, unborn, unceasing, and devoid of self-nature.

Sixth: Clarifying the Method of Bowing to the Buddhas

Having praised them, stand upright with single mind and proper deportment, bowing to the Buddhas in succession. When bowing, mindfully recall each Buddha bowed to: this Buddha’s Dharma-body is like empty space, manifesting forms in response to beings as if directly before one’s eyes, receiving my prostrations. Each Buddha is also like this; the mind must not wander. Furthermore, when bowing to the Buddhas, the practitioner knows that body and mind are empty and tranquil, devoid of marks of bowing; they also know that this body, though illusory and unreal, nonetheless manifests reflections, so that before each Buddha throughout the Dharma-realm this body bows with head and face touching their feet. Single-mindedly and reverently bowing to Śāriputra and all the great Śrāvaka assemblies in the Lotus Sūtra!

Single-mindedly and reverently bowing to all honored great provisional Bodhisattvas of the ten directions, and the holy and wise Saṅgha of Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas who have attained the Path!

Single-mindedly and reverently bowing to Bodhisattva Samantabhadra Mahāsattva!

Chant this Bodhisattva three times. He is the master of the Lotus Repentance. The practitioner should direct the mind to kneel upright before this Bodhisattva, confessing negativities, repenting, and making vows; all other Buddhas and Bodhisattvas bear witness. Throughout the twenty-one days, it is always like this.

Universally for the four debts of gratitude, the three realms of existence, and wandering beings of the Dharma-realm, wishing to sever the three hindrances, take refuge and repent!

Seventh: Clarifying the Method of Repenting the Six Senses, Entreating, Rejoicing, Dedicating, and Making Vows

Having bowed to the Buddhas, stand upright before the Dharma seat, burn incense, and scatter flowers. Visualize the Three Jewels filling empty space, and Bodhisattva Samantabhadra riding the six-tusked white elephant, surrounded by immeasurable adorned retinues, as if directly before one’s eyes. With single mind, practice the repentance Dharma for all wandering beings, giving rise to deep shame and remorse. Lay bare all evil karma created by the six senses from immeasurable kalpas down to this life by oneself and all wandering beings. Sever the continuous mind; from this moment until the end of future time, never commit any evil karma again.

Why is this? Though the nature of karma is empty, karmic retributions are not lost. One who knows emptiness does not even commit wholesome deeds with outpourings, how much less commit negativities! If committing evil does not cease, all are perverted conditions, resulting in illusory retribution. Therefore, because of knowing emptiness, the practitioner gives rise to great shame and remorse, burns incense, scatters flowers, lays bare negativities, and repents.

The repentance phrases expounded below mostly draw upon the intent of the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra. If one wishes to know the repentance methods extensively, read the sūtra directly; if unable to examine it extensively, the essential meaning is now summarized to complete the practice.

Initial Method of Repenting the Eye Faculty

The practitioner single-mindedly kneels upright with proper deportment, burns incense and scatters flowers, mentally reforming and repenting: From the past down to the present, the eye faculty of myself and wandering beings has in its nature ever been empty and tranquil; yet through perverted conditions, heavy negativities arose. Weeping tears of sorrow, proclaim the repentance. The deportment and methods for repenting the next five senses follow this pattern. Speak aloud:

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], together with all wandering beings of the Dharma-realm, repent:

From immeasurable lifetimes down to the present, through the causal conditions of the eye faculty, greedily attaching to forms; because of attaching to forms, craved sensory stains; because of craving stains, received a woman’s body; life after life in rebirth places, deludedly attached to forms; forms destroyed my eyes; because of being a slave to affection, forms drove me to transmigrate through the three realms; driven by this base servant, blind and seeing nothing. The unwholesome eye faculty has harmed me greatly. The Buddhas of the ten directions ever abide and are not extinguished; my turbid, evil eyes, obstructed by hindrances, do not see them. Now reciting the Great Vehicle Vaipulya scriptures, taking refuge in Bodhisattva Samantabhadra and all World-Honored Ones, burning incense, scattering flowers, speaking of the eye’s faults and transgressions, not daring to conceal them. May the wisdom-eye Dharma water of the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas wash them away! Through this causal condition, may all heavy transgressions of the eye faculty of myself and all wandering beings be completely and purely repented!

Having repented, bow to the Three Jewels. Having spoken these words, bring the five limbs to the ground. In the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra, it is explained that in repenting the six senses, each must be spoken three times; if time is limited and this is difficult, speaking once is also permissible, merely saying: The second and third repetitions are also spoken like this. The practitioner should reflect upon the heavy negativities arising from this body’s eye faculty, laying them bare and repenting before Samantabhadra; the next five senses also follow this intent.

Method of Repenting the Ear Faculty

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], together with all wandering beings of the Dharma-realm, repent:

Throughout many kalpas, through the causal conditions of the ear faculty, pursuing external sounds; when hearing wondrous sounds, delusion and attachment arose in the heart; when hearing evil sounds, one hundred and eight kinds of affliction thieves were aroused to inflict harm. Because of such an evil ear, receiving evil retribution, constantly hearing evil sounds, giving rise to attachments. Because of perverted listening, destined to fall into evil destinies, border lands, and places of false views where the Correct Dharma is not heard; deludedly attached at every point, without a moment’s pause. Relying on this aperture of sound, exhausting my spiritual consciousness, plunging into the three paths. The Buddhas of the ten directions are constantly expounding the Dharma; my turbid, evil ears, obstructed by hindrances, do not hear them. Only now do I awaken, reciting and upholding the Great Vehicle’s sea-treasury of merit, taking refuge in Bodhisattva Samantabhadra and all World-Honored Ones, burning incense, scattering flowers, speaking of the ear’s faults and transgressions, not daring to conceal them. Through this causal condition, may all heavy transgressions arising from the ear faculty of myself and wandering beings of the Dharma-realm be completely and purely repented!

Having repented, bow to the Three Jewels.

Method of Repenting the Nose Faculty

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], together with all wandering beings of the Dharma-realm, repent:

Throughout immeasurable kalpas, relying on this nose faculty, smelling various scents—the body scents of men and women, the scent of fine foods, and various kinds of scents—deluded and without comprehension, stirring up the bonds and outpourings; the affliction thieves that were dormant all arose, immeasurable sinful karma increasing thereby. Through greed for fragrance, discriminating perceptions, defiled and attached at every point, falling into birth and death, undergoing various bitter retributions. The wondrous merit-fragrance of the Buddhas of the ten directions fills the Dharma-realm; my turbid, evil nose, obstructed by hindrances, does not smell it. Now reciting the Great Vehicle’s pure, wondrous scriptures, taking refuge in Bodhisattva Samantabhadra and all World-Honored Ones, burning incense, scattering flowers, speaking of the nose’s faults and transgressions, not daring to conceal them. Through this causal condition, may all transgressions of the nose faculty of myself and all wandering beings be completely and purely repented!

Having repented, bow to the Three Jewels.

Method of Repenting the Tongue Faculty

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], together with all wandering beings of the Dharma-realm, repent:

Throughout countless kalpas, unwholesome evil karma committed by the tongue faculty: craving fine flavors, harming wandering beings, breaking prohibitions and precepts, opening the gate of heedlessness; immeasurable sinful karma was born from the tongue faculty. Furthermore, through the tongue faculty giving rise to faults of speech: lying speech, frivolous speech, harsh speech, divisive speech, slandering the Three Jewels, praising false views, speaking useless words, instigating contention and discord, speaking of Dharma as non-Dharma. All thorns of evil karma issued from the tongue faculty; severing the wheel of the Correct Dharma arose from the tongue faculty. Such an evil tongue severs the seed of merit, asserting many pretexts amidst non-principles, praising false views like adding fuel to fire. The faults and transgressions of the tongue faculty are immeasurable and boundless; through this causal condition, destined to fall into evil destinies for hundreds of kalpas and thousands of kalpas, with no time of release. The Dharma-flavor of the Buddhas fills the Dharma-realm; because of the tongue faculty’s transgressions, unable to distinguish it. Now reciting the Great Vehicle’s secret treasury of the Buddhas, taking refuge in Bodhisattva Samantabhadra and all World-Honored Ones, burning incense, scattering flowers, speaking of the tongue’s faults and transgressions, not daring to conceal them. Through this causal condition, may all heavy transgressions of the tongue faculty of myself and wandering beings of the Dharma-realm be completely and purely repented!

Having repented, bow to the Three Jewels.

Method of Repenting the Body Faculty

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], together with all wandering beings of the Dharma-realm, repent:

From long ago down to the present, the unwholesomeness of the body faculty, greedily attaching to touches—namely, the soft, smooth, fine bodies of men and women, and various such touches—perverted and without comprehension, afflictions blazing fiercely, committing physical karma, giving rise to the three unwholesome deeds: killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct; forming great enmity with wandering beings; committing inexpiable offenses and breaking precepts, even burning stūpas and temples, using property of the Three Jewels without shame. Such immeasurable, boundless transgressions arising from physical karma cannot be fully spoken. Through the causal conditions of sinful defilement, in future lives destined to fall into hells, fierce blazing flames burning my body, undergoing great agony for immeasurable billions of kalpas. The Buddhas of the ten directions constantly emit pure light illuminating and touching us; the heavy negativities of the body faculty, obstructed by hindrances, leave us unaware, knowing only greedy attachment to coarse, base, evil touches, presently undergoing numerous sufferings, and subsequently undergoing the sufferings of hells, hungry ghosts, and animals. Submerged within such diverse sufferings, unaware and uncomprehending.

Today, feeling shame and remorse, reciting and upholding the Great Vehicle’s true Dharma store, taking refuge in Bodhisattva Samantabhadra and all World-Honored Ones, burning incense, scattering flowers, speaking of the body’s faults and transgressions, not daring to conceal them. Through this causal condition, may all heavy transgressions of the body faculty of myself and wandering beings of the Dharma-realm be completely and purely repented!

Having repented, bow to the Three Jewels.

Method of Repenting the Mind Faculty

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], together with all wandering beings of the Dharma-realm, repent:

From beginningless time down to the present, the unwholesomeness of the mind faculty, greedily attaching to dharmas; deranged and foolish, without comprehension, following cognitive objects, giving rise to greed, anger, and delusion. Such false thoughts can give rise to all mixed karma, namely the ten unwholesome deeds and five inexpiable offenses; like a monkey, and like sticky birdlime, greedily attached at every point, pervading all six sense faculties. The karma of these six sense faculties, its branches, flowers, and leaves fill all birthplaces of the twenty-five realms of existence in the three realms; it can also cause ignorance, old age, death, and the twelve links of suffering to increase; the eight false paths and eight difficult realms, none that it does not pass through. Immeasurable, boundless evil, unwholesome retributions are born from the mind faculty. Such a mind faculty is the root of all birth and death, the source of all sufferings. As expounded in the scripture: ‘Śākyamuni is named Vairocana, pervading all places.’ You should know that all dharmas are the Buddha-Dharma; through false discrimination, undergoing scorching afflictions, this is seeing impurity within bodhi, and giving rise to bonds within liberation. Only now do I awaken, giving rise to deep shame, remorse, and great dread, reciting and upholding the Great Vehicle, cultivating according to the teaching, taking refuge in Bodhisattva Samantabhadra and all World-Honored Ones, burning incense, scattering flowers, speaking of the mind’s faults and transgressions, laying them bare and repenting, not daring to conceal them. Through this causal condition, may all heavy transgressions of the mind faculty of myself and wandering beings of the Dharma-realm, even down to all evil karma arising from the six senses—that which has arisen, is presently arising, and will arise in the future—be washed clean, repented, and become completely pure!

Having repented, bow to the Three Jewels. Used continually during the six periods; if considered too verbose, grasp the intent and abbreviate as appropriate.

Clarifying the Method of Entreating

Single-mindedly kneel upright with proper deportment, burn incense, scatter flowers, and single-mindedly form the thought, entreating the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas to expound the Dharma to liberate wandering beings. Ponder in mind and proclaim with the mouth; the next three dharmas follow this pattern. Speak aloud:

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], entreat the immeasurable Buddhas of the ten directions throughout the Dharma-realm: May you long abide and turn the wheel of the Dharma, causing conscious beings to return to original purity; only after that may the Tathāgatas return to ever-abiding tranquility!

Having entreated, bow to the Three Jewels. Having spoken these words, cast the five limbs to the ground. If the practitioner wishes to express thoughts in their own words, brief or extensive, speak according to one’s own heart. The next three dharmas follow this pattern.

Clarifying the Method of Rejoicing

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], rejoice in all the merits of the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas, in the conditioned wholesome deeds of ordinary beings in stillness or disturbance, and in all karma with outpourings and without outpourings; I, bhikṣu [name], rejoice in them all!

Having rejoiced, bow to the Three Jewels.

Clarifying the Method of Dedication

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], dedicate all wholesome deeds cultivated through the three karmas, offering them to Ganges-sands Buddhas of the ten directions; exhausting empty space, the Dharma-realm, and the bounds of the future, may this merit be dedicated to seeking the Buddha Path!

Having dedicated, bow to the Three Jewels.

Clarifying the Method of Making Vows

With utmost mind I, bhikṣu [name], make this vow: May my consciousness not be confused at the approach of death; with correct mindfulness, may I go directly to rebirth in the Land of Peace and Sustenance, face to face beholding Amitābha and meeting all the noble ones, cultivating the ten grounds and realizing superior, lasting bliss!

Having made vows, bow to the Three Jewels. As for the method of making vows, the practitioner should, before Bodhisattva Samantabhadra and all World-Honored Ones, formulate in mind and utter with speech all aspirations within the heart: namely, that in this body one may practice the Path without obstacles, that the four maras may not arise, that one may attain profound samādhi, enter the Dharma gates, broadly propagate the Correct Dharma, and liberate wandering beings; that at the time of relinquishing life, correct mindfulness will be perfected; even down to future lifetimes, that one may constantly encounter the Three Jewels, be born into families of right faith, leave home to cultivate the Path, make offerings to the Three Jewels, receive and uphold the Great Vehicle, have all pāramitās manifest before one, and together with all wandering beings accomplish equal, complete awakening. Such diverse vows within the heart should all be spoken freely according to the practitioner’s own disposition; they cannot be completely enumerated here.

Eighth: Clarifying the Method of Circumambulation

Having bowed to the Buddhas, stand upright with single mind and proper deportment, circumambulate the Dharma seat to the right, burn incense, scatter flowers, walk serenely and slowly, mindfully recall the Three Jewels, and chant three times in succession:

Namo to the Buddhas of the ten directions! Namo to the Dharma of the ten directions! Namo to the Saṅgha of the ten directions!

Namo Śākyamuni Buddha! Namo Prabhūtaratna Buddha! Namo emanation Buddhas of Śākyamuni! Namo Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Dharma! Namo Mañjuśrī Bodhisattva! Namo Bodhisattva Samantabhadra!

Having chanted the names of the Buddhas and Bodhisattva Mahāsattvas in this manner, recite the sūtra. The method of reciting the sūtra is explained extensively below. Not only does the practitioner realize that the sound of reciting the scripture is in its nature empty, they should also know that body and mind are like clouds and reflections; lifting and lowering the feet, the mind apprehends nothing, not abiding in marks of walking. They also know that this body manifests reflections throughout the ten directions, filling the Dharma-realm, universally appearing everywhere to circumambulate all Buddhas. Circumambulate three times, seven times, twenty-one times, seven times seven times, or a hundred times; there is no fixed number, so judge according to one’s capacity. When circumambulation is about to end, chant the names of the Three Jewels as before, burn incense, maintain correct mindfulness, and chant the verse hymn. When the hymn ends, go to the original place of bowing to the Buddhas, take refuge in the Three Jewels, maintain single-minded correct mindfulness, and chant aloud:

I take refuge in the Buddha; may wandering beings embody and understand the Great Path, generating the unexcelled resolve!

Having spoken, bow, and speak again:

I take refuge in the Dharma; may wandering beings penetrate deeply into the scriptural treasury, wisdom like the ocean!

Having spoken, bow, and speak again:

I take refuge in the Saṅgha; may wandering beings lead and guide the great assembly, all entirely unobstructed! Reverent homage to the holy assembly!

Bow.

Ninth: Clarifying the Method of Reciting the Scripture Further

During the prior circumambulation, after completing the chanting of the names of the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas, the practitioner with single-minded correct mindfulness recites the Lotus Sūtra.

There are two kinds of practitioners who recite: first, complete recitation; second, partial recitation.

Regarding complete recitation: the practitioner has previously memorized the entire Lotus Sūtra fluently before entering the bodhimaṇḍa. Starting from the first chapter, they may recite one chapter, two chapters, or up to an entire scroll. When circumambulation is about to end, stop reciting the scripture, chant the names of the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas as before, and after completing the three refuges, return to the original seat. If one does not yet wish to practice seated meditation, one may sit upright and recite the sūtra further, evaluating the amount according to one’s wish; however, seated meditation across the four periods must not be entirely abandoned, as prolonged sitting is necessary. If a person has not previously trained in sitting and merely wishes to recite scriptures and repent, when becoming exhausted from prolonged recitation during walking or sitting, they may temporarily gather their thoughts, rest, and upon recovering immediately resume recitation; this does not violate the ritual method. Hence it is said: Without entering samādhi, merely by reciting and upholding, they will behold superior, wondrous forms.

Second, regarding partial recitation: if the practitioner has not previously recited the Lotus Sūtra, in order to practice this samādhi, they should recite the Chapter on Peaceful Practices, making it completely fluent. When circumambulating, recite this chapter once, twice, or three times, according to inclination; reciting other chapters of the Lotus Sūtra alongside it is also permissible, but one must not recite other scriptures or texts.

The method of reciting scriptures requires distinct phrases and sentences, clear pronunciation, neither dragging nor rushing. Bind attention to the phrases of the sūtra as if facing the written text directly without error. Next, calm the mind, realizing that the nature of voice and sound is like an echo in an empty valley; though sound cannot be apprehended, the mind distinctly illuminates the meaning of every phrase, the wording completely clear. Project this Dharma sound to fill the Dharma-realm, making offerings to the Three Jewels, universally bestowing it upon wandering beings to lead them into the realm of the Great Vehicle’s Single Reality.

Tenth: Clarifying the Method of Seated Meditation and the Correct Contemplation of the Genuine Mark

Having completed circumambulation and recitation, the practitioner should approach the seating place, sit upon the rope-bed, arrange robes, sit upright with proper posture, close the eyes and mouth, regulate the breath, relax body and mind, completely as expounded in the preliminary expedients for seated meditation. Afterward, gather thoughts, apply correct contemplation, and destroy sinful karma.

How is correct contemplation explained? In accordance with the Bodhisattva’s practice: not severing bonds and outpourings, nor abiding in the ocean of outpourings, contemplating all dharmas as empty according to their genuine mark—this is named correct contemplation.

What is named contemplating all dharmas as empty? The practitioner should attentively contemplate the presently manifest single thought of the deluded mind in relation to cognitive objects: Is this mind a mind caused by mind? Is it a mind not caused by mind? Is it a mind both caused and not caused by mind? Is it a mind neither caused nor not caused by mind? Does it exist in the three periods of time? Does it exist internally, externally, or in between? What footprints does it possess, and in what location does it reside?

Seeking the mind within such diverse causal conditions, it is ultimately without apprehension. The mind is like a dream or illusion, unreal, tranquil like empty space, without name and without mark, impossible to discriminate.

At that time, the practitioner does not even see the mind as birth and death, how could they see the mind as nirvāṇa? Having apprehended neither the object contemplated nor preserving the subjective contemplation, neither grasping nor rejecting, neither relying nor attaching, all thoughts do not arise. The mind is constantly tranquil, yet does not abide in tranquility. The path of language is severed, ineffable. Though apprehending neither marks of mind nor non-mind, one clearly penetrates all dharmas of mind and non-mind; all are like illusions and transformations.

This is named contemplating the mind as having no mind, and dharmas as not abiding within dharmas: all dharmas are liberated, tranquil in the Truth of Cessation.

Practicing this repentance is named the Great Repentance, named the Adorned Repentance, named the Repentance of the Non-Mark of Transgression, named the Repentance of Destroying Mind and Consciousness.

Practicing this repentance, the mind is like flowing water, not abiding within dharmas.

Why is this? All dharmas of transgressions and blessings created by false thoughts and perversions arise from the mind; apart from the mind, there are no transgressions, blessings, or any dharmas. If one contemplates the mind as having no mind, transgressions and blessings have no master; knowing that the nature of transgressions and blessings is empty, all dharmas are completely empty.

Contemplating in this manner can destroy all birth and death, perversions, the three poisons, false thoughts, and extremely heavy evil karma, yet there is nothing destroyed. Body and mind are pure, thought-moment by thought-moment illuminating all dharmas, not receiving or attaching to subtle aggregates or sense realms.

Through this causal condition, one accords with samādhi. Through the power of samādhi, one immediately sees Samantabhadra and the Buddhas of the ten directions rubbing the crown of the head and expounding the Dharma. All Dharma gates manifest within a single thought-mind, neither one nor different, free of hindrances. It is like a wish-fulfilling jewel endowed with all rare gems; such a jewel-nature is neither internal nor external. When the practitioner skillfully contemplates the nature of mind as like empty space, seeing all Dharma gates penetrate unhindered within the ultimately pure mind, it is also like this.

This is named the practitioner contemplating the genuine mark of the mind, repenting the six senses without severing the five desires, attaining the purity of the senses, and seeing matters beyond all hindrances, as expounded extensively in the Lotus Sūtra and the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra.

Furthermore, when the practitioner first enters the bodhimaṇḍa, they must fully cultivate these ten dharmas during the single period. In this manner, across the six periods, use this method, only omitting the formal invitation of the Three Jewels; during the twenty-one days, cultivate the nine dharmas, cultivating this contemplation whenever performing each single dharma. In each and every period of the six periods, there must be no deficiency in phenomena or principle; this is named single-minded diligence throughout the twenty-one days.

Furthermore, when the practitioner cultivates repentance during the twenty-one days, whether walking, sitting, standing, entering, exiting, defecating, urinating, sweeping, sprinkling, washing, moving, acting, glancing, bowing, or looking up, they should in every thought-moment be mindful of the Three Jewels, contemplating the nature of mind as empty. They must not for the space of a kṣaṇa recall the five desires or worldly affairs, generating perverted thoughts, conversing with outsiders, indulging in sleep, laughing, gazing at forms, listening to sounds, attaching to sensory realms, or giving rise to unwholesome, neutral, or affliction-ridden wandering thoughts, violating what is expounded in the Four Peaceful Practices.

If one can maintain thought-moment following thought-moment in succession in this manner, not departing from the genuine mark, not sparing body or life, practicing the repentance Dharma for the sake of all wandering beings, this is named the true practice of single-minded diligence throughout the twenty-one days.

The marks of practice mostly derive from the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra and the Four Peaceful Practices. If the practitioner wishes to cultivate samādhi diligently and make their practice faultless, they should study the scriptural texts of these two places thoroughly.

Fifth: Briefly Clarifying the Marks of Realization in Practice

If the practitioner can cultivate single-minded diligence in this manner when practicing samādhi during the twenty-one days, during the twenty-one days or after twenty-one days are fulfilled, there are different marks of realization for three grades of practitioners; they will now be briefly distinguished:

  1. Marks of realization for practitioners of lower capacity
  2. Marks of realization for practitioners of medium capacity
  3. Marks of realization for practitioners of upper capacity

Marks of realization for lower capacity: during the twenty-one days or when twenty-one days are fulfilled, they attain the purity of the precept root.

How is this known? Within this there are also three grades:

First, practitioners of the lower grade: if they obtain diverse extraordinary and auspicious dreams, or perceive the sense faculties becoming clear and bright, the four great elements light and agile, the complexion fresh and clean, the body endowed with strength and majestic dignity, and the mind for the Path arising courageously—this is named the lower grade of knowing the mark of the gradual purification of the precept root.

Marks of the middle grade of precept root purity: during the twenty-one days or when twenty-one days are fulfilled, during circumambulation or seated meditation, they suddenly behold diverse auspicious omens: namely, bright light, pure colors, extraordinary fragrances, and fine voices singing praises, as expounded in the Bodhisattva precepts of the Brahmā Net Sūtra. Having seen these diverse auspicious marks, body and mind rejoice, attaining Dharma joy and bliss, free of evil marks—this is named the middle grade of knowing the mark of precept root purity.

Marks of the upper grade of precept root purity: during the twenty-one days or when twenty-one days are fulfilled, during circumambulation and seated meditation, though not yet realizing diverse Dharma gates, body and mind are peaceful, joyful, and tranquil. Within the tranquil mind, they personally see the marks of their own body’s pure precepts: namely, seeing their body wearing clean Dharma robes, deportment neat and dignified, body upright and majestic; amidst the pure assembly, they personally see the marks of wholesome karma, clearly and distinctly, the three categories of precept marks appearing in succession. Faith unfolds, the mind attains Dharma joy, peaceful and secure without fear; seeing such diverse marks of wholesome karma within the mind of concentration—this is named the upper grade of precept root purity.

By these three grades of marks, one verifies that the precept root has gradually become pure. The marks are numerous and cannot be fully spoken. If negativities are heavy and difficult to eliminate, but one can apply the mind with earnest repentance without ceasing, at times one may see marks of sin: namely, seeing headless or handless bodies, deep pits, turbid water, fierce fire, and foul filth—diverse evil realms. Seeing them, fear arises in the heart; one should intensify repentance. Repenting without ceasing, afterward one will also attain the purity of precepts.

How does one know the marks of realization attained by practitioners of medium capacity? They attain the purity of the concentration root. Within this there are also three grades:

Practitioners of the lower grade: when in seated meditation, they suddenly feel body and mind become clear and tranquil, generating diverse dhyāna-concentrations: namely, the desire-realm dwelling concentration and the access concentration, body and mind empty and tranquil. Diverse physical sensations arise in succession, initial thought and sustained thought distinct, joy, bliss, single-mindedness, silent and tranquil; or directing attention to wandering beings, they realize love, compassion, joy, and equanimity; or directing attention to the Buddha’s marks and signs, wholesome thoughts unfold, entering diverse samādhis. Such diverse concentrations are named the lower grade of marks of the purity of the concentration root.

Marks of the middle grade of concentration root purity: when in seated meditation, the practitioner’s body and mind are stable and calm, perceiving inhalation and exhalation as long, short, subtle, and fine, entering and exiting all pores of the body without hindrance. Because of this, they see the thirty-six components of the body clearly and distinctly, giving rise to diverse joys and entering diverse dhyāna-concentrations; or seeing the impurities of the internal and external body, white bones scattered about; or seeing white bones gleaming white and distinct, generating revulsion toward the worldly realm. Because of this, they generate diverse dhyāna-concentrations, body and mind joyful, peaceful, and in tranquil absorption; or directing attention to dharmas, giving rise to love, compassion, joy, and equanimity; or directing attention to the subtle wisdom and diverse virtues of the Buddhas, generating samādhi. Diverse concentrations unfolding in this manner are named the middle grade of marks of the purity of the concentration root.

Marks of the upper grade of concentration root purity: when in seated meditation, the practitioner’s body and mind are tranquil; the mind directing attention to the worldly aggregates, sense bases, and elements, they immediately perceive them as impermanent, suffering, and empty, the body, feelings, mind, and dharmas all unreal, the twelve links of dependent origination false and masterless, all dharmas unborn and unceasing, like empty space, body and mind tranquil. According with emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness, diverse profound dhyāna-concentrations arise, subtle, joyful, tranquil, and unconditioned, feeling revulsion toward the world and compassion for all, free of hindrances and evil dharmas—this is named the upper grade of marks of concentration root purity. This briefly explains the marks of the purity of the concentration root attained by practitioners of medium capacity.

How does one know the marks of realization attained by practitioners of upper capacity? They attain the purity of the wisdom root. Within this there are also three grades:

Marks of the lower grade of wisdom root purity: during the twenty-one days or when twenty-one days are fulfilled, during walking or sitting when entering diverse dhyāna-concentrations, they suddenly feel body and mind to be like clouds, reflections, dreams, and illusions, unreal. Because of this, awakening unfolds inwardly, wisdom becomes clear and distinct, fully penetrating all dharmas, skillfully explaining with expedient means without hindrance, penetrating the twelve divisions of scriptures, explaining according to meaning without blockage to questions, expounding the Dharma inexhaustibly—this is named the lower grade of marks of wisdom root purity.

Marks of the middle grade of wisdom root purity: during walking, sitting, recitation, and mindfulness, the practitioner’s body and mind are tranquil like empty space, entering diverse tranquil concentrations. Within correct wisdom, they behold Bodhisattva Samantabhadra face to face riding the six-tusked white elephant, surrounded by immeasurable Bodhisattva assemblies, manifesting before the person in a body that all wandering beings delight in seeing. Because of seeing Bodhisattva Samantabhadra, this person immediately attains samādhi and dhāraṇīs, named the Rotating dhāraṇī, the Hundreds of Thousands of Millions of Rotations dhāraṇī, and the Dharma Sound Expedient Means dhāraṇī. Dhāraṇī is great wisdom. Because of attaining this great wisdom, what the Buddhas expound is never forgotten once heard, penetrated without hindrance; within a single phrase, they penetrate all meanings, expounding inexhaustibly like wind in empty space. Attaining such diverse wisdom doors is named the middle grade of marks of wisdom root purity.

Marks of the upper grade of wisdom root purity: during walking, sitting, recitation, and mindfulness, the practitioner’s body and mind become openly pure, entering deep dhyāna-concentration, awakened wisdom distinct, the mind unshakeable. Within dhyāna-concentration, they behold Bodhisattva Samantabhadra, Śākyamuni, Prabhūtaratna, the emanation World-Honored Ones, and the Buddhas of the ten directions. They attain the unhindered great dhāraṇī, acquire the purity of the six sense faculties, universally manifest physical bodies, open the Buddha’s knowledge and vision, and enter the Bodhisattva’s position, as expounded extensively in the Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra—this is named the upper grade of marks of wisdom root purity.

This briefly explains the differences in marks of realization attained by practitioners of the three capacities.

Among these three kinds of correct marks, there may be maras presenting similar semblances; if one realizes these dharmas, one should discern them skillfully and not immediately give rise to an equating mind.

Therefore, if the practitioner wishes to attain these great merits and benefits, they should during the twenty-one days cultivate the prior methods with single-minded diligence. If not attained within twenty-one days, apply further effort and do not become negligent.

If negativities obstructing the Path gradually fade away, but the profound Dharma gates of samādhi have not yet appeared before one, and one wishes to practice samādhi constantly, it is not necessary to rely entirely on the ten dharmas above; merely grasp the intent expounded in the Chapter on Peaceful Practices and cultivate with single mind. Then one will naturally attain the purity of the six sense faculties, behold the Buddhas of the ten directions, acquire universally manifesting physical bodies, open the Buddha’s knowledge and vision, and enter the Bodhisattva’s position.

One should know that taking twenty-one days as a period and practicing according to the six periods expounded above is intended to teach newly practicing Bodhisattvas who cannot yet enter deep samādhi. First use phenomenal rituals to subdue their minds and destroy heavy negativities obstructing the Path, so that through this, body and mind become pure and taste the flavor of Dharma joy. If one wishes to abide with a single mind ever tranquil and enter deep samādhi, one should set aside the former practices, rely directly on the peaceful practices, constantly delight in seated meditation, contemplate all dharmas as empty according to their genuine mark, not giving rise to internal or external faults, feeling great compassion and pity for all wandering beings without uninterrupted thoughts—this is cultivating samādhi. If one relies on the former methods, phenomenal complexity becomes an obstruction.

Therefore, having grasped this intent, the practitioner should evaluate it with their own wisdom; what was expounded for a single period cannot be applied in its entirety. Beginners who lack skillful dexterity should for now rely on the present text, using the prior methods to cultivate samādhi.

Practical Instructions

1. Purpose and Duration

The Lotus Samādhi Repentance is a twenty-one day intensive retreat meant to purify heavy karmic obstacles, establish the purity of the six sense faculties, and enter the contemplation of the genuine mark of all dharmas. It may also be performed in order to benefit those who are greatly suffering in order to share with them the vast merit of such a practice.

Practitioners who have already trained in deep meditation and wish to abide constantly in samādhi may rely directly upon the four Peaceful Practices of the Lotus Sūtra without elaborate ritual.

For practitioners purifying obstacles or establishing concentration through structured action, Zhiyi sets forth a twenty-one day cycle combining physical prostration, sensory confession, circumambulation, sūtra recitation, and seated contemplation.

One may also use the condensed version daily or when necessary to purify oneself and the karmic conditions of others.

2. Seven Day Preliminary Purification

Before formally entering the twenty-one day retreat, dedicate seven days to preliminary preparation:

  • Cease worldly engagements and distracting activities.
  • Prepare and adorn the shrine room, cleanse your garments, and make offerings to the Three Jewels.
  • Reflect deeply on all unwholesome karma accumulated from past lives into the present, generating earnest shame, remorse, and resolve.
  • Practice bowing, circumambulation, sūtra recitation, seated contemplation, and focused vows, while becoming fluent in the repentance verses and liturgical recitations.

3. Entry Timing and Diligence

Formally enter the bodhimaṇḍa on one of the six monthly fasting days (the 8th, 14th, 15th, 23rd, 29th, or 30th day of the lunar month), when celestial guardians inspect the human realm and protect practitioners. Bind the mind with a firm resolve to maintain diligence across two complementary dimensions:

  • Diligence within phenomena: Performing every physical and vocal ritual act with undivided attention, free of wandering thoughts.
  • Diligence within principle: Illuminating throughout every action that the mind that acts and the nature of mind are non-dual, unborn, and unceasing, never departing from emptiness.

4. Spatial Arrangement and Enshrinement

The bodhimaṇḍa is the consecrated shrine room or sanctuary space itself, distinct from your personal meditation seat:

  • Central Dharma Seat: Erect a clean, elevated Dharma seat within the bodhimaṇḍa with enough open space around it for clockwise circumambulation.
  • Enshrinement Options: Zhiyi instructs enshrining a copy of the Lotus Sūtra alone upon the high Dharma seat, accompanied by banners, canopies, lamps, flowers, and renowned incenses, without requiring additional statues or relics. Where appropriate, one may also enshrine a stūpa or an image of Śākyamuni Buddha containing a microprinted copy of the Lotus Sūtra sealed within it, or an image or stūpa in whose presence the entire Lotus Sūtra has been read and consecrated.
  • Partitioned Meditation Seat: Arrange a separate personal seating place (such as a meditation mat or rope-bed), partitioned from the main altar area, to which you withdraw for seated contemplation.

5. Bodily Purity and Robe Protocol

At the start of the retreat, bathe with fragrant water and put on clean retreat robes (the great robe, newly dyed robes, or one’s finest clean garments). Whenever you must leave the bodhimaṇḍa to attend to bodily necessities or enter impure areas, remove the clean retreat garments and wear ordinary old garments. Upon completing your task, wash again and resume the clean retreat garments before re-entering the bodhimaṇḍa.

6. The Six Daily Periods

Across the twenty-one days, practice is divided into six daily periods spanning day and night (dawn, morning, midday, dusk, early night, and midnight):

  • First Period Only: Upon first entering the bodhimaṇḍa at the start of the retreat, perform all ten dharmas in full, including Reverently Inviting the Three Jewels.
  • All Subsequent Periods: Because the Buddhas, Bodhisattvas, Dharma treasury, and assemblies have already been invited and remain present in the bodhimaṇḍa, omit the invitation section in every period that follows, practicing the remaining nine dharmas identically.
  • Balancing Recitation and Sitting: If you wish to spend extra time sitting and reciting the Lotus Sūtra after circumambulation, you may adjust the amount according to your capacity, provided that formal seated contemplation is never omitted across at least four of the daily periods.

7. Verification Signs and Discernment

During or upon completing the twenty-one days, practitioners may experience signs verifying purification across three capacities (each subdivided into lower, middle, and upper grades):

  • Lower Capacity (Purity of the Precept Root): Auspicious dreams, physical lightness and clarity of the senses, radiant lights or fragrances, or visions of oneself wearing pure Dharma robes within a pure assembly. If heavy negative karma surfaces first through frightening images (such as pits, turbid water, fire, or mutilated forms), intensify repentance until pure signs appear.
  • Medium Capacity (Purity of the Concentration Root): Access concentration, subtle awareness of breath through all pores, contemplation of bodily impurity and white bones, the four immeasurables, and tranquil absorption in emptiness, signlessness, and wishlessness.
  • Upper Capacity (Purity of the Wisdom Root): Direct realization that body and mind are like clouds and illusions, unhindered eloquence in the twelve divisions of scripture, direct vision of Bodhisattva Samantabhadra on the six tusked white elephant granting dhāraṇīs, and vision of Śākyamuni, Prabhūtaratna, and the Buddhas of the ten directions.
  • Discernment: Never grasp at experiences or equate them with ultimate realization, as Maras can project deceptive semblances. Discern all phenomena through emptiness and continue diligent practice.

Methods for Condensing Repetitions

Zhiyi provides built-in flexibility to keep the six daily periods sustainable without compromising the integrity of the practice:

1. Condensing the Threefold Sense Repentance

The Samantabhadra Meditation Sūtra prescribes reciting the repentance confession for each sense faculty three full times. To prevent exhaustion across six daily periods, Zhiyi allows reciting the confession once and speaking the closing formula, “The second and third repetitions are also spoken like this,” before prostrating. This fulfills the threefold scriptural requirement in speech and ritual intent.

2. Abbreviating the Wording Across the Six Periods

At the conclusion of the six sense confessions, Zhiyi explicitly notes that during continuous six-period practice, if the full text is overly lengthy, the practitioner may grasp its essential meaning and abbreviate the recitation as appropriate. Unifying the six individual sensory confessions into a single comprehensive prayer directly applies this allowance.

3. Circumambulation Adjustments and Visualization

During circumambulation, you may walk three, seven, twenty-one, forty-nine, or one hundred circuits around the central Dharma seat according to your physical capacity and time. If physical walking is impossible or restricted by space or health, remain stationary and visualize your body manifesting throughout the ten directions to circumambulate all Buddhas, knowing that body and mind are like clouds and reflections.

4. Sūtra Recitation Options

If you have not memorized the entire Lotus Sūtra, Zhiyi permits reciting the “Chapter on Peaceful Practices” once, twice, or three times (or reciting selected chapters of the Lotus Sūtra) rather than reciting entire scrolls, provided outside texts are not mixed into the session. One may also read any chapter they like, especially the Avalokiteśvara Chapter and the Dhāraṇī chapter.

Unified Repentance Prayer for the Six Senses

This prayer is for times when the longer praying is not possible:

With utmost mind I, the practitioner (name), together with all wandering beings of the Dharma-realm, repent:

From beginningless time and immeasurable kalpas down to this present life, through the causal conditions of our six sense faculties—eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind—their nature has ever been empty and tranquil, yet through perverted conditions we greedily attached to forms, sounds, scents, flavors, touches, and cognitive dharmas.

Driven by craving and false discrimination across the eye, ear, and nose faculties, we became slaves to sensory stains, aroused the one hundred and eight affliction thieves, stirred up dormant bonds and outpourings, and wandered blindly through birth and death. Through the unwholesome tongue faculty, we craved flavors, harmed wandering beings, broke precepts, spoke lying, frivolous, harsh, and divisive words, slandered the Three Jewels, praised false views, and obstructed the wheel of the Correct Dharma. Through the unwholesome body faculty, we attached to coarse and subtle touches, committed killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct, formed enmity with wandering beings, and even damaged sacred places or misused property of the Three Jewels. Through the unwholesome mind faculty—the root of all birth and death, restless like a monkey and clinging like sticky birdlime—we gave rise to greed, anger, delusion, the ten unwholesome deeds, and the five inexpiable offenses, filling the twenty-five realms of existence, increasing ignorance, old age, and death, and seeing impurity within bodhi and bonds within liberation.

Because of these heavy obstructions across the six senses, though the Buddhas of the ten directions ever abide, constantly expound the Dharma, fill the Dharma-realm with wondrous merit fragrance and Dharma flavor, and radiate pure illuminating light, our turbid and obstructed faculties have neither seen, heard, smelled, tasted, felt, nor understood them.

Today we awaken, giving rise to deep shame, remorse, and great dread. Reciting and upholding the Great Vehicle secret treasury, taking refuge in Bodhisattva Samantabhadra and all World-Honored Ones, burning incense and scattering flowers, we lay bare all faults and transgressions of the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind faculties, not daring to conceal them. May the wisdom eye Dharma water of the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas wash them away! Through this causal condition, may all heavy transgressions and evil karma arising from the six senses of myself and all wandering beings—that which has arisen, is presently arising, and will arise in the future—be completely washed clean, repented, and become pure!

The second and third repetitions are also spoken like this.

This is the direct guidance.
